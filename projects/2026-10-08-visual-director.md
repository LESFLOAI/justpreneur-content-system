## 1. Overall Creative Concept

This is a contrast story, not a hype story: a borrowed four-day stage vs. owned, unbuilt four-acre land. The whole deck should visually argue that tension on every slide — not just state it in copy. The design language is "modern business/culture media explainer" (think Bloomberg/Axios-style split-screen graphics, or an Advertising Week trade-press deck), not a motivational-quote template. No confetti, no stock-photo hustle energy, no gradients-and-sparkle.

The through-line device: a vertical dividing line splits every interior slide (2-4) into two columns. Left column = THE HEADLINE (what's loud, temporary, borrowed — rendered in a "spotlight/stage" visual register: warm gold light, spotlight glow, stage-adjacent imagery). Right column = THE INFRASTRUCTURE (what's quiet, permanent, owned — rendered in a "blueprint/land" visual register: cool steel-gray and navy, architectural line work, groundbreaking/construction imagery). The gold-vs-navy palette split literally becomes the argument.

Cover (slide 1) and the two closing slides (5-6) break the two-column grid deliberately — they're where the reader exhales the idea, not compares two columns — to keep pacing varied across a 6-slide swipe.

## 2. Composition and Focal Hierarchy

Across all slides, the hierarchy is: (1) headline/column label — largest, highest contrast; (2) supporting line/claim text — mid-size, high legibility; (3) JustPreneur wordmark — small, fixed position, consistent every slide; (4) imagery — supports and frames text, never competes with it for the top third of the frame, where thumbs stop scrolling.

Slide 1 (Cover): Full-bleed single image, headline locked center-to-lower-third over a scrim, dek beneath it, JustPreneur wordmark top. This is the thumb-stopper — see section on hook below.

Slides 2-4 (Two-column body): Vertical split down the middle (approx 50/50, with a 4-6px dividing rule or thin gold line at the seam). Each side carries a small column header ("THE HEADLINE" / "THE INFRASTRUCTURE") at the top of its half in a fixed, repeating position, then its claim text, then a supporting image treatment in the lower portion of that half. Slide number/topic eyebrow (e.g., "THE ROLE") sits centered above the two columns, above the split line.

Slide 5 (Takeaway): Full-width single column, no split — this is the synthesis beat. Large pull-quote-style treatment for the first sentence, smaller supporting line below.

Slide 6 (Reflection CTA): Full-width, most minimal slide in the deck — nearly all negative space, question mark-driven, acts as the "save/share" beat.

## 4. Typography Direction

- **Headline typeface:** a bold, modern geometric or grotesque sans (e.g., in the family of Inter Bold / Söhne Breit / Neue Haas Grotesk Bold) — confident, editorial, not a script or decorative face. Avoid anything that reads "motivational template" (no brush scripts, no condensed impact-style display faces).
- **Body/supporting copy typeface:** a clean grotesque at regular/medium weight, same family or a close pairing, for consistency — e.g., Inter Regular/Medium.
- **Eyebrows/labels (THE HEADLINE / THE INFRASTRUCTURE / THE ROLE etc.):** small caps or all-caps, letter-spaced (+40-60 tracking), smaller size, used strictly as a navigation/labeling device — never competing with the main headline for attention.
- **Numerals (slide 3, slide 4 timeline):** can step up to a heavier display weight or even a numeral-only display face for "$100M" / "4 ACRES" / "4 DAYS" / "FEB 2027" — numbers are allowed to be the single boldest visual element on slide 3.
- **Minimum sizing for mobile legibility:** headline text no smaller than ~64-72px effective at 1080px width; column body copy no smaller than ~40-44px effective; eyebrow/labels no smaller than ~28-32px. Err larger — this is a swipe-and-glance medium.
- **Line length:** keep each column's body copy to short line breaks (aim 4-6 words per line within a ~480px-wide column at 1080px canvas) so two-column text never feels cramped against the center divider.

## 5. Color Palette

- **Deep navy** (#0B1A2B approx) — primary background for cover scrim, slide 5, slide 6, and the "infrastructure/owned" side's dark elements.
- **Steel gray** (#5A6472 approx) — secondary neutral, used for caveat lines, dividing rules, right-column ("infrastructure") accent treatments, timeline bar on slide 4 right side.
- **Warm white** (#F7F3EC approx) — primary text color on dark backgrounds, and background option for slide 6 if navy is used on slide 5 (alternate for contrast across the swipe).
- **Energy gold** (#D9A441 / #E8B84B approx) — accent only: eyebrow labels, "THE HEADLINE" column accents, oversized numerals on the stage/headline side, the one highlight on slide 5's "four acres," dividing-line echo on slide 6. Gold must never be the dominant field color — it's the "spotlight" accent, used sparingly so it still feels premium, not neon or gaudy.
- **Left column (Headline/Stage) register:** warm white text on navy, with gold accents and spotlight-glow image treatment.
- **Right column (Infrastructure/Land) register:** warm white text on navy, with steel-gray/cool-blue accents and blueprint/architectural-line image treatment. The two columns share the same navy base so the deck feels unified, but differentiate through accent color and image mood only — this keeps it premium rather than carnival-split.

## 6. Image Treatment

- All photographic imagery: desaturated slightly toward cool tones on the "infrastructure" side, warmer/higher-contrast with a visible spot/key light on the "headline/stage" side — this is the main nonverbal signal reinforcing the two-column argument.
- Apply a consistent navy color-grade/duotone wash across all photographic elements so images feel like they belong to one unified premium media brand, not stock photos dropped in raw.
- Images on slides 2-4 are cropped into fixed-size rectangle "windows" within each column (not full bleed) — this keeps the focus on the text argument and prevents imagery from overpowering the two-column comparison.
- No fake logos, no readable signage text, no invented quotes on any image. No crowds of indistinguishable extras — if people appear, keep it to the named, relevant figure(s) only, rendered respectfully and non-caricatured.
- No malformed hands, no duplicate people, no clutter, no small decorative icons (no lightbulbs, rockets, podiums-as-cliché, etc.).

## 7. Instagram Safe-Margin Instructions

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px (portrait) for all 6 slides, consistent across the carousel.
- Keep all text and the JustPreneur wordmark within a safe margin of 72px from every edge (left/right/top/bottom) to avoid UI crop on feed thumbnails and avoid the profile-grid crop losing content.
- For slide 1, keep the headline block within the vertical range of roughly y=850 to y=1150 (within the 1350 canvas) so it clears both the top status-bar overlap zone and the bottom caption/UI overlap zone on-device.
- For slides 2-4, keep the center dividing line and both column text blocks at least 72px clear of the left/right edges; column body text should not run closer than ~56px to the center divider itself, to avoid text looking like it's touching the seam.
- Do not place any critical text in the outer ~8% bleed on any edge, since Instagram's grid/thumbnail crop can clip slightly inward on some surfaces (Explore, profile grid).

## 8. Slide-to-Slide Consistency Rules

- JustPreneur wordmark: identical size, color treatment, and corner position on every slide (top-left), except slide 6 where it may step up slightly in visual weight as the save/share beat — position stays the same.
- Eyebrow label treatment (gold, small-caps, tracked) is identical in type size/position across slides 2-4; slide 1/5/6 do not use an eyebrow (they're not two-column comparison beats).
- The center dividing line (slides 2-4) is identical in weight, color, and position across all three — this is what makes the "Headline vs. Infrastructure" device feel like one consistent visual system rather than three separate layouts.
- Navy base background is the shared constant across all 6 slides; only the accent (gold vs. steel-gray) and image mood shift column-to-column. This is essential — a reader swiping should feel one deck, not six disconnected designs.
- Column order is fixed throughout: THE HEADLINE always left, THE INFRASTRUCTURE always right. Never swap sides between slides 2, 3, and 4 — consistency here is what lets a fast-swiping thumb track the argument without re-reading labels each time.
- Typography family, weight steps, and minimum sizes are identical across all 6 slides per section 4.

## 9. Accessibility and Contrast Check

- Warm white (#F7F3EC) text on deep navy (#0B1A2B) background: contrast ratio is approximately 14:1 — comfortably exceeds WCAG AA (4.5:1) and AAA (7:1) for body text at any size used here.
- Gold (#D9A441) text/accents on deep navy: contrast ratio is approximately 6.5-7:1 depending on exact gold value chosen — meets AA for body text and is at/near AAA; recommended for eyebrows and numerals (larger text), not for long body paragraphs. Keep body copy in warm white, reserve gold strictly for short labels/numerals/accents as specified above.
- Steel gray (#5A6472) on navy is lower-contrast by design (used for secondary/caveat text) — confirm any steel-gray text sits at ~16px-equivalent-or-larger effective mobile size and is never the sole carrier of essential information (always paired with or followed by full-contrast warm-white copy).
- On slide 1, the scrim gradient is mandatory, not optional — without it, headline-over-photo contrast cannot be guaranteed across varying image content. Do not ship slide 1 without the scrim.
- No information in this carousel should ever depend on color alone (e.g., "gold column = temporary, gray column = permanent" is reinforced by the eyebrow text labels "THE HEADLINE"/"THE INFRASTRUCTURE" on every slide, not by color coding alone) — this protects colorblind readers.

## 10. Thumb-Stop Hook (Cover Slide Specifically)

Slide 1 needs to stop a scrolling thumb in under a second. The hook is the contradiction embedded directly in the headline wording itself ("borrowed" vs. "owned") paired with a single, striking image of Dhar Mann mid-keynote under a visible spotlight — the spotlight literally visualizes "the stage." The headline typography should be large enough to be read while still scrolling (not require a stop-and-squint), and the dek line should be small enough that it doesn't compete — it's a caption, not a second headline. No logos, no AWNY event branding reproduced, no extra graphic elements on slide 1 beyond the scrim and the JustPreneur wordmark — maximum simplicity on the one slide that has to win the swipe decision in the grid/feed preview.
