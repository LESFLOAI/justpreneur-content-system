## 0. Hard constraints carried into every visual decision

1. **No real executives or named individuals depicted.** Bela Bajaria and Peter Rice are named in Slide 3's approved copy; the unnamed "leadership" quote appears in Slide 2. None of these people may appear as faces, recognizable likenesses, posed portraits, or named silhouettes in any generated image. Their names appear only as added typography (verbatim from approved copy), never as a subject requested inside an AI image prompt. No human figures of any kind appear in any motif in this set — the entire visual spine is abstract/iconographic.
2. **No real logos or trademarked marks.** Netflix is a real, named company; its wordmark, "N" mark, and red/black brand system are trademarks. No image-generation prompt asks for the Netflix logo, Netflix's specific brand red, a streaming-interface mockup styled to resemble Netflix, or any show/title art. The word "Netflix" is added only as typography in the design tool (it is in the approved copy), never rendered by an image model. **Note on color:** this story's own color-coding uses red for Oct 14 — that red is a story-specific sequencing marker (see §5), never styled to resemble Netflix's brand red/wordmark treatment; keep it a clean, flat, geometric accent color, not a brand-mimicking gradient or typographic style.
3. **Primary visual metaphor: a building horizontal timeline/sequencing strip.** The spine of this carousel is a clean, abstract horizontal strip with three evenly spaced circular nodes, representing the three days in the story (Oct 14, Oct 15, Oct 20). The strip is cumulative and cannot regress: each slide shows exactly the same strip in the same position, with only the node-lighting state changing (lit/bright = "today's news," dimmed/settled = "already happened," unlit/outline = "not yet"). This single device carries the entire editorial logic — a deliberate build across the week — without ever requiring a literal calendar, clock, or dashboard illustration.
4. **Dense factual copy must not compromise mobile legibility.** Slides 2, 3, and 4 each carry a two-part structure (a short bold lead stat/fact, then a smaller supporting line). Each uses a two-tier hierarchy — larger bold lead, smaller supporting detail — so no slide becomes a wall of same-size text. No wording is trimmed, reworded, or paraphrased; only size/weight/line-break hierarchy is applied to the verbatim approved copy.
5. **No invented labels beyond what's implied by the approved copy's own structure.** Short chyron headers restate each slide's own date or content only ("OCT 14," "OCT 15," "OCT 20," "THE PATTERN," "THE TAKEAWAY," "YOUR MOVE") — no slide-count badges ("1/7"), no additional dates, figures, or citations beyond what's already in the approved copy, no production marks, no extra icons implying unapproved claims (e.g., no literal "resignation" or "stock chart" icon that could be read as adding information).
6. **JustPreneur wordmark must be visible and correctly spelled** on every slide, in a small, consistent position, per §3/§7.
7. **No fabricated numbers or claims.** All figures (~1,000 roles, 6%, 12%, 44%, $4.8B) render only as approved text added in the design tool — never as a different number invented by an image model's background data-vis, and never as readable numerals baked into any generated graphic.
8. **Red/blue/green is a sequencing code, not a traffic-light value judgment.** Per the Strategist's direction, red = Oct 14, blue = Oct 15, green = Oct 20 — strictly a date-identification system, not "red = bad, green = good." Do not let any image-generation prompt introduce alarm iconography (warning triangles, siren shapes) on the red node, or celebratory iconography (confetti, trophies) on the green node — all three nodes use identical simple circular geometry, differing only in hue and lit/dimmed state.

## 1. Overall creative concept

Treat this as a **business/culture-media explainer** — visually closer to a premium trade-press timeline graphic (Bloomberg/Puck/Axios-style "here's the sequence" explainer) than a motivational quote template. The story's entire thesis is about *order* and *deliberate sequencing*, so the visual system must physically demonstrate sequencing, not just describe it.

The carousel runs on a single **visual spine: a horizontal three-node timeline strip** that appears, in the same position, on every informational slide (2 through 5 and 7). As the carousel is swiped, the strip **builds left to right and never resets**: Slide 2 lights node 1 (red/Oct 14); Slide 3 keeps node 1 dimmed-but-visible and lights node 2 (blue/Oct 15); Slide 4 keeps nodes 1-2 dimmed and lights node 3 (green/Oct 20); Slide 5 is the payoff — all three nodes lit simultaneously at full brightness, the completed sequence, directly under the "THE PATTERN" header. This gives the reader a literal, swipeable experience of "watching the week assemble in order," which is the entire point of the story.

**Slide 1 (Cover)** teases the device without revealing it — three faint, unlit outline nodes on the strip, establishing "there's a sequence coming" before any color appears.

**Slide 6 (Takeaway)** strips the strip away for a **text-only, high-contrast minimal treatment** (flat deep navy, no icons) so the thesis statement lands with maximum clarity, matching prior JustPreneur carousels' "cleared board" convention for the pivot/thesis beat — but keeps one small callback: a thin three-segment red/blue/green divider line beneath the headline, the only color note on an otherwise pure-navy slide, so the sequencing motif is never fully abandoned.

**Slide 7 (CTA)** returns to a related but forward-looking device: three small empty outline squares (not nodes) tinted red/blue/green respectively, echoing "the hard news / the change / the win" as a reusable decision checklist the reader can apply to their own announcements.

## 5. Color palette

**Brand base (unchanged):** deep navy, steel gray, warm white, energy gold — used for all text, wordmark, headers (apart from the red/blue/green date-underline accents), and chyron-style labels throughout.

**Story-specific sequencing code (the recurring color layer — strictly a date-identification system, never a value judgment per §0.8):**
- **Energy-red** = Oct 14 (Slide 2's node, header underline, dimmed/settled appearances on Slides 3, 4, 5) — a clean, flat, geometric red, never Netflix's specific brand red, never paired with alarm iconography.
- **Energy-blue** = Oct 15 (Slide 3's node, header underline, dimmed/settled appearances on Slides 4, 5) — a clean, flat, cool blue, distinct from steel gray.
- **Energy-green** = Oct 20 (Slide 4's node, header underline, appearance on Slide 5) — a clean, flat green, never paired with celebratory iconography.
- These three colors appear **only** on: the timeline-strip nodes, the thin date-header underline accents, the Slide 6 tri-color divider line, and the Slide 7 checklist-tile outlines. They never bleed into body text, background fields, or the JustPreneur wordmark.
- **Dimmed/settled node state:** same hue, reduced brightness/saturation (roughly 35–40% of full brightness) and smaller glow radius than the "active/today" node — visually distinct from "unlit" (steel-gray outline, no fill at all) and from "active" (full saturation, strongest glow).
- **Slide 1 teaser nodes:** steel-gray outline only, no red/blue/green fill at all — color is deliberately withheld until each day's own slide, so the first color reveal always happens on that day's slide.
- **Slide 6 (thesis):** flat deep navy base, warm white text, one gold emphasis phrase, and the single thin tri-color divider line only — no node/circle imagery.
- JustPreneur wordmark: energy gold on navy, identical across all 7 slides.

## 9. Accessibility and contrast check

- Warm-white text (#F5F3EE-equivalent) on deep-navy base (#0B1E32-equivalent) comfortably clears WCAG AA contrast for both large headline text and the smaller body/detail text used on slides 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, provided body text stays at or above roughly 32px cap-height equivalent at 1080px width (text-heavier slides must not shrink text below this floor — if a slide's full approved copy cannot fit above this floor within the body-copy zone in §7, flag for QC rather than shrinking further).
- Energy-red, -blue, and -green are used only on small graphic elements (nodes, tile outlines, thin underlines/divider) against the deep-navy base — never as small body text color — since colored text on navy at small sizes risks inconsistent contrast across the three hues. All body copy and headlines stay warm white (or gold for the two designated pull-lines) regardless of which date the slide covers.
- Energy-gold text/elements are reserved for the two short pull-lines ("that's sequencing" on Slide 6, the Slide 7 CTA line) only — never full paragraphs — since gold-on-navy body copy at small sizes loses comfortable contrast on compressed mobile screens.
- Steel-gray is used only for the "unlit/not yet" node outlines and the Slide 1 teaser — always against the deep-navy base, never as body text, since steel-on-navy contrast is weaker than warm-white-on-navy at small sizes.
- No text is ever placed directly over the motif's higher-detail area (the strip/nodes or tiles); a clear vertical gap separates the motif zone from the text block on every slide (per §7).
- Minimum single-line text size: headline/lead lines no smaller than ~56px cap-height equivalent; body/detail text on slides 2, 3, 4, 5, 7 no smaller than ~32px cap-height equivalent; Slide 6's thesis lines are the largest text in the carousel.
- Slide 7's three questions use no smaller than ~36px cap-height equivalent each, with generous spacing so none competes with or crowds another.
