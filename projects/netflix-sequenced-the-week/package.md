## Strategic Angle (Strategist)

**"Sequencing the News"** — the real story isn't that Netflix cut jobs or beat earnings, it's that it controlled the order the world learned both. Lesson: treat the order of your own announcements (setbacks, changes, wins) as a deliberate design choice — order shapes whether people read you as struggling or disciplined. Generalizes to any entrepreneur/small business owner, not just a mega-cap company.

## Final Caption

In one week, Netflix told the world three different stories — in a specific order.

Oct 14: ~1,000 layoffs (6% of the workforce). Co-CEOs Ted Sarandos and Greg Peters called the business "strong and growing" and on track for its full-year forecast.

Oct 15: Content chief Bela Bajaria departs after nearly 10 years. Peter Rice, former chairman of Walt Disney Television, steps in Oct 26.

Oct 20: Q3 revenue up 12%, net income up 44%, EPS beat estimates — alongside a lowered ad-revenue forecast (the company pointed to execution delays, not softening demand) and $4.8B in buybacks, Netflix's largest buyback quarter on record. Shares still dipped in after-hours trading.

None of these facts are in dispute. What's worth studying is the order they arrived in: hard news, then change, then proof.

You don't need a $400B media company's platform to use this. Your next hard conversation, team change, or win is going to land on your calendar whether you plan for it or not. The only real choice you have is what comes first.

Audit your next three announcements. Decide the sequence before the calendar decides it for you.
