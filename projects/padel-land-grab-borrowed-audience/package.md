## Facts Summary (Approved Facts Block, condensed)

Padel is expanding simultaneously as a hospitality business in Colombia and New York City, at very different financial scales.

- **Colombia:** The newly recognized Federación Colombiana de Pádel (FCP, recognized 2025) reports via industry census data roughly 233 padel clubs / 687 courts across Colombia as of early 2026, in 51–53 cities depending on source. Colombian gym chain **Spinning Center Gym** entered padel under the **Spin Padel** brand, opening a nine-court club at Centro Comercial San Rafael, north Bogotá (announced August 2024, opened second half of 2024).
- **New York City:** **Padel Haus** (founded 2022) closed a $7M Series B in July 2025 at a $33M valuation, at least $18M disclosed funding to date. **Reserve Padel** operates six clubs total — two in Manhattan, three in the Miami area, and a sixth location in Palm Beach County that is "opening soon," not yet operational. Kith founder **Ronnie Fieg** opened **Kith Ivy**, a members-only wellness/padel club at 120 Leroy Street, West Village, reportedly priced at a $36,000 initiation fee plus $7,000 annual dues (first reported by Emily Sundberg's "Feed Me" newsletter, corroborated by multiple outlets). The **Pro Padel League** announced a $15M Series A on March 24, 2026 (led by Rick Schnall), following a $10M seed round in March 2025.
- **Intentionally excluded:** Robert Farah / any "Padel House by Farah" claim — could not be independently verified, dropped per Fact Verifier recommendation.
- **Hedges carried throughout:** US national court-count totals are disputed and not cited as a single number; Colombian COP revenue/investment figures are census-sourced estimates/projections, not audited fact (and were not used in this copy at all).

## Final Caption

Two brands made the exact same move into padel — at opposite ends of the market.

In Colombia, gym chain Spinning Center Gym opened a nine-court club under the Spin Padel brand at Centro Comercial San Rafael in north Bogotá (announced August 2024, opened later that year).

In Manhattan, Kith founder Ronnie Fieg opened Kith Ivy — a members-only wellness and padel club in the West Village, reportedly priced at a $36,000 initiation fee plus $7,000 in annual dues (first reported by Feed Me, corroborated by multiple outlets).

Neither is a padel startup. Both are existing brands that redirected an audience they already owned into a new category — one at gym-membership scale, one at ultra-luxury scale.

Meanwhile, the capital flowing into dedicated padel startups is real: Padel Haus closed a $7M Series B in July 2025 at a $33M valuation (at least $18M disclosed to date), and the Pro Padel League just landed a $15M Series A in March 2026.

But the fastest entries into the category weren't the startups raising rounds. They were the brands that already had an audience — and pointed it somewhere new.

Before you look at the market for your next move: look at what you already own.

Save this. Then audit your own audience.
