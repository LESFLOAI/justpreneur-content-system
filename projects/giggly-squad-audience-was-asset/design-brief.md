## 0. Hard constraints carried into every visual decision

These come directly from the approved copy, the strategist's visual guidance, and brand-safety rules, and they override the general brand defaults wherever they conflict:

1. **No real-person likenesses.** Hannah Berner and Paige DeSorbo are named public figures whose names appear in the approved copy (Slide 2). No image-generation prompt below asks for a recognizable likeness, face, or identifiable portrait of either woman. Any human presence in generated art is hands-only, back-of-frame, silhouette, or a generic/anonymized figure — never a posed "two women podcasting" portrait that could read as an attempt at their likenesses.
2. **No fabricated logos or trademarked UI.** Instagram, SiriusXM, Amazon, Acast, iHeartPodcast Awards, The Ankler, and Like & Subscribe are all real, trademarked brands/platforms named in the copy. No prompt asks the image generator to reproduce any of their logos, wordmarks, or exact app interfaces. The "IG Live grid" visual is rendered as a **generic, unbranded live-stream video-grid app mockup** (tiles, a pulsing "LIVE" dot, a viewer-count style element) with no Instagram logo, no Instagram color-gradient camera icon, and no real UI chrome copied from the actual app.
3. **Every hedge/attribution phrase stays intact as text.** "Reportedly," "per The Ankler/Like & Subscribe (citing sources familiar with the process)," "per trade reports," and "not officially confirmed by SiriusXM" are added verbatim as a type layer after image generation — see §3 and §11. No visual may imply the $140M–$160M figure or the deal terms are officially confirmed (e.g., no "OFFICIAL" stamp, no confetti/celebration cliché that reads as confirmed triumph, no press-release-mockup graphic).
4. **No implied guarantee for the viewer.** This is a business-editorial case study, not a "you can do this too" promise. Slide 7's reflective question is visual-neutral — no "your turn" trophy, no "you're next" iconography, no dollar-sign-rain, no motivational-poster clichés.
5. **No tabloid framing.** No paparazzi-style photo treatment, no gossip-column red/yellow color cues, no exclamation-heavy graphic treatment, no "SHOCKING" or "BREAKING" banner styling anywhere. This stays in modern business/culture editorial territory (think Puck, The Ankler's own visual register, or Sportico) — confident and clean, not sensational.
6. **No deal-document mockups.** Slide 6 covers the announced deal, but the visual must not render a fake contract, term sheet, or signature page — that risks looking like a fabricated real document. Use abstract representation instead (see §3, Slide 6).

## 1. Overall creative concept

The story is a **six-year timeline compressed into one climbing structure** — a literal visual "ladder" or ascending rail that runs through the whole carousel, starting low and plain at Slide 2 (an unglamorous 2020 IG Live, no studio, no deal) and rising, slide by slide, to a stylized "multiple distributors converging" graphic at the top by Slides 5–6. The reader should feel the climb physically as they swipe, without any slide needing extra labels to explain it — the ladder's height relative to the frame tells the story on its own, reinforced by the approved copy's own inline dates (2020, 2026 SXSW, September 17, 2026).

Visual language: **modern business/culture editorial** — clean grotesk type, generous negative space, restrained photographic/abstract art, gold used as an accent for growth/achievement beats only. Not a hustle-culture motivational template, not a tabloid deal-leak graphic.

Two textures recur:
- **The plain/early texture** (Slides 2–3): flatter, more restrained, less gold — a live stream with a handful of viewers, unglamorous.
- **The ascended/later texture** (Slides 4–6): more light, more gold accent, more visual weight — an award, a converging-interest graphic, an abstract "signed" moment — without ever tipping into confetti-cliché celebration.

Slide 7 pulls back to a calm, spacious navy field for the reflective CTA — quiet rather than triumphant, since the question is meant to make the reader think, not to sell an outcome.

## 2. Composition and focal hierarchy (applies to all 7 slides)

- **Top ~40–48% of every slide:** clean navy or warm-white field reserved for headline/body copy — no busy visual detail behind text.
- **Lower ~50–58%:** the slide's visual art, including its position on the continuous ladder motif.
- **The ladder motif:** a thin vertical gold rail running along the right edge of every interior slide (Slides 2–6), roughly at x: 980–1020px, from y: 150px to y: 1250px. A single small solid-gold dot marks that slide's "rung" position on the rail — low on Slide 2, rising slide over slide, reaching near the top by Slide 6. This is a pure graphic element (line + dot), **no text, numbers, or dates on the rail itself** — the rail shows position, the approved copy's own dates carry the meaning. Slide 1 shows the full rail compressed bottom-to-top as a preview; Slide 7 shows the rail fading out into open space (the "your ladder starts here" idea, implied purely visually, never stated as a promise).
- **JustPreneur wordmark:** small, gold, centered in the bottom safe margin on every slide, identical size/position throughout.

## 4. Typography direction

- **Headline/cover typeface:** bold, semicondensed grotesk (the family used across prior JustPreneur carousels — keep consistent). Sentence case for the long Slide 1 headline, broken across 2–3 lines at natural phrase points, e.g.:
  - Line 1: "They Went Live on Instagram in 2020."
  - Line 2: "In 2026, Distributors Competed to Own It."
- **Subhead (Slide 1):** smaller, gold, single line, letter-spaced slightly.
- **Body copy (Slides 2–7):** warm white, regular-to-medium weight, generous line-height, left-aligned or centered consistently across all six slides (pick one alignment and hold it — recommend left-aligned for these longer, more information-dense lines, since centered alignment reads awkwardly once lines wrap to 3–4 lines as on Slides 5–6).
- **Attribution clauses (Slide 5's "per The Ankler/Like & Subscribe (citing sources familiar with the process)" and Slide 6's "reported as," "Reported value," "per trade reports," "Not officially confirmed by SiriusXM"):** same body typeface, no special script/italic treatment needed, but may drop one step in size relative to the lead sentence on that slide (never smaller than the accessibility floor in §9) so the lead fact stays visually primary while the hedge stays fully legible and un-cropped — never truncated, never dropped to an ellipsis.
- No script fonts, no tabloid/gossip-column display lettering, no ALL-CAPS body paragraphs (kickers/kicker-style words are not used in this deck — the deck relies on body copy alone per the approved text).

## 5. Color palette

**Brand base (unchanged):** deep navy, steel gray, warm white, energy gold.

**Story-specific accent layer (background art only, not text):**
- Early beats (Slides 2–3): more steel-gray, more restrained gold, flatter — reflects the unglamorous, no-studio, "just showing up" origin.
- Later beats (Slides 4–6): warmer gold weighting, more light — reflects rising momentum, capped at Slide 6 by the "not officially confirmed / restrained" rule in §0.3.
- Slide 7: returns to a calm, spacious navy field with only a soft gold fade at top — deliberately the quietest slide, not the flashiest.
- Avoid: any red/yellow tabloid-gossip color cue, any "confetti" or "cash" green, any color combination that could evoke a specific real company's brand palette (e.g., do not lean into SiriusXM's or Amazon's brand colors when rendering the Slide 5–6 abstract graphics).

## 6. Image treatment

- Clean, restrained, editorial — soft gradients and abstract geometric/light forms rather than busy photographic detail, so the dense body copy (especially Slides 5 and 6) stays legible without a heavy scrim fighting for space.
- Where any human presence appears (Slide 2's phone-holding framing), it is hands/forearm only or over-the-shoulder — no visible face, no identifiable likeness.
- No text baked into any generated image anywhere — all copy, including every hedge phrase, is added as a type layer after generation.
- No fake logos (Instagram, SiriusXM, Amazon, Acast, iHeartPodcast Awards, The Ankler), no fabricated document/contract mockups, no watermarks, no small corner icons, no slide-count labels, no dates baked into the art (dates live only in the approved text layer).

## 8. Slide-to-slide consistency rules

- The gold ladder rail (thin vertical line, right edge) and its rising dot position appear on every slide (compressed preview on Slide 1, fading out on Slide 7) so the reader can track progress through the swipe without needing added labels.
- Same grotesk type family and same body-copy alignment (left-aligned, per §4) on Slides 2–7; Slide 1 is the only centered-headline exception, consistent with prior JustPreneur cover-slide convention.
- JustPreneur wordmark identical size/position/opacity on Slides 2–6; scaled up slightly only on Slides 1 and 7 (open/close weighting), consistent with prior carousel conventions.
- Gold accent weighting increases gradually from Slide 2 through Slide 6, then resets to a quiet, mostly-navy field on Slide 7 — this arc must read consistently; no slide should "jump" gold-heavy out of sequence.

## 9. Accessibility and contrast check

- Warm-white body text on deep-navy top field clears WCAG AA at the body-copy sizes needed for Slides 2–7; gold is reserved for the Slide 1 subhead and any single emphasized word, never for full paragraph body copy (gold-on-navy body paragraphs drop below comfortable contrast at mobile scale).
- **Slide 5 and Slide 6 are the contrast-risk slides** — they carry the longest text blocks and the hedge/attribution phrases that must not be dropped or shrunk into illegibility. Both slides get the largest clean-field allocation in the deck (48% and 58–60% respectively, vs. ~45% on Slides 2–4) specifically to protect this. If body copy still threatens to crowd the safe margins at these sizes, reduce the visual art area further before reducing text size or line-height — text legibility and completeness (including every hedge word) takes priority over decorative art size on these two slides.
- No text sits directly over high-frequency art detail without the clean navy/warm-white field behind it — the top-field convention in §2 is mandatory on every slide, not optional.
- Minimum body-copy size should render no smaller than roughly 40–44px cap-height equivalent at 1080px width so the longer Slide 5/6 paragraphs remain readable on a phone without zooming.
