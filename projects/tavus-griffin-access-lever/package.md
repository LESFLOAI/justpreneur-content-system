## Facts Summary (Approved by Fact Verifier — Cleared with qualifications)

- On October 1, 2026, AI video company Tavus announced "Griffin," which it calls the first "Human Interaction Model" (HIM) — a full-duplex, video-to-video AI designed to see, listen, and respond in real time (nods, verbal acknowledgments) during live video calls. Fronted by co-founder/CEO Hassaan Raza and Head of Research Ioannis Patras (also a Professor of Computer Vision/ML at Queen Mary University of London).
- The tested version is "Griffin-Lite." In a study **Tavus designed, ran, and reported itself — not independently verified or replicated** — 26 of 54 participants (48%) on a one-minute video call with a Griffin-Lite avatar said they believed they were talking to a real person. Tavus's own prior model stack (Phoenix-4.5, Sparrow-2, Raven-1) scored 1 of 41 (2.4%) under comparable conditions.
- According to Tavus, Griffin-Lite scored on NVIDIA's independently-developed VideoFDB benchmark (a real academic benchmark, not a Tavus metric): **3.83/5 on "generation"** vs. 3.92 for humans, and **3.73/5 on "perception"** vs. 4.20 for humans — the perception gap is wider. These are self-reported leaderboard submissions, not an independent re-test.
- As of 2026-10-03, Griffin-Lite is a **closed research preview for select trusted testers only** — not available to the public or to Tavus customers, no public pricing, API, or model card. Tavus ties the limited release to ongoing AI-disclosure-safety work.

## Final Caption

On October 1, Tavus introduced Griffin — what it's calling the first "Human Interaction Model," a full-duplex AI built to see, listen, and respond on live video calls in real time. Fronted by CEO Hassaan Raza and Head of Research Ioannis Patras (also a professor at Queen Mary University of London).

The headline number: in a study Tavus designed, ran, and reported itself — not independently verified or replicated — 48% of participants (26 of 54) said they thought they were talking to a real person after a one-minute call with the tested version, Griffin-Lite. That's up from 2.4% for Tavus's prior model stack under comparable conditions.

Tavus also self-reported scores on NVIDIA's independent VideoFDB benchmark: 3.83/5 on generation (humans: 3.92) and 3.73/5 on perception (humans: 4.20) — a wider gap on perception than generation, and a leaderboard submission, not a third-party re-test.

Here's the part worth sitting with: Griffin-Lite isn't available. Not to the public, not to Tavus's own customers. No pricing, no API, no model card — just a closed preview for select testers, which Tavus links to its ongoing AI-disclosure-safety work.

That's not a flaw in the rollout. It's the rollout. Pairing an unverified number with zero outside access turns scarcity into both a safety story and a credibility shield — nobody outside the room can stress-test the claim while it spreads. (To be clear: that's an observation about incentives and timing, not a claim that Tavus's numbers are false.)

Access is a strategic lever, same as product or pricing — especially before your proof points are checked by anyone but you. Worth asking how you're sequencing yours.
