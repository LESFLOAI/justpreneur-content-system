## 1. Verified facts (confirmed by multiple independent reports and/or primary filing)

- Pop Mart International Group Limited is listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange under stock code **9992** (listed since December 11, 2020). *Source: HKEX/company IPO records.*
- Pop Mart released its **H1 2026 interim results after market close on Thursday, August 20, 2026** (HKEX filing dated 2026-08-20, filing ref 2026082000353). *Primary source: HKEX filings (hkexnews.hk, filing 2026082000353.pdf).*
- **H1 2026 revenue: RMB 17.17 billion (RMB 17,172,921 thousand), up 23.8% year-on-year** (prior-year period RMB 13,876,276 thousand). This is commonly rounded to "+24%" in press coverage.
- **Gross profit RMB 11.97 billion, up 22.6% YoY; gross margin 69.7%, down 0.6 points YoY.**
- **Profit attributable to owners of the Company: RMB 5.04 billion, up 10.1% YoY.**
- **Adjusted net profit: RMB 5.16 billion, up 9.5% YoY** (adjustment for share-based compensation). This matches claim #5 exactly once "net profit" is correctly identified as the figure growing 9.5% (not revenue — revenue grew 23.8%/~24%).
- On **August 21, 2026** (the trading session after the release), Pop Mart shares **fell as much as 8.8% intraday and closed down 3.1% at HKD149 per share**. *Strongest source: Yicai Global, Aug 21, 2026.*
- Pop Mart announced a **share buyback of up to RMB 2–5 billion (≈US$740 million) to be executed over the next six months**, disclosed alongside the H1 2026 results. *Sources: Yicai Global; SCMP (scmp.com/business/china-business/article/3364719).*
- **Labubu/"The Monsters" franchise revenue concentration has materially declined between reporting periods:**
  - **Full-year 2025: ~38.1%** of total revenue (also reported elsewhere as "close to 40%"), on Monsters-series sales of roughly RMB 14.2 billion, up ~365% YoY.
  - **H1 2026: Monsters/Labubu franchise revenue was RMB 4.45 billion, now ~26% of total H1 revenue** — a significant drop in concentration from the FY2025 figure. **The 38.1% figure is a 2025 full-year number and must never be presented as the current (H1 2026) concentration figure.** The current, reportable figure for the story's news hook is ~26%.
  - A new IP, **Twinkle Twinkle, generated RMB 2.65 billion in H1 2026, up 580.6% YoY**, making it one of the company's fastest-growing lines and a genuine second IP pillar (not merely a dessert-menu flavor name).
- Pop Mart's **March 25, 2026 full-year-2025 results** triggered a stock decline reported across outlets in the **~20–23% range** (Reuters: "more than 20%"; AP: "nearly 23%"; Bloomberg/The Edge Singapore: "record 23%"; one outlet: "22.5%"). The claim's "~22–23%" framing is reasonable but note the spread of reported figures (20%–23%) across sources.
- **May 2026**, ahead of Pop Mart's Q1 2026 trading update, **Morgan Stanley cut its full-year 2026 revenue growth estimate to 13%**, versus the company's own "no less than 20%" guidance; Morgan Stanley also cut its price target to HK$247 from HK$278. Deutsche Bank's Sammi Xu reportedly modeled a 2% revenue *decline* for 2026. *Sources: Business Times/The Edge Singapore coverage (via longportapp), dated on/around May 12, 2026.*
- **Pop Bakery**: Pop Mart has opened bakery/dessert retail concepts, with the first location in Qinhuangdao, China (after testing via 30+ dessert-truck pop-ups), and its first international bakery in Singapore (opposite Universal Studios Singapore, opened ~July 2026), selling ~45 toy-themed pastries/drinks priced S$5–S$32. Pop Mart has said it is considering Europe/US locations and further Southeast Asia expansion (Thailand, Indonesia, Malaysia), timing not yet confirmed. *Source: Inside Retail Asia (July 2026); Oman Observer/DealStreetAsia.*
- **Home appliances**: Pop Mart has launched a real, if small, home-appliance line. A limited-edition "The Monsters"/Labubu-themed refrigerator (999-unit global cap, two variants "Home" and "House of the Monsters," RMB 5,999) launched via JD.com in late April 2026 (sources disagree: Yicai says April 24; China Daily/The Standard say April 30). CEO Wang Ning said in March 2026 the company would extend IP franchises into small appliances (coffee machines, electric toothbrushes, hair dryers also reported). One brokerage estimated total 2026 home-appliance revenue at only ~RMB 487 million, about **1% of company revenue** — i.e., currently a minor, early-stage diversification line, not yet material.
- **Labubu feature film**: In March 2026, Pop Mart and **Sony Pictures Entertainment** announced a live-action/CGI hybrid Labubu feature film in **early development**, with Paddington director Paul King attached to direct/produce and Steven Levenson (Dear Evan Hansen) co-writing the script. Separately, Pop Mart has reportedly launched its own film studio and registered a copyright for the Season 1 script of an unrelated **animated series**, "Labubu與朋友們" (Labubu and Friends) — these are two distinct projects and should not be conflated.

## 2. Attributed claims (require explicit attribution language in copy)

- "Could miss its 20% full-year growth target" — **this is Yicai Global's characterization** of CEO Wang Ning's remarks on the H1 2026 results call ("may not hit the 20% annual target... pressure in H2 will be greater than H1"). Required language: *"according to Yicai Global's report on CEO Wang Ning's comments"* — NOT "Pop Mart confirmed it will miss its target" or any phrasing implying a confirmed miss. **Important disagreement to flag:** at least one other outlet's paraphrase of the same results characterized management's position more optimistically — that ~20% growth remained "likely achievable" though H2 would be tougher. Copy must not resolve this tension into a flat statement; use "may fall short of / is at risk of missing" rather than "will miss" or "missed."
- "Citing cooling overseas demand for Labubu" — Yicai's own headline frames this as an **overseas sales slump** at the regional/company level (overseas revenue -11.9% YoY in H1 2026; Asia-Pacific -10%, Americas -17%, Europe/other +5.9%), not a Labubu-product-specific figure. No source in this check gave a Labubu-specific overseas unit/revenue breakdown for H1 2026. Required softening: attribute the decline to "overseas sales" broadly, and note Labubu/Monsters is the dominant overseas product rather than stating overseas Labubu-specific data exists.
- Buyback size and framing ("softer domestic sales and normalizing demand following last year's Labubu-driven surge") — attribute to SCMP/Yicai reporting of company statements.
- Morgan Stanley's 13% estimate and Deutsche Bank's -2% estimate — attribute explicitly to each bank; these are analyst forecasts, not company guidance or confirmed outcomes.
- March 2026 stock-decline percentage — attribute the specific number used (recommend "~20–23%, reported variously across outlets") rather than asserting a single precise figure as fact.

## 3. Reasonable inferences (label clearly as inference, not fact)

- That Pop Mart is likely to hold a **Q3 2026 trading update in October 2026** (by analogy to the October 21, 2025 Q3 update). **This is NOT confirmed.** No company announcement or confirmed calendar date for a Q3 2026 trading update was found in this check. One financial calendar listing showed only "Q2 2026 Earnings Release (Projected) 25/08/2026" and "Q4 2026 release 31/03/2027" with no Q3 2026 date — and calendar projections are not company confirmations. Any reference to an upcoming Q3 update must be explicitly flagged as an inference/expectation based on prior-year timing, not a scheduled fact.
- That the broader diversification push (bakery, appliances, film, new IP) is a *direct, management-stated response* to Labubu-dependency concerns is a reasonable inference supported by analyst commentary (e.g., Morningstar's Jeff Zhang calling the Sony film deal "a milestone in diversifying revenue streams"), but is analyst interpretation, not a verbatim company statement of strategic rationale.

## 4. Claims that must be removed or softened

- **Remove/correct:** Describing **Skullpanda** as "new IP." Skullpanda is an **existing, previously established Pop Mart secondary character line** (not new), now simply being re-emphasized by analysts (Citigroup) as a growth driver alongside Twinkle Twinkle and Crybaby. If Skullpanda is mentioned, it must be described as "an existing IP being pushed harder," not "a new line."
- **Soften:** "Small home appliances" as a diversification pillar should be qualified as **very early-stage and currently immaterial** (~1% of estimated 2026 revenue per one brokerage estimate), not presented as a comparable growth pillar to Labubu or Twinkle Twinkle.
- **Soften:** "Labubu movie in script development" should be corrected to **"Labubu feature film in early development with Sony Pictures (Paul King directing; Steven Levenson co-writing the script, announced March 2026)"** — avoid implying the film is imminent or confirmed to be released, and do not conflate it with the separate in-house animated series.
- **Do not state** that Pop Mart "will miss" or "has missed" its 20% target — no fiscal year has closed yet (H1 2026 only); only "may fall short of" / "is at risk of missing" is supported.
- **Do not carry forward the 38.1% Labubu-concentration figure as current.** It is a FY2025 figure. Current reporting-period (H1 2026) concentration is ~26%, which is itself a materially different, now-declining-dependency story worth highlighting — the drop in concentration partially undercuts a straightforward "hit-dependency" narrative and should be reflected honestly.

## 5. Missing context that could materially change interpretation

- The Monsters/Labubu share of revenue **falling from ~38–40% (FY2025) to ~26% (H1 2026)** is itself evidence that diversification is already working to some degree — this cuts against a simple "Pop Mart is still dangerously dependent on one hit" framing and should be incorporated, not omitted.
- Revenue is still growing robustly (+23.8% YoY in H1 2026); the concern reported by analysts is specifically about **profit-margin/net-profit deceleration (single-digit growth, 9.5%)** and **overseas deceleration**, not an overall revenue collapse.
- China domestic revenue grew 47% YoY to RMB 12.2 billion (71% of H1 total) while overseas revenue fell ~12% — the "overseas slowdown" is the specific driver of concern, domestic demand remains strong.
- Multiple outlets disagree on exact stock-decline percentages for both the March 2026 event (20–23%) and the August 2026 reaction (3% vs. "more than 3%" vs. the specific 3.1%/HKD149 figure from Yicai) — flag precision only to the sourced figure, avoid false precision when citing secondary paraphrases.
- No Q3 2026 trading-update date has been confirmed; readers/downstream copy should not imply a specific upcoming date.
