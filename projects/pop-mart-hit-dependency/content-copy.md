## Final Caption

Pop Mart's Aug 21, 2026 earnings call made headlines for the wrong reason: according to Yicai Global's report on CEO Wang Ning's remarks, growth may fall short of its 20% target, tied to an overseas sales slowdown — plus a CNY 2–5 billion buyback to steady the stock.

But the headline buried the real story. Labubu's share of Pop Mart's revenue had already fallen from 38.1% in FY2025 to roughly 26% in H1 2026 — before the slowdown ever became news.

The market flinched twice this year: Pop Mart's stock fell 20–23% in March on fears its growth was slowing, and in May, Wall Street analysts (Morgan Stanley, Deutsche Bank) slashed their 2026 growth estimates well below the company's own guidance. Pop Mart wasn't reacting to either moment — the shift in its revenue mix was already showing up in the numbers.

Diversification isn't a crisis response. It's a decision made early enough that the crisis doesn't define you.
