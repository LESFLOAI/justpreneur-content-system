## Facts Summary (Approved Facts Block, cleared by Fact Verifier)

British chef Thomas Straker built a large social media following — currently more than 2.5 million followers on each of Instagram and TikTok — with viral cooking and butter videos that he began posting while working as a private chef during the 2020 COVID lockdown. In 2022, he opened his debut restaurant, Straker's, at 91 Golborne Road in Notting Hill, London, which became one of the city's most in-demand reservations (per Resy booking data cited by Time Out London). Straker is now opening his first restaurant outside the U.K. — Straker's New York — at 59 Grand Street in Soho, the address that housed Keith McNally's bistro Lucky Strike for roughly three decades (1989–2020) before McNally closed it in April 2020, citing a landlord dispute during the pandemic. As of September 30, 2026, Straker's NYC has not been confirmed as publicly open: press coverage as of late September 2026 described the opening as imminent ("next week"), and the space had so far hosted at least one private event (a Net-a-Porter New York Fashion Week party on September 10, 2026) ahead of any confirmed public opening. No investors or financial backers for the New York location have been publicly disclosed in available reporting.

**Hard constraints honored in all copy below:** never "has opened"/present-tense-operating language — always "hasn't opened," "still pending," "opening imminently," or equivalent; no "self-funded" claim — backing stated as undisclosed; McNally's stated closure reason attributed directly to him ("McNally has said..."), never narrated as settled fact; no implication of a Straker/McNally partnership or concept-copying relationship — site history framed strictly as a change of operator at the same address; the Sept 10 Net-a-Porter event framed strictly as private/invite-only, never as a public preview.

## Final Caption

Straker's New York hasn't opened. But it already ran the test.

On September 10, the space at 59 Grand Street — formerly home to Keith McNally's Lucky Strike (1989–2020, closed amid what McNally has said was a landlord dispute) — hosted a private Net-a-Porter Fashion Week party. Invite-only. Not a public opening.

Weeks later, as of September 30, the public opening is still pending — reported as imminent, but not confirmed.

Chef Thomas Straker has built things this way before. Since going viral as a private chef during the 2020 lockdown, he's grown to 2.5M+ followers on both Instagram and TikTok, and turned his 2022 Notting Hill debut into one of London's hardest reservations, per Resy data cited by Time Out London.

The lesson isn't about restaurants. It's about sequencing. Run the riskiest, most exposing version of your work privately — under real conditions — before you ever call it "open."

Opening day isn't your first performance. It's your last rehearsal, dressed up as a debut.
