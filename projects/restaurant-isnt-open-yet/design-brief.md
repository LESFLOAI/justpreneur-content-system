# Visual Design Brief — "The Restaurant Isn't Open Yet — That's the Point." (6-slide timeline carousel)

Status: DRAFT MODE — no images generated yet (no image-generation API key available in this cloud environment). All content below is ready for `/justpreneur-finish` to execute later.

## 1. Overall creative concept

Frame this as a quiet, confident business-editorial timeline — closer to a Bloomberg/Puck/Fortune "how we got here" explainer than a hospitality or lifestyle post. The story is not "here's a hot new restaurant," it's "here's everything that had to happen before anyone could even call it open." Every slide stays in a state of *before* or *in-between* — never a state of *open and operating*. A single recurring graphic device — a thin horizontal gold timeline line along the bottom edge of every slide, carrying one small circular marker whose state (unlit / lit / gapped / resolved) advances slide to slide — ties the six beats together visually so the "timeline" concept reads instantly, even without reading the words: dim/unlit marker (cover) → lit marker (viral rise) → brighter marker moving right (first restaurant) → marker continuing right (new address, mid-transition) → lit marker, a visible gap, then a hollow marker (private event, then still no public opening) → single steady, fully-lit marker (closing reflection).

No slide depicts a crowded, actively-serving dining room, a "now open" moment, or any signage/branding for either operator. Thomas Straker and Keith McNally are referenced only through the approved copy text and through anonymous, faceless imagery (hands, silhouettes, empty rooms, exteriors) — never through a rendered likeness of a real named person, and never through any composition that visually pairs both men or both eras together (which would risk implying a partnership/collaboration that did not happen — it was a change of operator at the same address).

## 2. Composition and focal hierarchy

Every slide follows the same top-to-bottom hierarchy:
1. Headline or body-copy block — upper portion of frame, largest text weight per slide's role (headline treatment on Slides 1 and 6; body-paragraph treatment on Slides 2–5)
2. Photographic/graphic focal environment — lower half to two-thirds of frame, always a quiet/empty/in-between space (kitchen, facade, empty pass), never an active service scene
3. Thin gold timeline line + single marker — fixed position along the bottom edge, identical on every slide, marker state advancing per the sequence above
4. JustPreneur wordmark — small, fixed bottom-center position, identical across all six slides

## 4. Typography direction

- Headline typeface (Slides 1 and 6 only): a bold, high-contrast editorial serif with a business/culture-press feel (e.g., a Tiempos Headline / Canela / Freight Display style) — signals premium journalism, not a motivational template.
- Body copy typeface (Slides 2–5): a clean grotesk/sans (e.g., Söhne / Inter / Neue Haas style) for maximum mobile legibility at small sizes.
- No kicker labels, no slide numbers, no date tags added as separate small text anywhere — all dates and facts (2020, 2022, Sept 10/30, 2026) appear only within the approved body-copy sentences themselves, never as extra corner labels.
- Numeral callouts on Slide 2 ("2.5M+" x2): extra-bold, gold, set noticeably larger than body copy to function as a pull-quote-style stat, but never overlapping the body paragraph.
- Headline sizing: no line should exceed ~34–38 characters at the chosen weight; target minimum ~64px effective headline size on the 1080-wide canvas so it stays legible at feed-thumbnail size.
- Body copy sizing: minimum ~40–44px effective size at 1080 width, line-height ~130–140%, left-aligned (never justified). Slide 4's body block is the longest in the set — confirm it comfortably fits within its reserved zone without shrinking below the minimum size; if it runs long, tighten line-height slightly before reducing font size.
- Slide 5's two-part body block: separate the "Sept 10" line and the "Sept 30" line with a visible paragraph break or a thin rule, so the reader instantly perceives the gap in time, mirroring the timeline-marker graphic below it.

## 5. Color palette

- Deep navy (#0B1220–#10172A range) — dominant background/shadow tone, identical across all six slides
- Steel gray (#4B5563 range) — secondary surfaces (stone, countertop, facade tones)
- Warm white (#F5F1E8 / #FAF7F0) — primary text color on dark backgrounds and warm highlight tones
- Energy gold (#C9A24B / #D4AF37 range) — timeline line + marker, numeral callouts, the "Where are you running yours?" line, and the wordmark
- No red, no additional accent colors — this story is quieter and more restrained than a "collapse/scandal" story; keep the palette disciplined across all six slides for cohesion

## 6. Image treatment

- All backgrounds desaturated toward navy/gray with warm gold/warm-white highlight pops, consistent color grade across all six slides so the carousel reads as one continuous set.
- Apply a subtle dark navy gradient overlay (~40–55% opacity) behind every text zone on every slide to guarantee contrast regardless of the underlying photo.
- No text, logos, signage, or real identifiable faces should be generated inside the AI images themselves — all copy is added in a design tool afterward per this brief. AI-generated backgrounds supply mood, environment, and negative space only.
- No depiction of any actively-serving, crowded, or "now open" dining room on any slide — every environment must read as empty, closed, in-transition, or invite-only-and-quiet.
- No split-screen or dual-era composition on Slide 4 — render the 59 Grand Street environment as one unified present-day scene to avoid any visual suggestion of a McNally/Straker partnership or collaboration.

## 7. Instagram safe-margin instructions

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 (portrait) for all six slides, consistent throughout the carousel.
- Keep all text and the wordmark within a safe zone of at least 96px from every edge. Instagram's feed UI can crop into the very top ~90px and very bottom ~250px of the preview — keep headline/body anchor points starting no higher than ~150px from the top, and keep the wordmark comfortably above ~120px from the absolute bottom edge (the timeline line itself can sit lower, in the ~1290–1310px band, since it is a thin graphic device rather than text).
- Keep body copy line length under ~860px wide (leaving ≥110px margins each side) for comfortable mobile reading, with extra care on Slide 4 and Slide 5 where body copy is longest.

## 8. Slide-to-slide consistency rules

- Identical canvas size (1080 x 1350) on all six slides.
- Identical thin gold timeline line + marker position along the bottom edge on every slide, with the marker's lit/unlit/gapped state advancing in this exact order: unlit (1) → lit, left (2) → brighter, moved right (3) → continuing right (4) → lit-then-gap-then-hollow (5) → single steady centered marker (6).
- Identical JustPreneur wordmark size/position/color (bottom-center, just above the timeline line) on every slide.
- Identical color grade (navy/gray/gold) across all six backgrounds — no slide should shift cooler or warmer than the others; this is a steady, disciplined story, not a dramatic reversal.
- Identical body-copy typeface, size range, and left-alignment convention on Slides 2–5; Slides 1 and 6 share the centered editorial-serif headline treatment as the carousel's bookends.
- No slide may include a crowd, a signage/logo, or a rendered likeness of Thomas Straker or Keith McNally.

## 9. Accessibility and contrast check

- Warm white (#F5F1E8) text over the navy-gradient-overlaid backgrounds targets a contrast ratio well above WCAG AA (4.5:1) for normal text — confirm the gradient overlay is opaque enough behind every line of text, not just the general background, especially over the golden-hour facade on Slide 3 and the string-light glow on Slide 5.
- Gold (#C9A24B) is reserved for large display text (numeral callouts, the Slide 6 question line, the timeline line/marker) on dark backgrounds only — do not set gold body copy at small sizes, where contrast drops.
- Minimum text size on mobile: no body text should render below ~40px effective size on a 1080px-wide canvas, so it remains legible at feed-thumbnail scale.
- Slide 4's longer body paragraph and Slide 5's two-part body block are the highest contrast-risk zones (most text over photographic detail) — prioritize a slightly heavier gradient overlay behind text on these two slides specifically during layout.
