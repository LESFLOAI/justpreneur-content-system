## Facts Summary (Approved Facts Block)

Blank Street, the NYC-founded coffee chain known for small-format shops (historically about 350–500 sq ft, largely without seating) using Eversys semi-automated espresso equipment, closed a funding round reported at approximately $105 million (about $75 million in new primary capital plus roughly $30 million in secondary share sales), led by General Atlantic, with existing investors including Left Lane Capital, General Catalyst, and Tiger Global also participating. Press reports (Financial Times, Axios, Daily Coffee News) value the company at approximately $650 million following the round, up from a reported ~$500 million valuation in mid-2025; these figures come from investor/press reporting, not an independently confirmed company statement. An SEC Form D tied to the round was reportedly filed in late August 2026, per Daily Coffee News' review of the filing; this has not been independently verified via EDGAR. Blank Street opened its first Los Angeles-area and first West Coast location in Studio City, CA (13273 Ventura Blvd) on August 12, 2026. As of the most recent available reporting (through approximately early September 2026), the company had announced plans — not confirmed openings — for additional Los Angeles-area locations in Beverly Hills, West Hollywood (Sunset Plaza), and Malibu, targeted for "late fall" 2026; their status as of October 2026 could not be confirmed in this review. The company's total store count is reported variously as approximately 100 to 106 locations across the US and UK as of mid-2026, with some churn (including a reported NYC East Village closure in August 2026). Separately, Bloomberg has reported that Blank Street is piloting a larger-format (~1,300 sq ft) cafe concept with seating in 2026, representing a shift away from its original tiny, seatless store model — this should be treated as an active, concurrent strategic change, not a settled characterization of the brand's current format.

**Angle:** "The Model That Made You Famous Isn't the Model That Keeps You" — Blank Street raised capital and expanded geographically while concurrently piloting a seated format roughly 4–5x larger than its original tiny, seatless boxes, illustrating that the lean model that earns attention isn't automatically the model that sustains an audience relationship.

## Final Caption *(corrected per QC: "nearly 4x" → "roughly 3x")*

Blank Street got famous for having no chairs.

Tiny boxes. Historically about 350–500 sq ft. Fast espresso on semi-automated machines. Largely no seating. That model built a chain reported at roughly 100–106 locations across the US and UK — and came alongside a new funding round that press reports (FT, Axios, Daily Coffee News) value at approximately $650M.

But Bloomberg reports the brand is also piloting something very different in 2026: a cafe roughly 3x the size — with seats.

Same brand. Two models, reportedly running at the same time.

Here's the thing about scaling: the model that gets you attention isn't guaranteed to be the model that keeps your audience. What works to get noticed and what works to build a relationship over time aren't always the same thing — and the operators who plan for that shift early don't get caught flat-footed by it later.

Full breakdown — and every hedge we're not willing to skip — in the slides.
