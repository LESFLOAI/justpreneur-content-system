## 0. Hard constraints carried into every visual decision

These override the general brand defaults wherever they conflict. All are non-negotiable — flag them clearly to whoever runs generation locally.

1. **No real company logos, wordmarks, or trade dress.** Ticketplus and Autoentrada are real, named, currently operating companies. No generated image contains a readable "Ticketplus" or "Autoentrada" wordmark, no attempt at their real logos, no fabricated substitute logo either. All signage/screens/scanners in generated art are blank, turned away from camera, or softly out of focus. Company names, the ticker, and every stat live in the copy layer only (added after generation) — never faked as on-screen branding inside any AI-generated image.
2. **No real-person likeness.** Eric López, Autoentrada's founder, is a named real individual who stays on as Country Manager per the approved copy. No prompt asks for a recognizable face. Any human presence in generated art is hands-only, rear-view, over-the-shoulder, or fully silhouetted — never a posed portrait that could be read as an attempted likeness.
3. **No fabricated financial/exchange chyrons or tickers.** "NYSE American: TP" is a real listing. Do not generate a stock-ticker board, exchange floor, or news-chyron graphic with readable (even gibberish-but-ticker-shaped) text, invented ticker symbols, price charts, or "BREAKING"-style banners. Any trading-floor/financial mood is conveyed through abstract light and architecture only — see slide 6 prompt. The ticker and date live in the copy layer only.
4. **No claim to depict specific real venues, offices, or buildings photographically.** Autoentrada's and Ticketplus's actual offices, specific Argentine stadiums/theaters, and the real NYSE American exchange are not to be reproduced as identifiable real places. Backgrounds are generic, editorial-style representative environments (a generic mid-size arena entrance, a generic modern glass boardroom, a generic Buenos Aires–style theater facade abstracted at the level of architectural mood, not a specific landmark).
5. **No national flag or government emblem treatment.** Argentina's scale (18 provinces) is conveyed through an abstract map-outline/pinpoint-light graphic, not an official flag or seal, to avoid any implied government or civic endorsement.
6. **No text baked into any generated image.** All headlines, kickers, dates, stats, and the progress rail are added afterward as a type layer — see §11. This also avoids AI text-rendering artifacts, which are especially likely given how much of this story's visual vocabulary (tickets, tickers, signage) is naturally text-shaped.
7. **Approved figures are exact and non-negotiable.** "18 Argentine provinces," "55,000+ events," and "18 million+ tickets issued since the brand launched in 2005" must appear in the type layer exactly as written in the approved copy — no rounding, no abbreviating (e.g., never "18M+"), no restating in different units. Large-numeral typographic treatment (§4) may isolate the numeral visually for impact, but the full exact phrase must still be legible on the slide.

## 1. Overall creative concept

This is a **business-editorial case-file** — the visual language of a Bloomberg Businessweek or Fast Company deal-analysis feature, not a "congrats on the exit!" founder-hype template. The core visual idea: **quiet, compounding operating discipline is what eventually gets bought.**

The carousel is built as an accumulating case file rather than a strict calendar timeline (the copy itself moves from 2019 launch, to an operating-scale stat, to a 2005-origin stat, to a 2026 announcement — not a straight line of years). To hold that together visually, every interior slide (2–7) carries a persistent **progress rail** — a slim gold horizontal line with six small circular checkpoint markers near the top of the frame — that accumulates across the sequence exactly like building a case file: each new slide lights one more checkpoint gold while prior checkpoints stay lit and future ones stay dim. The six checkpoints are labeled (small, in the rail itself): LAUNCH → REACH → VOLUME → SCALE → ANNOUNCEMENT → TERMS. By Slide 7, all six are lit. This gives a scrolling viewer a spatial sense of "the file is building" without adding a single extra word of copy.

Two visual textures alternate with the story's phase:
- **Operating years (Slides 2–5):** warm, textured, ticket-and-venue reality — box-office light, ticket-stub paper grain, marquee glow, a lit map of a country running on one brand's licensed rails. This is the "seven years of quiet execution" half of the story.
- **The deal (Slides 6–7):** cooler, more architectural, modern-glass-and-light textures — an acquisition being decided in a boardroom/finance register, not a party. This is the "ownership changes hands" half.

The final slide (8) breaks from the photographic case file entirely and resolves as a clean navy-and-gold quote card — the piece's single moment of direct address, holding the takeaway with maximum legibility and zero competing texture.

It's this:") sits smaller, directly above it.

## 4. Typography direction

- **Headline/cover typeface:** bold, semicondensed grotesk (same family used across prior JustPreneur carousels — keep consistent). Slide 1 headline set exactly as given, in caps, per the approved copy; subhead in sentence case directly beneath it, smaller.
- **Kicker (dates/stage labels, interior slides):** all-caps, bold, gold, noticeably larger than a typical kicker label elsewhere in the brand system — the stage of the deal *is* the story's spine here and should read at a glance in a fast scroll.
- **Body copy:** warm white, regular-to-medium weight, generous line-height, word-for-word from the approved copy — no paraphrasing, no shortening, no altered figures (§0.7). Slide 7 carries the longest block; keep it at a slightly smaller but still comfortably legible size on that slide only, rather than shrinking type globally.
- **Oversized numeral treatment (Slides 4 and 5 only):** the key figure ("55,000+" and "18 million+") is set as the single largest, boldest type element on its slide, in gold, with the remainder of the exact sentence ("events managed." / "tickets issued since the brand launched in 2005.") set directly beneath it in a smaller warm-white line. This is a line-break/scale choice for visual impact only — no word or numeral is abbreviated, rounded, or altered from the approved copy.
- **Slide 8 pull-quote:** largest single treatment in the whole carousel. The setup line ("The lesson isn't 'get acquired.' It's this:") sits smaller, in warm white, directly above the main lesson sentence, which is set large and centered, warm white with the closing clause ("...that owning you becomes the platform's most efficient move.") optionally weighted in gold for emphasis — this is the "save this" slide and should be the most typographically confident frame.
- No script fonts, no faux-ticket-stub "admit one" novelty lettering, no stock-ticker digital/LED font cliché anywhere — despite the subject matter, this stays in JustPreneur's own premium-business-editorial voice, not a themed pastiche.

## 6. Image treatment

- Photographic, editorial-documentary texture throughout Slides 2–7 — natural/practical light sources (marquee bulbs, box-office glass, boardroom window daylight, spotlight glow), shallow depth of field, believable material texture (ticket paper grain, glass, brushed steel, polished concourse floor) rather than flat vector/illustration style.
- Operating-years slides (2, 3, 4, 5) get a warmer, richer amber/cream color grade; deal slides (6, 7) get a cooler, more desaturated steel-navy grade — the contrast itself carries the "seven years of running it well" vs. "the deal" beat without any extra text.
- No text baked into any generated image anywhere — all copy, kickers, figures, and the progress rail are added as layers after generation (see §11).
- No reproduction of the real Ticketplus or Autoentrada wordmarks/logos, no fabricated substitute logos, no readable stock-ticker or exchange-board text (see §0.1, §0.3). Signage/screens in frame are always blank, turned away, or softly out of focus.
- Human presence, where used at all, is hands-only, rear-view, or silhouetted — no posed portraits, no recognizable faces (see §0.2).

## 7. Instagram safe-margin instructions

**Carousel (1080x1350):** Minimum 72px margin on all sides for any text or wordmark. Interior slides (2–7): keep the top ~35% of the frame (through y ≈ 380) clean and low-detail for the progress rail + kicker + body copy stack; the generated visual occupies the lower ~55–60%, full-bleed to the side margins. Cover (Slide 1): headline block centered with equal top/bottom breathing room, no rail. Slide 8: entirely typographic — reserve generous margin around the pull-quote (at least 100px top/bottom beyond the 72px minimum) so it reads as a deliberate quote card, not a cramped text dump. JustPreneur wordmark centered in the bottom safe margin, identical size/position, on all 8 slides.

## 8. Slide-to-slide consistency rules

- Progress rail position, checkpoint size, labels, and gold/dim styling stay pixel-identical across Slides 2–7 (and its completed form on Slide 8) — only the number of lit checkpoints changes.
- Kicker style (position, size, gold color) stays identical across all six interior slides; only the label content changes.
- The warm/cool grade alternation (§6) must be visually consistent every time a given "phase" reappears, so a viewer feels the rhythm of operating-years vs. deal-stage without rereading the copy.
- The oversized-numeral treatment on Slides 4 and 5 uses identical scale, weight, and placement logic so the two "stat" slides read as a matched pair.
- JustPreneur wordmark treatment (size, position, opacity) is identical across all 8 slides.
- Grotesk type family is identical throughout — no font switching between slides.

## 9. Accessibility and contrast check

- Warm-white text (#F5F3EE-equivalent) on a navy scrim (#0B1E32-equivalent) at ≥70% scrim opacity clears WCAG AA for large text at carousel scale.
- Gold kicker/numeral text (energy-gold, e.g., #D4A017-equivalent) is reserved for short, large-size labels and the oversized figures on Slides 4/5 — never for full paragraph body copy, since gold-on-navy body text at small sizes drops below comfortable contrast on compressed mobile screens.
- Body copy is never placed directly over high-frequency photographic texture (ticket-stub grain, glass reflections, marquee bulbs) without a navy scrim between text and image — scrim is mandatory on every interior slide, not optional, especially on Slide 7 where the copy block is longest.
- Minimum body-copy size renders no smaller than is comfortably legible at arm's length on a phone; Slide 7 (longest copy) should be checked first during local assembly since it has the least margin for error.
