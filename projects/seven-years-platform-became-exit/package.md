## Facts Summary (Approved Facts Block, cleared by Fact Verifier)

Ticketplus Ltd. (NYSE American: TP), a Santiago, Chile-headquartered live-entertainment ticketing technology company founded in 2014 and operating across 11 Latin American markets, began trading on NYSE American on August 7, 2026, with its IPO formally closing August 10, 2026 ($8.00/share, ~$15 million raised). On September 21, 2026, Ticketplus announced the acquisition of Autoentrada S.A., a long-established Argentine ticketing company based in Córdoba with roots dating to 1999 and a commercial brand launched in 2005 — its first acquisition since the IPO. Autoentrada has operated on Ticketplus's platform as a licensed white-label partner since 2019, running under its own brand across 18 Argentine provinces, having managed more than 55,000 events and issued more than 18 million tickets since 2005. The acquisition converts Autoentrada from a licensed partner into a directly owned Ticketplus operation, giving Ticketplus direct control over promoter relationships, payments, access control, and analytics in Argentina; financial terms were not disclosed. Because Autoentrada's volume was already reflected in Ticketplus's consolidated platform figures, the company says the deal is not expected to materially affect consolidated fiscal 2026 results. Autoentrada founder and president Eric López joins Ticketplus as Country Manager for Argentina.

## Final Caption

Autoentrada didn't spend seven years trying to outgrow its platform.

It spent seven years proving it was the best possible operator of it — 18 provinces, 55,000+ events, 18 million+ tickets, all under its own brand, all running on Ticketplus's licensed technology since 2019.

On September 21, 2026, that track record became the deal. Ticketplus (NYSE American: TP) acquired Autoentrada outright — its first acquisition since its August 2026 IPO. Founder Eric López is now Country Manager for Argentina.

The company isn't calling this a growth story — Autoentrada's volume was already counted in Ticketplus's numbers. What it actually is: proof that a license, run well for long enough, can quietly become an offer.

If you're operating on someone else's platform — white-label, franchise, licensed infrastructure — your operational record isn't just how you deliver the service. It might be the asset you're building toward.

What would your seven-year track record be worth to the platform you run on?
