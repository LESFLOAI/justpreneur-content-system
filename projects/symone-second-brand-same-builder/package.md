## Story / Facts Summary

Chef DeVonn Francis — founder of the Jamaican/Caribbean-rooted culinary studio Yardy (est. 2017) and executive chef for MoMA PS1's 50th-anniversary gala in May 2026 — is opening Symone, billed by MoMA as his first standalone restaurant, in a café space inside MoMA PS1 in Long Island City, with an announced opening window of "early October 2026" (no specific calendar date has been publicly confirmed). Symone is a French-African café drawing on Francis's Jamaican/Caribbean heritage, named by Francis for his niece, according to MoMA press materials. The space is designed by architectural studio Marsollier Villacorta, reusing historic details of the PS1 schoolhouse building, and includes terrace dining. The menu includes curry chicken salad sandwiches, patties, cod-and-rice porridge, soft serve, seasonal pastries, and La Cabra coffee. The café is accessible as part of general museum admission, with no separate ticket required.

**Not asserted anywhere in this package:** a specific calendar opening date; any specific financial/lease/revenue-share deal structure between Francis and MoMA PS1 (undisclosed in all sources reviewed); any link between this opening and MoMA PS1's separate Jan 1, 2026 free-admission initiative (funded by a Sonya Yu gift) — that initiative is deliberately left out of this package entirely, as it is a distinct announcement.

## Final Caption

DeVonn Francis already had a working brand. Yardy — his Jamaican/Caribbean-rooted culinary studio, founded in 2017 — built his name.

So when he got the chance to open what MoMA is billing as his first standalone restaurant, inside MoMA PS1 no less, he didn't just put the Yardy name on the door.

He built Symone. A French-African café, named for his niece (per MoMA press materials), opening in the window MoMA has described as "early October 2026." Different menu language. Different format. Different brand.

That's the part worth sitting with: growth doesn't always mean stretching what you've already built further. Sometimes the smarter move is starting something distinct — built for the new context, not bent to fit the old one.
