## 2. Required Corrections (ranked by severity)

### #1 — HIGH SEVERITY: Image prompts depict active construction for a project that is "not yet built"
The visual director's image-generation prompt for **Slide 4, right half** describes photographic-style imagery of **"a construction crane and steel scaffolding silhouette against a navy dusk sky, evoking something being built for the long term."** This is not an abstract rendering — it is specified as a photographic-style background showing active construction equipment. The Approved Facts Block and the Fact Verifier's non-negotiable constraint #5 are explicit that Creator City is "slated (as announced, not yet built/operational)." A crane and scaffolding visually assert that construction is already underway, directly contradicting the adjacent text on the same slide ("targeting Super Bowl weekend, Feb 2027" with no groundbreaking confirmed) and the explicit "Announced. Not built yet." language on Slide 3.

Secondary, lower-severity instance of the same issue: **Slide 2, right half** prompt includes "an empty flat plot of land / early construction site... survey stakes and a simple low-rise building foundation outline visible." This is somewhat more defensible since it's framed as an "architectural rendering style" (concept/plan imagery, not documentary photography) and survey stakes are consistent with pre-construction planning — but "building foundation outline visible" edges toward implying a structure is already being laid out physically.

**Required fix before any image is generated (`/justpreneur-finish`):** Revise the Slide 4 right-half prompt to remove the crane and scaffolding (active-construction equipment) and replace with pre-construction/concept-stage imagery — e.g., an architectural blueprint, a site plan rendering, an aerial view of an empty surveyed lot, or a simple navy/steel line-art campus elevation drawing. For Slide 2's right half, soften "building foundation outline visible" to something like "a faint sketched site-plan outline overlaid on an empty lot" to keep the imagery unambiguously pre-construction.

### #2 — MEDIUM SEVERITY: Caption opening phrasing deviates from the Fact Verifier's mandated construction
The caption opens: **"Dhar Mann just became Advertising Week New York's first 'Chief Creator Officer'..."**
The Fact Verifier's Required Attribution Language (Section 4) is explicit: use "Advertising Week New York **named** Dhar Mann its first 'Chief Creator Officer' for the 2026 event" — **"not 'Mann became' or 'Mann is now' without the event qualifier."** The caption does append the event qualifier in the same sentence ("an event title for AWNY 2026... not a new corporate role"), which meaningfully mitigates the risk of a reader inferring an ongoing executive role. Still, the caption uses exactly the construction the Fact Verifier called out as disfavored, and the fix is trivial.

**Required fix:** Change the caption's opening sentence to lead with Advertising Week as the subject, matching the mandated framing used correctly elsewhere in the package (e.g., Slide 2's "AWNY names Dhar Mann..."): **"Advertising Week New York just named Dhar Mann its first 'Chief Creator Officer'..."**

### #3 — LOW-MEDIUM SEVERITY: Slide 2 copy risks a mid-swipe misreading on Creator City's construction status
Slide 2's right-column body reads: **"Behind the keynote stage, Mann is building something meant to outlast the week: Creator City."** The present-progressive "is building" is not corrected to "announced/not yet built" until Slide 3. A reader who stops swiping at Slide 2 (a realistic scenario in a 6-slide carousel) could reasonably conclude physical construction is already underway, which is not supported by the Approved Facts Block.

**Required fix:** Soften Slide 2's right-column line to something like "Behind the keynote stage, Mann has announced something meant to outlast the week: Creator City." or "...is planning something meant to outlast the week." This keeps the metaphorical "borrowed stage vs. owned infrastructure" argument intact while not requiring Slide 3 to retroactively correct an impression Slide 2 already created.

### #4 — LOW SEVERITY: Internal inconsistency in the Visual Director's own brief about whether Slide 1 depicts Dhar Mann or a generic figure — plus a caption-pairing risk
Section 10 of the Visual Director's brief (Thumb-Stop Hook) describes the cover concept narratively as "a single, striking image of **Dhar Mann** mid-keynote under a visible spotlight." But the actual Section 11 image-generation prompt for Slide 1 deliberately avoids naming or specifically describing Mann's likeness, instead specifying a generic **"confident male creator-entrepreneur in his late 30s"** with no identifying features tied to the real Dhar Mann. This generic/non-likeness choice is the right call — it avoids the real legal/reputational risk of an AI image-generator producing an unauthorized, possibly inaccurate likeness of a specific, identifiable living public figure (right of publicity / misrepresentation risk) — but the brief should say so explicitly and consistently rather than describing the same cover concept two different ways in two sections.

There is a second, related risk worth flagging: Slide 1's dek is **"Dhar Mann at Advertising Week New York, Oct 2026."** directly beneath a generic, non-likeness AI-generated image. A viewer has no way to know the image isn't an actual photo of Mann. Pairing a real named person + real named event in the dek with a generic AI-generated stand-in image (with no disclosure) creates a mild misrepresentation risk, even though no likeness is actually used.

**Recommended fix (required before finish, low-cost):** (a) Align Section 10's language with Section 11's actual prompt — state explicitly in the brief that Slide 1 intentionally uses a generic, non-likeness figure rather than attempting to generate Dhar Mann's actual likeness, and why. (b) If a rights-cleared real photo of Mann from the event becomes available before finish mode, prefer that over an AI-generated generic stand-in for the cover. (c) If the generic AI image is used, consider either loosening the dek's specificity (e.g., "Advertising Week New York, Oct 2026" without naming Mann directly next to an unrelated face) or making clear in slide treatment that the image is illustrative/stylized rather than documentary.
