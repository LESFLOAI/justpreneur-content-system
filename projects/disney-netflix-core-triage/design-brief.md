## 0. Hard constraints carried into every visual decision

These override general brand defaults wherever they conflict, per the assignment notes:

1. **No real executives depicted.** Bob Iger or any real Disney/Netflix executive must never appear — no faces, no recognizable likenesses, no silhouettes implying a specific named person. Any human presence in generated art is hands-only, abstract silhouette, or fully iconographic (e.g., a generic sorting-desk hand placing a card into a bin) — never a posed portrait of anyone identifiable.
2. **No real logos or trademarked character art.** Disney, Netflix, Hulu, Pixar, Marvel, Star Wars, and all named title properties ("Percy Jackson," "Ice Age," "Will Trent," "Soul," etc.) are real trademarks. No image-generation prompt asks for their logos, wordmarks, brand-specific color systems, or any character likeness from any of these franchises. All platform/company references in generated art use neutral, abstract, streaming-platform-agnostic iconography (generic screen/interface panels, generic film-reel/folder/box motifs) — never a literal mark.
3. **Primary visual metaphor: vault + triage/sorting.** The spine of this carousel is a "vault door" device (what's protected vs. what moves) paired with a "sorting/triage" device (PROTECT vs. DISTRIBUTE) introduced in slide 6 and echoed in slide 7. These two devices carry the entire editorial logic without needing any real brand imagery.
4. **Dense factual copy must not compromise mobile legibility.** Slides 2, 3, and 4 contain long, detail-heavy paragraphs (dates, title lists, windows). Every such slide uses a two-tier typographic hierarchy — a short bold lead/kicker line at larger size, then the fuller supporting detail in a smaller but still clearly legible body size — so the slide never becomes a wall of same-size text. No wording is trimmed, reworded, or paraphrased to make this fit; only size/weight/line-break hierarchy is applied to the verbatim approved copy.
5. **No invented labels.** The only slide headers used are the ones already present in the approved copy itself ("The Deal," "What's Leaving the Vault," "What's Notably Absent," "The Lesson," "Translate This to Your Catalog," "Your Move"). No slide-count badges ("1/7"), dates, citation marks, or production marks are added anywhere.
6. **JustPreneur wordmark must be visible and correctly spelled** on every slide, in a small, consistent position, per §3.

## 1. Overall creative concept

Treat this as a **business/culture-media explainer**, visually closer to a premium trade-press graphics package (think a Puck or Bloomberg explainer card) than a motivational quote template — appropriate for a story about licensing strategy and IP triage, not a lifestyle moment.

The carousel runs on a **two-device visual spine**:

- **The Vault** (slides 1–4): an abstract, premium vault-door motif that progressively opens (light spilling, boxes/folders visible) as the carousel reveals what's moving, then visibly begins closing again on slide 4 as the story turns to what's staying locked. This single device shows "something is leaving, something is staying" without needing any real title or brand art.
- **The Triage Desk** (slides 6–7): an abstract sorting-desk/conveyor motif with two visual lanes — a gold-sealed "PROTECT" lane and a steel-arrowed "DISTRIBUTE" lane — that gives the reader-facing takeaway (their own catalog) a concrete sorting action to visualize.

**Slide 5 (The Lesson)** strips all of this away for a **text-only, high-contrast minimal treatment** — flat deep navy, no vault, no icons — so the piece's thesis statement lands with maximum clarity at the carousel's midpoint, exactly as established in prior JustPreneur carousels' "cleared board" convention for the pivot/thesis beat.

## 2. Composition and focal hierarchy

- Slides 1, 5: full-bleed minimal background, headline vertically centered — these are the two "punch" slides (hook and thesis).
- Slides 2, 3, 4, 6, 7: a contained graphic motif occupies roughly the **top 30–38%** of the frame (vault door detail, sorting desk, etc.), with the remaining **62–70%** kept as a clean, low-detail navy field reserved for the two-tier text block (lead line + body copy). This ratio is deliberate — it protects readability on slides carrying the heaviest factual copy (2, 3, 4) by keeping the generated-image footprint small and text-safe.
- JustPreneur wordmark: small, steady, top-left corner, identical position/size/opacity on every slide (1–7) — a consistent watermark presence, never competing with headline weight. (Per brand guidance, this wordmark is added as a text/logo layer in the design tool, not relied upon from image generation.)
- Slide numbering/arrows: none added — Instagram's native carousel dots communicate position; no custom "1/7" labels per §0.5.

## 4. Typography direction

- **Carousel headline/lead family:** bold grotesk/sans (the family already established across JustPreneur posts — a Neue-Haas-Grotesk-Condensed-style weight), sentence case, warm white.
- **Slide 1 cover headline:** heaviest weight available, two-line stack, largest optical size on that slide, centered, warm white with "That's The Lesson." allowed a subtle gold tint on "The Lesson" only (short emphasis, not a full repaint) to foreshadow the recurring gold = "protect/the lesson" coding used later.
- **Slide headers (2, 3, 4, 6, 7):** small, bold, all-caps or title-case label (matching the approved copy's own header text exactly — "The Deal," "What's Leaving the Vault," etc.), warm white at ~85% opacity, positioned top-left beneath the JustPreneur wordmark, acting as a chyron-style section tag.
- **Lead lines (2, 3, 4, 6, 7):** bold grotesk, larger size than body, warm white — the "first thing your eye reads" after the header.
- **Body/supporting detail (2, 3, 4):** same grotesk family, regular/medium weight, smaller size, generous line-height (≥1.3x) for scanning dense factual detail (dates, title lists) comfortably on a phone.
- **Gold pull-lines** ("Non-exclusive = reach, not giveaway." / "Flagship IP doesn't." / "ARE the moat" / "PROTECT or DISTRIBUTE"): set in energy gold, same grotesk family, bold, slightly larger than surrounding body text so each lands as a standalone emphasis moment — never gold for full paragraphs.
- **Slide 5 thesis lines:** heaviest weight, largest size in the entire carousel (this is the pivot beat — it should be the single most dominant line across all 7 slides), warm white, with "ARE the moat" in gold caps as the one color break.
- No script fonts, no decorative/legal-document lettering — keep the single editorial grotesk system consistent across all 7 slides.

## 5. Color palette

**Brand base (unchanged):** deep navy, steel gray, warm white, energy gold — used for all text, wordmark, headers, and chyron-style labels throughout.

**Story-specific coding layer (recurring motifs only):**
- **Vault motif (slides 1–4):** deep navy base with warm gold interior light when open (slides 1–3), shifting to a cooler, more desaturated navy-steel tone as the vault visibly closes (slide 4) — gold = "what's moving/lit," steel/dark = "what's staying sealed."
- **PROTECT lane (slide 6, and the PROTECT stamp on slide 7):** energy gold — padlock icon, stamp text, rim light.
- **DISTRIBUTE lane (slide 6, and the DISTRIBUTE stamp on slide 7):** steel gray — arrow icon, stamp text, rim light.
- **Slide 5 (thesis):** flat deep navy only, no gold/steel accents except the single "ARE the moat" gold-caps break, to maximize contrast at the pivot.
- JustPreneur wordmark: energy gold on navy, identical across all 7 slides.

## 6. Image treatment

- Clean, flat-lit, graphic-design-forward treatment throughout (not photographic/cinematic) — premium motion-graphics-plate quality, consistent with a business/culture trade-press explainer, not lifestyle photography.
- Vault-door and sorting-desk motifs rendered as simple, bold, dimensional graphic illustrations with soft directional lighting — never cluttered, never more than 2–3 distinct graphic elements visible per slide's motif zone.
- Abstract streaming-interface panels (slide 2) are generic rectangular screen/panel shapes with soft glow only — no app icons, no UI chrome, no recognizable platform layout.
- Content-card/folder/box shapes (slides 3, 6, 7) are plain geometric placeholders (rounded rectangles, simple folder silhouettes) — never styled to resemble any real title's key art, poster, or franchise iconography.
- No text, logos, or real identifiable people/faces generated inside any AI image — all headers, headlines, body copy, and stamp words are added in the design tool afterward per this brief. AI-generated art supplies motif, mood, and negative space only.
- Every motif zone is confined to the top 30–38% of each non-punch slide (§2) specifically to preserve the large, clean lower field needed for dense body copy — this is a readability requirement, not just a style choice.

## 7. Instagram safe-margin instructions

Keep all text within x: 70px–1010px and y: 90px–1260px on the 1080x1350 canvas (Instagram's standard safe-margin guidance for feed/carousel posts, avoiding UI crop and corner overlap). Specific notes:
- JustPreneur wordmark: top-left, anchored around x: 70–260px, y: 60–110px, identical across all slides.
- Slide headers (2, 3, 4, 6, 7): directly beneath the wordmark, starting no higher than y: 140px.
- Motif zone: roughly y: 160px–560px (top ~30–38% of frame) on slides 2, 3, 4, 6, 7.
- Body-copy block on dense slides (2, 3, 4): starts no higher than y: 600px, ends no lower than y: 1240px, with left/right margins held at x: 70px/1010px throughout — never edge-to-edge.
- Centered punch-slide text (1, 5): vertically centered around y: 550–850, horizontally centered with the same x: 70–1010px side margins.

## 8. Slide-to-slide consistency rules

- Every slide uses the identical grotesk type family and identical navy base — no font or base-color switching across the carousel.
- The gold = PROTECT/lit/lesson-emphasis ↔ steel = DISTRIBUTE/sealed color coding must stay consistent every time it recurs (vault-open gold light in slides 1–3, vault-closing steel/dark in slide 4, PROTECT-lane gold vs. DISTRIBUTE-lane steel in slides 6–7).
- The vault motif's physical state must progress logically across slides 1–4 (ajar → fully open/spilling → beginning to close/sealed) so the carousel reads as one continuous visual story, not four unrelated graphics.
- JustPreneur wordmark position, size, and opacity stay pixel-identical across all 7 slides.
- Slide headers use identical type treatment (size, weight, top-left position) every time they appear (2, 3, 4, 6, 7).
- Slide 5's cleared-board treatment is the only slide that drops the top-left motif zone entirely — this deliberate break signals "this is the thesis, pay attention here."

## 9. Accessibility and contrast check

- Warm-white text (#F5F3EE-equivalent) on deep-navy base (#0B1E32-equivalent) comfortably clears WCAG AA contrast for both large headline text and the smaller body-detail text used on slides 2–4, provided body text stays at or above roughly 32px cap-height equivalent at 1080px width (dense slides must not shrink text below this floor — if a slide's full approved copy cannot fit above this floor within the body-copy zone in §7, flag for QC rather than shrinking further).
- Energy-gold text/elements are reserved for short pull-lines, single-word stamps ("PROTECT"), and single-phrase emphasis ("ARE the moat") only — never full paragraphs — since gold-on-navy body copy at small sizes loses comfortable contrast on compressed mobile screens.
- Steel-gray is used for icon linework and the "DISTRIBUTE" stamp word only, always against the deep-navy base, never as small body text, since steel-on-navy contrast is weaker than warm-white-on-navy at small sizes.
- No text is ever placed directly over the motif's high-detail area (light beams, icon linework) — a soft navy scrim sits beneath any text that overlaps the motif zone's lower edge.
- Minimum single-line text size: headline/lead lines no smaller than ~56px cap-height equivalent; body/detail text on dense slides (2, 3, 4) no smaller than ~32px cap-height equivalent; slide 5's thesis lines are the largest text in the carousel.
