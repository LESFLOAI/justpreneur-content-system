## 1. Overall Creative Concept

Treat this like a business-media explainer, not a restaurant-review post. The visual world is "systems and blueprints," not "food photography." We are illustrating a *strategic framework* (fixed core vs. deliberate flex) that happens to be dressed in Corner Store's own aesthetic language — so the carousel should feel like a sharp trade-press infographic (think Puck, The Information, Morning Brew) crossed with the restaurant's own moody, upscale, Rockwell Group interior sensibility (deep brass, velvet, dark wood, martini-bar lighting).

Visual thesis for the whole set: a **two-column "LOCKED / FLEX" system** that recurs across the deck, reinforced by a consistent navy-and-gold color system and a recurring architectural/blueprint motif (thin gold line-work, corner brackets, grid marks) that visually says "this is infrastructure," not "this is a menu flyer."

Because several facts are time-sensitive and must stay in future tense (opening Nov 9, 2026, not yet open), no image should depict a "grand opening," a crowd, a ribbon-cutting, or any signage claiming the restaurant is operating. Interiors shown as mood/atmosphere renders, not as documentary photos of the real space (we have no licensed photography of the actual build-out).

---

## 2. Composition and Focal Hierarchy (applies to every slide unless noted)

1. **Headline** — top third, largest type, highest contrast, first thing the eye hits.
2. **JustPreneur wordmark/lockup** — small but legible, fixed position every slide (see branding section) — never competing with headline for attention, but always present.
3. **Supporting body copy** — middle band, secondary weight, generous line spacing, left-aligned for readability.
4. **Visual/imagery** — occupies no more than the bottom 45-55% or is used as a restrained full-bleed background with a dedicated dark gradient "text-safe" zone behind the copy (never busy imagery directly behind text).
5. **Slide-system motif** (thin gold corner brackets + a one-word tag like "LOCKED" / "FLEX" / "FRAMEWORK" where relevant) — smallest visual weight, reinforces the series without becoming a label/watermark.

Reading order on every slide: Headline → Body → Image → Branding (branding is a steady anchor, not a stop on the reading path).

---

## 3. JustPreneur Branding — Exact Placement

- Wordmark "JustPreneur" set in clean gold or warm-white sans serif, all one size across all 7 slides (do not let it grow/shrink slide to slide).
- Position: **bottom-left corner**, inside the safe margin, on every slide — same x/y coordinates throughout the deck so the eye learns where to find it.
- Size: small enough to read as a steady brand signature, large enough to be legible at thumbnail size on a phone feed (treat it like a channel bug, not a footnote).
- Never rotated, never placed over high-detail imagery without a scrim behind it — always on a solid or gradient-darkened zone for contrast.
- No tagline, no handle, no slide-count numeral, no date stamp accompanying the wordmark — name only.

## 4. Slide-by-Slide Direction

### Slide 1 — Cover / Hook
**Headline (verbatim):** THE CORNER STORE ISN'T COPYING ITSELF IN VEGAS — IT'S SYSTEMIZING ITSELF
**Kicker (small, optional):** A look inside the real playbook for scaling a hospitality brand.

- Layout: Headline set in two visual beats — "ISN'T COPYING ITSELF IN VEGAS" in warm white, "IT'S SYSTEMIZING ITSELF" in energy gold — stacked, left-aligned, upper two-thirds of frame, inside generous negative space.
- Kicker sits directly beneath the headline in a smaller, steel-gray weight — quiet, not competing.
- Background: a dark, moody architectural/interior-mood render (brass fixtures, velvet banquette, warm pin-spot lighting) kept intentionally soft/out-of-focus in the lower third so the headline block above it stays on a clean dark navy gradient.
- JustPreneur wordmark bottom-left per branding spec.
- No plates of food as the hero image here — this is a systems story, lead with mood/atmosphere, not a menu shot.

### Slide 2 — Lesson Framing
**Headline:** Most expansions fail one of two ways.
**Body:** Improvise everything, and you lose what made the brand work in the first place. Copy-paste everything, and you ignore the market you're entering.

- Layout: Editorial, text-forward slide — minimal imagery. Headline top, body directly below in two short lines with a visible gap between the two "failure modes" so each sentence reads as its own beat.
- Optional restrained graphic: two thin diverging gold lines/arrows (one labeled implicitly by position, not by added text) suggesting "two paths" — purely abstract line art, no icons, no clip-art.
- Background: solid deep navy or a very subtle dark gradient — no photographic imagery competing with this text-heavy slide.
- JustPreneur wordmark bottom-left, consistent position.

### Slide 3 — The Constant Core
**Headline:** What doesn't change, by design.
**Body:** The Lobster & Caviar Rolls. The Five Cheese Pizza Rolls. The Wagyu French Dip. The Rockwell Group design language. The service identity that made the SoHo original one of the toughest reservations in Manhattan.

- Layout: This is the first "LOCKED" slide in the two-column system. Headline top. Below it, the body copy presented as a short vertical list (one item per line) inside a bracketed gold-outlined panel — visually reads as "the fixed list," reinforcing permanence.
- Small recurring tag in the corner of the panel: the word **LOCKED** in gold, tracked-out small caps — this is the series motif, not a new label type (keep it to one word, consistent typeface, same placement as Slide 4's "FLEX" tag).
- Supporting image: a single, elegant, softly-lit interior/atmosphere render (dark wood, brass, velvet) placed behind or beside the panel at low contrast — evokes the SoHo original without claiming to document it. No readable menu boards, no signage with invented text.
- JustPreneur wordmark bottom-left.

### Slide 4 — The Vegas-Only Flex
**Headline:** What's built to flex, on purpose.
**Body:** A Vegas-exclusive Butcher's Counter steak program — Rosewood Ranch Wagyu Tomahawk, Wagyu Porterhouse. A Vegas-only Dirty Martini, served alongside the Tomato and Sour Cream & Onion Martinis carried over from SoHo.

- Layout: Mirrors Slide 3's panel system exactly (same panel size/position/typography) but tagged **FLEX** in gold instead of LOCKED — the repetition is the point; the audience should feel the "same system, new content" rhyme.
- Visual accent: a warm, slightly more vivid gold-lit tone than Slide 3 (still same palette family) to signal "this is the adapted, energized part of the system" without breaking brand consistency.
- Supporting image: a refined steak/chophouse-mood render (dark marble counter, warm spotlight, a single plated tomahawk silhouette) and/or a martini-glass silhouette — treated as elegant mood photography, not literal product photography (we have no licensed photo of the actual Vegas dish).
- JustPreneur wordmark bottom-left.

### Slide 5 — Design Continuity
**Headline:** Even the architecture repeats the system.
**Body:** Rockwell Group designed the original SoHo space — and designed the Las Vegas location too. Inside The Cosmopolitan's third floor: a Martini Room, a train-car-inspired dining room.

- Layout: Full-bleed architectural interior mood render as the dominant visual (this is the one slide where imagery leads), with headline and body set inside a bottom-anchored dark gradient scrim for guaranteed legibility.
- Visual motif: a faint blueprint/line-grid overlay across the image (thin gold architectural lines, like a floor-plan sketch) reinforcing "this is a designed system," tying back to the LOCKED/FLEX panels visually without repeating the words.
- Do not render any real Rockwell Group trademarked floorplan, any MGM/Cosmopolitan logo, or any identifiable real-world branding — this must read as an evocative, generic upscale train-car-inspired dining room / martini lounge mood, not a claimed likeness of the actual built space.
- JustPreneur wordmark bottom-left, placed on the scrim for contrast.

### Slide 6 — The Takeaway
**Headline:** This is the real lesson.
**Body:** Scalable brands don't run on improvisation or rigid copy-paste. They run on a fixed, non-negotiable core — plus deliberate, limited localization.

- Layout: Text-forward, highest-contrast slide in the deck — this is the thesis statement, give it the most breathing room. Headline large, centered or left-aligned block, body directly beneath in a slightly smaller warm-white weight.
- Minimal/no photographic imagery — solid deep navy background with a single restrained gold architectural corner-bracket motif (same family as Slide 2/5) to keep the series visually tied together without clutter.
- JustPreneur wordmark bottom-left.

### Slide 7 — CTA / Steal This Framework
**Headline:** Steal this framework.
**Body:** Before you expand: define what never changes. Then decide — on purpose — what's allowed to flex. The Corner Store's Las Vegas location is set to open November 9, 2026, inside The Cosmopolitan.

- Layout: Headline top in energy gold (this is the one slide where gold leads instead of white, signaling "action/CTA"). Body beneath in warm white, with "set to open November 9, 2026" readable but not shouted — future-tense framing must be visually unambiguous (do not use any "NOW OPEN" or "GRAND OPENING" visual treatment).
- Visual accent: the same LOCKED/FLEX gold corner-bracket motif, now closed into a single bracket/frame around the whole text block — visually "closing the system" as the deck ends.
- Background: a soft, dark moody interior render (quiet, same brass/velvet family as Slide 1) at low opacity behind a navy scrim — closing the loop visually with the cover slide.
- JustPreneur wordmark bottom-left.

## 5. Typography Direction

- **Headline font:** a bold, modern geometric or grotesque sans serif (e.g., in the family of Neue Haas Grotesk Bold / Söhne Breit / Inter Black) — confident, business-editorial, not a script or display/decorative font.
- **Body font:** a clean humanist sans serif at a lighter weight (e.g., Inter Regular/Medium or Söhne Buch) for contrast against the bold headline.
- **Tag words (LOCKED / FLEX):** same headline family, small size, wide letter-tracking, all caps — treated as a design motif, not a caption/label.
- Headline sizes must stay large enough to read at Instagram feed thumbnail size — no headline should ever drop below roughly 60-70pt equivalent at 1080px width.
- Never use more than two typefaces total across the deck (one for headlines/tags, one for body/kicker).
- Left-align body copy; avoid fully justified or heavily centered long paragraphs — ragged-right reads faster on mobile.

---

## 6. Color Palette

- **Primary:** deep navy (#0E1A2B approx.) — dominant background across all slides.
- **Accent/energy:** energy gold (#C9A24A approx.) — headlines' second color beat, tag words, corner-bracket motif, CTA headline.
- **Neutral text:** warm white (#F5F1E8 approx.) — primary body copy and most headline text for contrast against navy.
- **Secondary neutral:** steel gray (#8A93A1 approx.) — kickers, de-emphasized supporting lines.
- Keep the gold accent restrained — it should feel like trim/accent lighting (brass fixtures, pin-spots), not a wash across entire slides. No other colors introduced (no reds/greens) to keep the system tight and premium.

---

## 7. Image Treatment

- All supporting imagery treated as **moody, atmospheric, slightly underexposed interior/product renders** — warm pin-spot or practical lighting, shallow depth of field, premium hospitality-editorial tone (think a high-end restaurant-design magazine feature, not a menu flyer or food-delivery ad).
- Desaturate slightly and push toward navy/brass tonal range in post so every image sits naturally inside the fixed palette regardless of what the generator returns.
- Every image needs a deliberate dark, low-detail zone (gradient or genuinely empty negative space) sized to the headline/body block that will sit on top of it — this is specified explicitly in every prompt below.
- No readable signage, no invented logos, no real trademarks (Rockwell Group, MGM, Cosmopolitan, Catch) rendered as logos/wordmarks inside any image — represent the *mood* of the spaces only.
- No people's faces as hero subjects (avoids malformed-hands/duplicate-face risk and avoids implying a specific celebrity or diner appeared in a scene we can't document) — human presence, if any, should be anonymous, out-of-focus, non-identifiable silhouettes at most, and is optional on every slide.

---

## 8. Instagram Safe-Margin Instructions

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350px per slide.
- Keep all text and the JustPreneur wordmark inside a safe margin of **at least 80px from every edge** (top, bottom, left, right) — Instagram's UI (profile bar, caption, like/comment icons on single-image view; swipe-dot indicator on carousels) crops/overlaps near edges.
- Keep critical headline text additionally clear of the **top 120px** and **bottom 150px** where Instagram's UI chrome is most likely to overlap on various devices/aspect ratios.
- Do not place any essential word within 60px of the left/right edges to protect against minor crop variance across devices.

---

## 9. Slide-to-Slide Consistency Rules

1. Same canvas size (1080x1350) and same navy/gold/white/steel palette, every slide, no exceptions.
2. Same JustPreneur wordmark — size, color, position (bottom-left) — identical across all 7 slides.
3. Same headline typeface/weight and same body typeface/weight across all 7 slides; only color emphasis (white vs. gold) shifts intentionally (gold leads on Slide 1 secondary line and Slide 7 CTA; white leads everywhere else).
4. The LOCKED (Slide 3) / FLEX (Slide 4) panel treatment must be visually identical in size, position, and style — this pairing is the visual core of the entire concept and must read as a matched set.
5. The gold corner-bracket/line motif recurs on Slides 2, 5, 6, and 7 at consistent weight/style to visually bookend the "systems" idea without introducing new motifs mid-deck.
6. No slide introduces a new accent color, a new font, or a new branding position — the only intentional slide-to-slide "surprise" is content, never system.

---

## 10. Accessibility and Contrast Check

- Warm white (#F5F1E8) body/headline text on deep navy (#0E1A2B) background exceeds WCAG AA contrast for large text comfortably (very high contrast ratio, dark-navy base is close to black).
- Energy gold (#C9A24A) on deep navy also reads with strong contrast for headline-size text; avoid gold-on-white or gold text at body-copy size, where contrast drops — gold is reserved for large headline/tag use only, never for small body copy.
- Steel gray kicker/supporting text is for de-emphasized short lines only (kickers, not core body copy) — never use steel gray for the primary message a reader must retain.
- Every slide must pass a "squint test": headline legible as a shape/contrast block even blurred, before any fine detail is visible — this is enforced by keeping headlines on a flat navy or gradient-scrim zone, never directly on high-detail imagery.
- No information in this carousel is conveyed by color alone (LOCKED/FLEX are also distinguished by word and by slide content, not color alone) — safe for color-vision-deficient readers.
