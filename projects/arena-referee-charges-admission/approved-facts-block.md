# Approved Facts Block — Arena (formerly LMArena)

**Fact Verifier status: Cleared with qualifications**
**Date checked: 2026-10-20**

Arena (formerly LMArena, and originally the crowdsourced "Chatbot Arena" leaderboard) began in 2023 as a UC Berkeley-led research project within LMSYS, a multi-university AI benchmarking collaboration. It was spun out as a company, raising a reported ~$100 million seed round (~$600 million valuation) in May 2025, then a $150 million Series A at a roughly $1.7 billion valuation announced January 6, 2026. According to multiple reports, Arena announced a $200 million Series B on or around October 8, 2026, co-led by Lightspeed Venture Partners and Khosla Ventures, at a valuation of roughly $3.1 billion — an increase of about 82% (commonly described in coverage as "nearly doubling") from the January 2026 Series A valuation roughly nine months earlier. Additional participants reportedly named across coverage include Salesforce Ventures, 01 Advisors, Dell Technologies Capital, Endeavor Catalyst, a16z, and Felicis, though the complete investor list varies slightly by outlet and was not confirmed against a single primary source. In September 2025, Arena launched a paid commercial product, "AI Evaluations," selling model-performance benchmarking services to AI labs and enterprises; the company has said (self-reported, unaudited) that this business reached $100 million in annualized revenue run-rate by June 2026, up from a reported $30 million in January 2026. Separately, an academic preprint titled "The Leaderboard Illusion" (arXiv 2504.20879), led by Shivalika Singh and Sara Hooker of Cohere Labs with co-authors from Cohere, Stanford, Princeton, Waterloo, MIT, the Allen Institute for AI, and the University of Washington, alleges that Chatbot Arena/LMArena gave a cohort of large, proprietary AI labs structural advantages through undisclosed private testing, preferential sampling, and asymmetric model deprecation disclosure. The paper is a preprint (peer-review status not confirmed as of this writing), and LMArena has publicly disputed aspects of its findings. Any characterization of Arena having a "conflict of interest" because it is simultaneously a free public judge and a paid vendor to the same industry is this story's own editorial analysis/inference — no source reviewed makes that claim directly — and must be labeled explicitly as framing/analysis, not as a fact or a claim attributed to any named source.

## Verification limitation

Primary-source direct fetch (Arena's own release, TechCrunch, Bloomberg, Reuters) was unavailable this session due to a tool/network (DNS) outage affecting all WebFetch attempts, confirmed via a failed google.com sanity check. Core Series B figures are corroborated by ~10 independent, mutually consistent secondary/aggregator sources with no contradicting figures found anywhere in search. A follow-up primary-source confirmation pass is recommended before claims are published as unhedged fact.
