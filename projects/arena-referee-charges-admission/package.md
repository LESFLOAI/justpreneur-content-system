## Facts summary

Arena (formerly LMArena, originally the crowdsourced "Chatbot Arena" leaderboard) began in 2023 as a UC Berkeley-led research project within LMSYS, a multi-university AI benchmarking collaboration. It spun out as a company, raising a reported ~$100M seed round (~$600M valuation) in May 2025, then a $150M Series A at a roughly $1.7B valuation announced January 6, 2026. According to multiple reports, Arena announced a $200M Series B on or around October 8, 2026, co-led by Lightspeed Venture Partners and Khosla Ventures, at a valuation of roughly $3.1B — about 82% above the January 2026 Series A (commonly described in coverage as "nearly doubling"). In September 2025, Arena launched a paid product, "AI Evaluations," selling benchmarking services to AI labs/enterprises; the company has said (self-reported, unaudited) this business reached $100M in annualized revenue run-rate by June 2026, up from a reported $30M in January 2026. Separately, an academic preprint, "The Leaderboard Illusion" (arXiv 2504.20879, led by Cohere Labs' Shivalika Singh and Sara Hooker with co-authors from six other institutions including Stanford and MIT), alleges select proprietary labs received undisclosed private testing and preferential treatment on the leaderboard; peer-review status is unconfirmed, and Arena has publicly disputed aspects of the findings. The "conflict of interest" framing used in this post's angle is explicitly this content's own editorial analysis, not a claim made by any source reviewed.

## Final caption

Arena started as a free referee. Now it's a $3.1B business.

What began in 2023 as a UC Berkeley–led research project (Chatbot Arena, inside the multi-university LMSYS collaboration) spun out into a company in May 2025 — a reported ~$100M seed at a reported ~$600M valuation. By September 2025, it had launched a paid product, "AI Evaluations," selling deeper benchmarking to the same AI labs it ranks for free. A $150M Series A followed in January 2026 at a ~$1.7B valuation, with a self-reported, unaudited revenue run-rate of ~$30M. Then, on or around October 8, 2026, a $200M Series B — co-led by Lightspeed Venture Partners and Khosla Ventures — reportedly pushed the valuation to roughly $3.1B, about 82% above January, with a self-reported, unaudited run-rate near $100M.

In between: a preprint, "The Leaderboard Illusion," from Cohere Labs researchers and co-authors at six other institutions including Stanford and MIT, alleging select labs got undisclosed advantages on the leaderboard. It's unreviewed, and Arena disputes parts of it.

Here's our take — labeled plainly as our take, not a sourced fact: being the free judge and the paid vendor to the same industry at the same time is a structural tension worth watching.

The lesson isn't "don't monetize trust." It's: if you're the trusted judge in your category, that trust is an asset — but selling access to it demands guardrails you build before someone else demands them for you.

Save this one. Then look at your own business: where are you the recognized arbiter, and how would you productize that responsibly?
