## 1. Facts Summary (Approved Facts Block, verbatim from Fact Verifier)

> NY State pied-à-terre surcharge on non-primary-residence luxury homes, signed by Gov. Hochul May 28, 2026, effective July 1, 2026–June 30, 2031 (Phase One). Thresholds: Class One (1-3 family homes) ≥$5M assessed value; Class Two (condo/co-op) ≥$1M assessed value.
>
> Rates (Phase One): Class One (houses) 0.8%-1.3% of assessed value (tiered). Class Two (condo/co-op) 4%-6.5% of assessed value. *(Corrected from Story Scout's original draft, which had misstated the house rate as 0.8%-4%.)*
>
> Revenue/scope estimates — attributed separately, not merged: Gov. Hochul's office originally estimated ~13,000 properties/~$500M per year, later revised down to ~10,000 properties. NYC Comptroller's independent estimate: ~11,200 properties, ~$500M gross, net likely $340M-$380M after adjustments.
>
> Exemption filing deadline extended three times: Aug 21 → Sept 18 → Oct 13, 2026 (CityRealty called Oct 13 the "third and final" extension). ~9,300 exemption applications filed; ~5,200 (~55%) approved as primary-residence exemptions.
>
> Sept 29, 2026: Justice Wayne M. Ozzi (NY Supreme Court, Richmond County) ruled the city's rollout/notices (to ~17,000 owners) unlawful; ordered them redone and the ~900,000-property public roll taken down. He left the underlying STATE tax intact — only the city's process was invalidated. City appealed; Second Department stayed his ruling ~Oct 6, 2026; enforcement continuing. Second Department said it would rule by Nov. 10, 2026 — still pending as of today, Oct 26, 2026.
>
> Separate suit "Ross v. State of New York" (Suffolk County, Index No. 628629/2026, filed Sept 28, 2026, plaintiffs Wilbur Ross and Steve Wynn) challenges the constitutionality of the underlying state law itself — pending, no ruling.
>
> Per CityRealty's Oct 6-12, 2026 report (attributed specifically to CityRealty, single-sourced): more than 250 **NYC** listings cut asking prices that week, tied to the looming tax/exemption-deadline pressure. *(Corrected from "Manhattan" in the initial copywriter draft — see QC correction below.)*
>
> The city intends to put the surcharge on tax bills due Jan. 1, 2027, regardless of how the pending appeals resolve.

**Mandatory framing rules honored throughout this package:**
1. Never states the tax was "struck down," "blocked," or "upheld" — only the city's rollout/notice process was ruled unlawful, and that ruling is itself stayed pending appeal. Even negated phrasing ("not struck down... not upheld...") was removed from the voiceover per QC, due to Instagram auto-caption chunking risk.
2. Framing stays on business/market behavior — no commentary on the mayor, the judge, or the policy's political merits. No judge, plaintiff, or official is named anywhere in the copy.
3. The CityRealty 250+ figure is attributed to CityRealty by name, on-screen (not just in caption), and scoped to NYC (not narrowed to "Manhattan" or a specific property-type breakdown that isn't in the source).
4. The seller-motive causal claim ("pricing in the uncertainty") is hedged as inference ("it looks like... / appear to be..."), not stated as settled fact.
5. Two separate, distinct lawsuits are not conflated in the underlying facts (Ozzi/Richmond County process challenge vs. Ross/Wynn/Suffolk County constitutionality challenge) — this package's chosen angle doesn't require surfacing the Ross/Wynn suit by name, so it isn't referenced in the copy, avoiding any risk of misattribution.
