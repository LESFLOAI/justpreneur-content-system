## 0. Hard constraints carried into every visual decision

These override the general brand defaults wherever they conflict. All are non-negotiable — flag them clearly to whoever runs generation locally.

1. **No real-person likenesses.** No named individual from the underlying story (the ruling judge, the plaintiffs referenced in the source material, any city official) is rendered as a recognizable face anywhere in this piece. Any human presence in generated art is hands-only, back-of-frame, rear-view, or fully silhouetted — never a posed portrait.
2. **No identifiable real buildings.** The story concerns Manhattan real estate broadly, not any single named tower or address. No prompt asks for a recognizable landmark (no specific supertall silhouette readable as "that one building"). Skyline and condo-exterior art is generic/composite — evocative of "Manhattan luxury residential," not a photographic claim about one real structure.
3. **No fabricated CityRealty logo, and no invented substitute logo either.** The on-screen text "— Source: CityRealty" in Scene 4 is approved copy added as a type layer only (see §11). No generated image attempts to render CityRealty's actual logo/wordmark, and no prompt invents a fake real-estate-data-firm logo to stand in for it. Any "attribution card" look in the background art is a blank, unbranded card shape only.
4. **No fabricated news chyrons, network mastheads, "BREAKING" banners, legal-document close-ups with readable fine print, or courtroom-drama clichés (judge's bench with an identifiable seal, gavel mid-strike with dramatic lens flare).** This is a JustPreneur editorial Reel, not a simulated newscast or a legal-thriller trailer. Courthouse/legal imagery stays generic, architectural, and restrained — a symbol of "the law," not a specific case.
5. **No legible numbers, prices, or addresses baked into any "listing" or "price cut" visual.** Scene 4's stylized price-cut graphics show abstract card shapes, down-arrow motifs, and strikethrough bars only — any digits, dollar signs, or street names in the generated image read as fabricated real listings, which this brand does not do. All real figures ("250+", "Oct 6–12") are approved copy added afterward as type (see §11).
6. **No countdown numerals baked into any generated image.** The "15," the decreasing beat-to-beat numbers, and the "0" are a motion-graphics/type layer applied in edit (see §3, §11) — never part of the AI-generated background. AI image generators cannot reliably render crisp, accurate numerals across a sequence, and this motif is too central to the piece to risk garbled digits.
7. **Tone stays analytical and restrained, not alarmist or celebratory.** No "doom tax" fear framing, no gavel-slam dread, no "loophole hack" clickbait energy, no triumphant market-boom imagery either — the story is about pricing uncertainty intelligently, which is a calm, confident, editorial posture in both script and visuals.

## 1. Overall creative concept

This is a **business-editorial countdown piece** — the visual language of a Bloomberg or Business Insider markets explainer with a live-clock HUD, not a legal-thriller teaser and not a real-estate-influencer reel. The spine of the whole piece is a **persistent countdown HUD** — a small, clean digital-ticker-style readout (gold numerals on a navy chip, think stock-ticker/terminal styling, not a cheap digital-watch font) that appears in a fixed position and counts down across the Reel's run, landing on "0" exactly at Scene 6 to dramatize the Nov 10 ruling arriving — a storytelling device that compresses the real 15-day wait into the Reel's 45–60 seconds, not a literal real-time clock.

Two visual "worlds" alternate with the story's two forces, exactly mirroring the script's own structure (law vs. market):

- **The Legal World** — cool steel-navy, formal, architectural: courthouse facades/columns, generic bench-and-gavel still-life abstraction, procedural and unhurried. Carries the signed-law/ruling/stay beats (Scenes 2–3).
- **The Market World** — warmer, amber-gold, glass-and-skyline energy: condo towers, stylized listing cards, price-cut motifs. Carries the CityRealty data beat (Scene 4) and the closing deadline beat (Scene 6), where the two worlds visually converge — both are hitting their deadlines regardless of each other.

The piece opens and closes on pure-brand minimal cards (Scene 1 hook, Scene 5 lesson, Scene 7 brand sting) that strip away the photographic world entirely and let the countdown HUD and typography carry the moment — these are the "if someone only watches 3 seconds" frames and must be the most legible in the set.

## 2. Composition and focal hierarchy

- **Scene 1 (hook, 0:00–0:03):** The countdown HUD is not a small corner element here — it is the dominant visual. A large, clean "15 DAYS." numeral treatment sits centered on a dark, atmospheric, abstract HUD-texture background. Nothing else competes.
- **Scenes 2–4 (0:03–0:30):** Full-bleed photographic/editorial background per beat. On-screen text sits in the lower-third safe zone. The countdown HUD shrinks to a small, fixed chip in the top-safe-zone corner (consistent position every time it appears) and ticks down one notch per beat.
- **Scene 5 (lesson, 0:30–0:42):** Breaks from the photographic world — a clean navy-gold text-on-color card (no literal scene), countdown HUD still present small in its corner, exactly as the approved copy specifies.
- **Scene 6 (deadline, 0:42–0:52):** Two stacked dates (Nov 10 / Jan 1, 2027) are the focal content, set against a background where the Legal World and Market World visually blend — the countdown HUD ticks to "0" here and this is the moment it disappears for good.
- **Scene 7 (CTA + brand hit, 0:52–0:60):** Countdown HUD is gone. Clean navy-gold brand card, JustPreneur wordmark centered, calm resolution — no competing texture, silent brand card per the approved copy.

## 4. Typography direction

- **Headline/on-screen-text typeface:** bold, semicondensed grotesk — same family used across prior JustPreneur Reels/carousels, kept consistent. Sentence case for all on-screen text lines (matches the approved copy's own mixed-case styling; the few all-caps words like "CITY'S ROLLOUT" and "YOUR" are emphasis within the sentence, not a full-caps treatment).
- **Countdown HUD numerals:** a distinct, tabular/monospaced digital-ticker style — think a premium stock-ticker or terminal readout, not a cheap digital-watch or neon-countdown-timer cliché. Gold numerals on a small navy chip with a thin gold hairline border. This typeface is used nowhere else in the piece, so the HUD reads as its own consistent "clock" element at a glance.
- **Scene 1 "15 DAYS.":** the single largest text treatment in the piece — this is the hook and must read instantly even muted/mid-scroll.
- **Scene 3 body copy:** this beat's three-clause sentence is the longest in the script; render it at a slightly smaller but still comfortably legible size and allow it to break across three short lines rather than shrinking the whole piece's type scale to fit it.
- **Scene 5 pull-line:** second-largest treatment after Scene 1 — this is the Reel's "save this" quote and should be typographically confident, ideally with "Usually the expensive one." in gold against "Waiting for certainty IS a strategy." in warm white.
- **Scene 6 dates:** "Nov 10" and "Jan 1, 2027" set larger than the surrounding label words ("Appeals ruling expected" / "Tax bills go out — regardless"), since the dates are the beat's actual payload.
- No script fonts, no courtroom-document serif pastiche, no neon-digital-clock novelty font — stays in JustPreneur's own premium-business-editorial voice throughout.

## 7. Instagram safe-margin instructions

**Reel (1080 x 1920):** Keep all text and the countdown HUD within x: 80px–1000px and y: 250px–1550px. The top ~230px is covered by the account name/follow button/audio bar in the app UI; the bottom ~370px is covered by caption text, like/comment/share/save icons, and the audio disc. The countdown HUD's fixed corner position (Scenes 2–6) should sit inside this safe zone at a consistent corner (recommend top-right, y≈280–360) so it never collides with the account-name UI above it or the lower-third text below it. Punch-beat centered text (Scenes 1 and 5) should sit roughly at y: 850–1100 (true vertical center of the safe zone).

## 8. Slide-to-slide (scene-to-scene) consistency rules

- The countdown HUD's font, chip shape, color, and corner position are pixel-identical every time it appears (Scenes 1–6) — only the numeral value and, in Scene 1, its scale/position change.
- The Legal World (cool steel-navy) and Market World (warm amber-gold) palettes must read identically every time each reappears, so a viewer recognizes "we're in the courtroom thread" vs. "we're in the market thread" without rereading the text.
- The grotesk type family and scrim treatment are identical across all 7 scenes — no font or scrim-style switching mid-piece.
- JustPreneur wordmark treatment (size, position, opacity) is identical wherever it appears, scaling up only for the final Scene 7 brand card.

## 9. Accessibility and contrast check

- Warm-white text (#F5F3EE-equivalent) on a navy scrim (#0B1E32-equivalent) at ≥70% scrim opacity clears WCAG AA for large text at Reel scale.
- Gold text/HUD numerals (energy-gold, e.g., #D4A017-equivalent) are reserved for short, large-size elements (the countdown readout, emphasis words, the Scene 6 dates) — never for full multi-line body copy, since gold-on-navy body text at small sizes drops below comfortable contrast on compressed mobile screens. This matters most in Scene 3, which has the longest line count.
- Body copy is never placed directly over high-frequency photographic texture (glass reflections, architectural detail, listing-card clutter) without a navy scrim between text and image — scrim is mandatory on every photographic scene (2, 3, 4, 6), not optional.
- Minimum on-screen text size renders no smaller than is comfortably legible at arm's length on a phone; Scene 3 (longest copy, three clauses) should be checked first during local assembly since it has the least margin for error.

## 11. Layout prompt — where exact text and the countdown HUD go after image generation

Use this as the standing instruction for whoever assembles the final Reel in an editing tool once images exist.

1. Place each `bg-reel-*` image as the full-bleed background for its corresponding scene, per the timing table in §3, using a slow Ken-Burns push-in (105%→112% scale) over each scene's duration if using a static image rather than motion footage.
2. Add a soft navy gradient scrim (bottom-anchored for lower-third scenes 2–4, full-frame vignette for centered punch scenes 1, 5, 6) at 60–75% opacity before placing type.
3. Build the countdown HUD as a vector/motion-graphics element (never baked into the AI image) — gold tabular numerals on a navy chip with a thin gold hairline border. Animate it as a single continuous element: large and centered for Scene 1 ("15"), then shrinking and relocating to the fixed top-right corner position for Scenes 2–5 (ticking down per the suggested values in §3, or the exact day-count confirmed at publish time), then animating to "0" and holding through Scene 6, then fading out before Scene 7.
4. Set the exact on-screen text for each scene (word-for-word from the "On-screen text" column in §3 — do not paraphrase or shorten) in the grotesk type family, warm-white (gold for the specified emphasis elements per §4), positioned per the "Text placement" column, within the safe margins in §7.
5. On Scene 6, set "Nov 10" and "Jan 1, 2027" as the visually larger elements of their respective lines, stacked per the approved copy.
6. On Scene 7, center the JustPreneur wordmark/logo lockup below the final CTA line, silent (no voiceover under this card per the approved copy), holding for the full 8-second duration of the scene.
7. Cut cleanly between scenes on the timestamp boundaries in §3 — no crossfades longer than ~4 frames between the photographic scenes; a slightly longer (6–8 frame) soft dissolve is acceptable into and out of the three minimal navy-gold card scenes (1, 5, 7) since they're a deliberate tonal break.
