## 0. Hard constraints carried into every visual decision

These override the general brand defaults wherever they conflict:

1. **No Subway trademark or logo reproduction.** No prompt renders Subway's actual wordmark, logo, signage, storefront, sandwich packaging, or the brand's yellow/green color combination as an identifiable trademark. The visual world stays inside a generic advertising-agency / corporate-archive setting (files, boardroom, drawers, abstract data), not a restaurant or food environment. This also sidesteps any accidental "sandwich shop" cliché that would cheapen the story's actual subject — a marketing-strategy decision, not a food story.
2. **No real-person likenesses.** Will Arnett (on-screen talent), Jeff Klein (U.S. CMO), and Greg Lyons (former Global CMO) are named public figures. No prompt asks an image generator to render a recognizable face or identity for any of them. Any human presence in generated art is fully backlit silhouette, hands-only, or absent entirely — never a posed portrait that could be read as an attempt at a specific person's likeness.
3. **No baked-in exact text or numbers.** None of the hedged, attributed facts in the copy (agency-review outcome, closure counts, royalty-revenue trend, net-income figures, acquisition price) are rendered as legible text, chart-axis labels, or numerals inside any generated image. All exact wording and numbers — including the "reportedly" / "trade press reports" hedging language required by the Fact Verifier — are added afterward as a type layer, so nothing gets locked into an image in a way that could drop the attribution. Generated charts/graphics are abstract shapes only (a bar stepping down, a line curving up) with no readable labels.
4. **No panic or crisis imagery.** The copy's own reframe (Slide 7) is explicit: this is a "positioning move," not a "panic move." Visuals must never lean into closure/decline imagery that reads as distress — no "closed" signage, no boarded storefronts, no red down-arrows, no alarm/warning iconography. The financial-backdrop slide (6) stays measured and journalistic, not dramatic.
5. **One consistent visual metaphor carries the whole carousel.** The copy's own hook — "they reopened a drawer" — becomes the literal, recurring visual system: a steel archive drawer that is closed on Slide 1, cracks open across Slides 2–3, splits into a before/after contrast on Slide 4, and fully floods the frame with warm gold light by Slide 8's CTA. This gives the story a distinctive, ownable visual identity instead of a generic "motivational quote card" look.

## 1. Overall creative concept

**"The Drawer."** A dormant idea, literally stored away, gets pulled back into the light — under time pressure, backed by new leadership, against a business backdrop press is actively watching. The visual language is modern business-and-culture editorial: think Ad Age's own visual desk, or a Fast Company feature on brand strategy — moody, cinematic, low-key lighting, a single dramatic light source (the drawer's gold glow) doing the emotional work, not clip-art icons or stock-photo cheerfulness.

Every slide lives inside the same quiet, dark navy/steel "archive room" world. The one variable that changes slide to slide is how much gold light is escaping the drawer and how it's being used — sealed shut, cracking open, flooding a folder, splitting into before/after, threading into an org chart, tempering into a steady flame, finally flooding open toward the viewer as an invitation. This progression mirrors the copy's own arc (urgency → revival → proof → backing → context → reframe → invitation) without needing any literal food, retail, or portrait imagery, which keeps the piece safely inside trademark- and likeness-clean territory while still feeling premium and specific to this story rather than generic.

## 2. Composition and focal hierarchy

- Same top-45%-clean / bottom-two-thirds-visual convention used in prior JustPreneur carousels: a generous negative-space band across the top of each interior slide for kicker + body copy, the generated visual occupying the lower ~55–65% of the frame.
- Cover (Slide 1) carries the full two-line headline in the top ~50%, drawer visual below.
- Slide 4 (Before/After) uses a vertical split visual with dual kicker/body columns (BEFORE / AFTER) rather than a single copy block.
- Slide 8 (CTA) reserves the top ~45–50% for the takeaway line and question, with the JustPreneur wordmark centered in the bottom safe margin, sitting on the warmest, most open version of the drawer visual in the whole set.

## 4. Typography direction

- **Headline/cover typeface:** bold, condensed-to-semicondensed grotesk (same family used across prior JustPreneur carousels — keep consistent). Sentence case for the long cover headline, not all-caps.
- **Kicker labels** (THE CLOCK, THE MOVE, BEFORE 2018 / AFTER 2026, THE BACKING, THE BACKDROP, THE REFRAME, THE TAKEAWAY): all-caps, energy-gold, small size, letter-spaced — identical style across every interior slide.
- **Body copy:** warm white, regular weight, generous line-height. Slides 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8 run multiple short lines/paragraphs per the copy above — keep each paragraph visually separated (line-break or small vertical gap) rather than compressing into a dense block, since these slides carry more text than a typical single-line JustPreneur slide.
- **Slide 6 numerals** (631, 729, ~10%, ~$9.6B, ~$688M, ~$397M, 2024, 2025) render in warm white or gold within the body copy type layer — never inside the generated background image (see §0.3).
- No script fonts, no decorative "fast food menu board" lettering — this is a business-editorial piece about a marketing decision, not a food-brand pastiche.

## 5. Color palette

**Brand base (unchanged):** deep navy, steel gray, warm white, energy gold — used for all text, wordmark, kickers, and UI chrome.

**Story-specific treatment (background art only):**
- Deep navy and steel gray dominate every slide's "archive room" environment — consistent low-key, editorial mood throughout.
- Energy gold is reserved specifically for the drawer's light/glow and the org-chart connecting lines on Slide 5 — it should read as "the revived idea" every time it appears, not as generic brand decoration.
- No Subway yellow/green color combination anywhere, in any slide, in any proportion (see §0.1).
- No red, no warning/alert colors on the financial-backdrop slide (6) — keep it in the same navy/steel/gold family as every other slide so it doesn't visually flag "bad news" (see §0.4).

## 6. Image treatment

- Photographic-leaning, cinematic, low-key lighting throughout — a single dramatic light source (the drawer glow) against dark navy/steel shadow, consistent with modern business-editorial photography (the kind of frame you'd see leading an Ad Age or Fast Company feature), not flat vector icon graphics.
- Slide 6 is the one exception: a clean abstract data-shape graphic (bars + a curve), still rendered in the same navy/steel/gold palette and lighting mood as the photographic slides, so it doesn't feel like a jarring style break.
- No text baked into any generated image anywhere — all copy, kickers, and numerals are added as a type layer after generation.
- No fake Subway logos, no fabricated agency logos, no fabricated NFL/advertising-award iconography, no watermarks, no small corner icons or production slates.
- Human presence, where used at all (Slide 5 only), is fully backlit silhouette with no visible facial features — never a posed portrait that could be read as an attempt at Jeff Klein's or Greg Lyons's likeness.

## 8. Slide-to-slide consistency rules

- Every slide lives in the same dark navy/steel "archive room" environment — no slide should look like it belongs to a different visual world.
- The gold-light "drawer" motif must visibly progress in the sequence specified in §3's "Visual motif stage" column — sealed (1) → cracking (2) → open/rising folder (3) → split before/after (4) → threading into org-chart lines (5) → abstract data shapes in the same palette (6) → steady centered calm glow (7) → fully flooding toward viewer (8). A viewer flipping through quickly should feel the light gradually opening up across the carousel.
- Kicker label style (all-caps, gold, small, letter-spaced) is identical across all 7 non-cover slides.
- JustPreneur wordmark treatment (size, position, opacity) stays identical across all 8 slides — small and centered at the bottom margin — with no size change until the final CTA slide, where it may sit fractionally larger to close the piece, matching the convention used in prior JustPreneur carousels.

## 9. Accessibility and contrast check

- Warm-white text (#F5F3EE-equivalent) on a deep-navy background/scrim (#0B1E32-equivalent) at ≥70% scrim opacity where the visual has any texture comfortably clears WCAG AA contrast for large text.
- Gold kicker text (energy-gold, e.g., #D4A017-equivalent) is used only for short, large-size labels (kickers) — never for the longer body-copy paragraphs on Slides 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, since those slides carry more text than usual and gold-on-navy body copy at smaller sizes risks dropping below comfortable contrast on compressed mobile screens. Body copy stays warm white throughout.
- No text is ever placed directly over high-frequency background detail (folder texture, org-chart node clutter, chart-shape edges) without a navy scrim behind it — scrim is mandatory on every slide with body copy, not optional.
- Because Slides 2, 3, 5, 6, and 7 carry noticeably more text than a typical single-line JustPreneur slide, the top negative-space band should be treated as a hard minimum of 45% of the frame height, not a target to shrink — legibility takes priority over maximizing the visual's footprint on these text-heavy slides.
