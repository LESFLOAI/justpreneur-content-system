# JustPreneur Strategist Brief — BTS Bogotá Economic Balance
Target slot: 2026-10-15, 9:00 PM

## Approved Facts Block (verbatim, in force)
"BTS played two sold-out concerts on October 2-3, 2026, at Estadio Nemesio Camacho El Campín in Bogotá, Colombia, drawing a reported 100,000+ total attendees, with Bogotá's city government estimating roughly 43% of attendees came from outside Bogotá.

In a post-event economic balance released around October 6-8, 2026, Bogotá's city government (coverage attributes this to the Secretaría Distrital de Gobierno and the city's Observatorio de Turismo, working from Ticketmaster Colombia and Mabrian data) estimated approximately $99,000 million COP (roughly US$25.4 million, per wire-service conversion — not an official USD figure) in city-wide economic activity generated by the two concerts. This is a modeled estimate based on ticketing and travel-flow data, not an audited or confirmed actual, and the city did not publish a full methodology.

Within that estimate, the largest reported shares were food & beverage (~$39,108 million COP) and lodging (~$29,384 million COP), followed by internal transport (~$17,557 million COP) and shopping/souvenirs (~$12,976 million COP) — figures that sum to approximately the reported $99,000 million total.

This post-event $99,000 million estimate is a different figure, from a different point in time, using a different methodology, than an earlier pre-event projection of $126,054 million COP published in February 2026; the two numbers must never be conflated or averaged.

Separately, according to Mabrian data cited in the city's balance, air/flight bookings into Bogotá for arrivals in the concert week (Sept 30-Oct 4, 2026) rose 46.6% year-over-year — this is a flight-booking metric, not a hotel-booking figure, and should not be described as 'hotel bookings.'

Travel agency Viajes Falabella separately reported that Bogotá's broader October 2026 concert calendar (including BTS and other acts) was driving city-wide hotel bookings up roughly 110% and tour packages up roughly 90% year-over-year. This figure is single-sourced to Viajes Falabella (a commercial travel agency, not a government statistic), republished by multiple outlets without independent corroboration, and reporting across outlets is internally inconsistent about which percentage applies to October overall versus specific concert weeks. It should be presented as agency-reported travel-industry data, clearly attributed, and not treated as a confirmed or precise citywide statistic."

## Mandatory constraints (carried forward)
- $99,000M COP is always "estimate" — never "audited actual."
- Never conflate/average the Feb 2026 ($126,054M) projection with the Oct 2026 ($99,000M) post-event figure.
- 46.6% = flight bookings, never "hotel bookings."
- Falabella 110%/90% stays attributed to Viajes Falabella, hedged as uncorroborated/inconsistently reported.
- Avoid saturated angle families: "Format Is the Product," "Access-as-strategy," deal/ownership-control cluster, funding/valuation cluster, "Buying Certainty Not Outcome," "Build the Category," "owned infrastructure" framing.
- Story Scout's suggested direction (anchor event as halo-economy infrastructure) is available to refine/replace — see Angle 2 for a partial refinement and Angle 1 for a full replacement.

## ANGLE 1 — The Forecast Discount (RECOMMENDED)

1. **One-sentence thesis:** Bogotá's own numbers show the pre-event hype projection ($126,054M COP) overshot the post-event modeled estimate ($99,000M COP) by roughly 21%, proving that anchor-event economic projections are marketing math, not an inventory plan.
2. **Entrepreneurial lesson:** Treat big pre-event projections as a ceiling, not a floor. Build variable-cost capacity (staffing, short-term inventory, flexible leases/pricing) around anchor events instead of committing fixed costs to the biggest number circulating in the press before the event happens.
3. **Opening hook:** "Bogotá projected $126 billion pesos in economic impact from BTS. The city's own post-event number came in about 21% lower. Here's the lesson most operators will miss."
4. **Audience benefit:** Gives hospitality/event operators and local entrepreneurs a concrete risk-management framework — how to size capacity around a projected anchor event without overbuilding on hype.
5. **Best format and why:** Carousel — the angle hinges on a direct numeric comparison (two figures, two dates, one gap) that reads cleanly in sequential slides and invites a save/screenshot; a Reel would compress the needed before/after data pairing.
6. **Recommended length:** 7-8 slides.
7. **Visual concept:** Slide 1 states the hook. Slide 2 shows the Feb 2026 projection figure large. Slide 3 shows the post-event Oct 2026 estimate figure large, visually "shrinking" against slide 2 (e.g., bar comparison, not averaged). Slides 4-5 unpack the spend breakdown (F&B, lodging, transport, retail) as the texture behind the smaller-than-projected number. Slides 6-7 land the entrepreneurial lesson (flex capacity vs. fixed commitment). Final slide = CTA.
8. **Risk of misunderstanding:** Readers could read the smaller post-event figure as the concerts "underperforming" or "flopping." Must state plainly: both figures are estimates/projections from different methodologies at different times, $99,000M COP is still a very large one-weekend modeled estimate, and the story is about planning discipline — not an underwhelming outcome.

## ANGLE 2 — The Spend Composition Map (refines Story Scout direction)

1. **One-sentence thesis:** Within Bogotá's own estimate, food & beverage and lodging together account for roughly 70% of the modeled economic activity — which tells builders exactly where anchor-event dollars concentrate, and it isn't evenly spread across "the event economy."
2. **Entrepreneurial lesson:** Before an anchor event lands in your market, use the category breakdown (not the headline total) to decide where to put prep, pricing, and availability — F&B and lodging operators have the clearest shot at capture; transport and retail are a smaller, secondary opportunity.
3. **Opening hook:** "Bogotá's estimate puts BTS's economic impact at $99 billion pesos. Almost 70% of that modeled activity sits in two categories: restaurants and hotels."
4. **Audience benefit:** Gives F&B and lodging operators a data-backed prioritization lens for pre-event prep ahead of the next concert, conference, or festival in their city.
5. **Best format and why:** Carousel with a simple breakdown chart — the four-category split is inherently visual data and benefits from a chart slide readers can screenshot.
6. **Recommended length:** 6-7 slides.
7. **Visual concept:** Donut or stacked-bar chart slide showing the four categories with their COP figures, followed by slides interpreting what the top two categories mean for operator positioning.
8. **Risk of misunderstanding:** Could read as treating city-modeled figures as audited/precise; the city did not publish a full methodology, so slides must keep "estimate" language intact and avoid implying category-level numbers are more certain than the total.

## ANGLE 3 — The Import Economy

1. **One-sentence thesis:** Nearly half of the 100,000+ concertgoers weren't Bogotá residents, meaning the real prize behind the ticket sales was a short, dense window of outside spending power moving through local businesses.
2. **Entrepreneurial lesson:** Anchor events function as a brief import of non-local demand; the move is building visibility, packages, and booking systems in advance so transient high-spending visitors can find and choose you during that narrow window — not assuming foot traffic alone will convert.
3. **Opening hook:** "43% of the people at BTS in Bogotá didn't live there. That's a 72-hour wave of outside money most local businesses weren't positioned to catch."
4. **Audience benefit:** Prompts creators/operators to treat anchor events as short-window visitor-acquisition opportunities and plan pre-event visibility specifically for travelers, not just locals.
5. **Best format and why:** Reel — the "wave of outside money" framing is narrative and benefits from voiceover pacing and quick visual cuts; a punchier short-form hook performs better than a slow data reveal here.
6. **Recommended length:** 30-45 seconds.
7. **Visual concept:** Quick-cut crowd/concert energy transitioning into generic hospitality visuals (restaurant counter, hotel front desk) with on-screen stat overlays timed to narration beats.
8. **Risk of misunderstanding:** Risk of conflating the 43% out-of-town attendee stat with the 46.6% flight-booking YoY growth as if they describe the same dataset — these must stay clearly separate and correctly labeled, and flight bookings must never be called hotel bookings.

## RECOMMENDATION: Angle 1 — The Forecast Discount

**Why this is the strongest angle:** It is the most genuinely overlooked story beneath the headline. Nearly every outlet covering this story will lead with "BTS generated ~$99,000M COP for Bogotá" as the takeaway. Almost none will surface that the city's own pre-event projection was materially higher — that gap is the non-obvious insight, and it converts a feel-good economic-impact headline into a concrete, counterintuitive lesson about timing, forecasting discipline, and capacity planning. It also cleanly sidesteps every excluded angle family (no ownership/deal framing, no "infrastructure," no access-as-strategy) while staying squarely inside the brand's execution/timing lens.

### Final strategic thesis
Bogotá's own economic balance shows pre-event hype projections overshot post-event modeled reality by roughly 21% — the lesson for builders isn't the size of the number, it's learning to discount hype projections and build capacity that flexes to the real outcome, not the biggest number circulating before the event.

### Primary takeaway
Treat anchor-event economic projections as directional hype, not a planning baseline — build flexible capacity, not fixed commitments, around them.

### Desired emotional response
Productive recognition/caution — "I would have built for the big number too" — not cynicism about the event or the city's reporting.

### Desired audience action
Save/share the post and reassess how they size capacity (inventory, staffing, pricing, short-term leases) ahead of the next anchor event in their own market, treating modeled estimates as a range rather than a guarantee.

2. "The Number Everyone Will Quote About BTS in Bogotá — and the Number They Won't."
