## Facts Summary

BTS played two sold-out concerts on October 2-3, 2026, at Estadio Nemesio Camacho El Campín in Bogotá, Colombia, drawing a reported 100,000+ total attendees, with Bogotá's city government estimating roughly 43% of attendees came from outside Bogotá.

In a post-event economic balance released around October 6-8, 2026, Bogotá's city government (coverage attributes this to the Secretaría Distrital de Gobierno and the city's Observatorio de Turismo, working from Ticketmaster Colombia and Mabrian data) estimated approximately $99,000 million COP (roughly US$25.4 million, per wire-service conversion — not an official USD figure) in city-wide economic activity generated by the two concerts. This is a modeled estimate based on ticketing and travel-flow data, not an audited or confirmed actual, and the city did not publish a full methodology.

Within that estimate, the largest reported shares were food & beverage (~$39,108 million COP) and lodging (~$29,384 million COP), followed by internal transport (~$17,557 million COP) and shopping/souvenirs (~$12,976 million COP) — figures that sum to approximately the reported $99,000 million total.

This post-event $99,000 million estimate is a different figure, from a different point in time, using a different methodology, than an earlier pre-event projection of $126,054 million COP published in February 2026; the two numbers must never be conflated or averaged — the ~21% gap between them is the core of this post's angle, explicitly framed as a forecasting lesson, not underperformance (both concerts sold out).

Separately, according to Mabrian data cited in the city's balance, air/flight bookings into Bogotá for arrivals in the concert week (Sept 30-Oct 4, 2026) rose 46.6% year-over-year — this is a flight-booking metric, not a hotel-booking figure.

Travel agency Viajes Falabella separately reported that Bogotá's broader October 2026 concert calendar (including BTS and other acts) was driving city-wide hotel bookings up roughly 110% and tour packages up roughly 90% year-over-year. This figure is single-sourced to Viajes Falabella (a commercial travel agency, not a government statistic), republished by multiple outlets without independent corroboration — presented in the copy as agency-reported data, explicitly hedged.

Full verification detail: `research/2026-10-15_fact-verification-bts-bogota.md`

## Final Caption

Bogotá projected $126,054 million COP from BTS's two sold-out shows before a single flight landed.

The post-event modeled estimate came in about 21% lower — roughly $99,000 million COP, built from ticketing and travel-flow data, not an audited actual.

Both numbers are legitimate. Neither is "wrong." They're answering different questions, at different points in time, using different methods — which is exactly why they shouldn't be compared as if they were the same number.

The builder lesson here isn't about BTS, or Bogotá, or even concerts. It's about what you do with a big projection before real demand shows up. Anchor-event hype numbers are directional signals — not a planning baseline. Build the capacity that flexes to what actually happens, not the capacity sized to the loudest number you heard first.
