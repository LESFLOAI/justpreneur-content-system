## 1. Facts Summary (Approved Facts Block, verbatim from Fact Verifier)

Getty Images Holdings, Inc. and several U.S. subsidiaries filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions on October 21, 2026, in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware, implementing a prepackaged restructuring plan under a Restructuring Support Agreement (RSA) signed October 12, 2026. Under the RSA, members of the Getty family and affiliates of Koch Equity Development (KED), the investment arm of Koch Industries, have committed to invest up to $533 million in new equity and are set to own 100% of the reorganized, private company. All existing public equity interests, including common stock, are expected to be cancelled with no recovery. Holders of approximately ~83.5% of Getty's unsecured notes agreed to eliminate $270 million of senior unsecured notes, while secured lenders (representing ~89% of secured funded debt and 100% of revolving loans) agreed to extend maturities and reduce interest costs. Getty reported total debt of approximately ~$2.1 billion as of June 30, 2026 (Reuters, citing company financials). Getty's Class A common stock was suspended from the NYSE on September 29, 2026, and had traded on OTC Pink since September 30, 2026. This restructuring follows Getty's termination, in early July 2026, of its proposed $3.7 billion merger with Shutterstock, after the UK Competition and Markets Authority conditioned clearance on Shutterstock divesting its editorial business — a condition Getty declined to accept; the U.S. Department of Justice had already cleared the deal unconditionally. Getty's CEO stated the company incurred more than $100 million in professional fees and financing costs over more than 18 months pursuing that merger (this was sunk deal cost, not a contractual breakup fee). Vote solicitation on the prepackaged plan began October 20, 2026. Getty expects the restructuring to be completed by year-end 2026.

**Not verified / excluded:** a reported additional ~$143 million Koch DIP financing commitment; any claim that the $533M nets to ~$433M after debt conversion. Neither figure appears anywhere in this package.

**Hard constraints that shaped all copy below:** shareholders are genuinely wiped out with no recovery — never minimized; this is a legitimate, court-supervised process — never implied as deceptive or "fake"; the $100M+ figure is sunk professional/financing cost, attributed to Getty's CEO, never called a "breakup fee"; the UK regulator never described as having "blocked" the deal — framed as conditioning clearance on a divestiture Getty declined.

## 5. Final Caption

On Oct 21, 2026, Getty Images filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy.

But the real decision was made nine days earlier — on Oct 12 — when Getty signed a Restructuring Support Agreement with its creditors and incoming owners.

By the time the public heard "bankruptcy," the outcome was already locked:

→ The Getty family + Koch Equity Development committed up to $533M in new equity and are set to own 100% of the reorganized company.
→ ~83.5% of unsecured noteholders agreed to wipe out $270M in debt.
→ Secured lenders (~89% of that debt, 100% of revolving loans) agreed to extend terms and cut interest costs.
→ Existing public shareholders are expected to be wiped out completely — no recovery. Stock was suspended from the NYSE on Sept 29 and has traded OTC Pink since Sept 30.

This follows Getty's decision in July 2026 to walk away from a $3.7B merger with Shutterstock, after the UK's competition regulator would only approve it if Shutterstock sold its editorial business. Getty said no — after already spending, per its CEO, $100M+ chasing that deal over 18+ months. (The US DOJ had cleared the merger with no conditions.)

To be clear: this is a legitimate, court-supervised process. A "prepackaged" Chapter 11 means the plan is negotiated and agreed before the filing — not a workaround, not a trick. Getty reported ~$2.1B in total debt as of June 30, 2026, and expects the restructuring complete by year-end.

The lesson isn't about Getty specifically. It's about sequencing: line up your key stakeholders privately before you take a hard call public. The announcement should execute a decision already made — not open a negotiation in front of everyone.
