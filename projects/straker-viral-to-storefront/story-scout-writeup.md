## 1. Working headline

"The TikTok Chef Who Took Over Keith McNally's Ghost Restaurant" — Thomas Straker's first U.S. restaurant, Straker's, opens in SoHo in the long-dark space of McNally's legendary Lucky Strike.

## 2. What happened (core facts)

Thomas Straker — a British chef who went viral on TikTok/Instagram during COVID lockdown for his "All Things Butter" cooking videos (now cited at 1 billion+ cumulative views on that content format) and who turned that following into a hit London restaurant (Straker's, Notting Hill, opened 2022) — has opened his first restaurant outside the UK: **Straker's, New York**, at 59 Grand Street in SoHo. The space is the former **Lucky Strike**, the French bistro Keith McNally (Balthazar, Minetta Tavern, Pastis) opened in 1989 and was forced to close permanently in April 2020 during the COVID shutdown, after which the address sat dormant for roughly five years.

Straker announced he'd "secured the keys" to the space in April 2025. The restaurant had a celebrity-studded pre-opening moment on **September 10, 2026** — the first day of New York Fashion Week — when Net-a-Porter took over the still-not-officially-open dining room for an "Incredible Women" cocktail event honoring Cindy Crawford (cover star of PORTER's September issue), hosted by Net-a-Porter CEO Heather Kaminetsky. Reported guests included Serena Williams, Katie Holmes, Emmy Rossum, Nara Smith, Madeline Brewer, Aurora James, Athena Calderone (or, per a conflicting outlet, Joseph Altuzarra and stylist Kate Young instead of Calderone), and designers Willy Chavarria, Wes Gordon, and Christopher John Rogers. Coverage at the time described the restaurant's "official debut" as coming "later this month" (September 2026).

- It is one of the most talked-about fall 2026 NYC restaurant openings across food/lifestyle press (Haute Living, Wine Spectator, The Infatuation, The Spectator all flagged it in fall-2026 "openings to watch" roundups).

- It plugs into two always-hot NYC cultural narratives at once: nostalgia for a lost "old SoHo" institution (Lucky Strike/Keith McNally), and the ongoing "creator economy invades real-world business" conversation.

## 5. Potential JustPreneur lesson

The core, credible takeaway: **Straker didn't just monetize attention with merch or sponsorships — he converted it into a owned, physical, high-capex asset (a full-service restaurant) in one of the most expensive real estate markets in the world, and he did it twice, in two different countries, proving the content-to-brick-and-mortar pipeline repeats.** Secondary angles available to the writer:
- A five-year-dormant, "ghost" commercial space (one with real cultural cachet) got revived specifically because a creator's audience de-risked the real estate bet for landlords/investors.
- The slow build (lease secured April 2025 → opening September 2026, ~17 months) is a useful counter-note to "move fast" creator-economy narratives — this was a patient, considered expansion, not an impulsive cash-in.
- The NYFW/Net-a-Porter activation shows how a hospitality brand can borrow a bigger brand's audience and press cycle for a pre-launch moment instead of buying it.

This is NOT a deal/M&A, ownership-structure, or funding-valuation story, so it sits outside the clusters flagged as saturated/in cooldown (owned infrastructure, deal/ownership-control, funding/valuation-divergence, and the specific cooled angles like dormant-IP-reactivation — note: this is dormant *real estate*, not dormant IP, though the Fact Verifier/writer should double check this distinction is being preserved and not blurred into the cooled "dormant-IP-reactivation" framing).
