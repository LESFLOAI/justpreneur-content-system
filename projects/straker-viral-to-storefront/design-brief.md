# Straker's NYC — "Borrowed Audience, Borrowed Press Cycle" — Visual Design Brief
**Format:** Instagram carousel, 10 slides, 1080x1350 (4:5 portrait)
**Angle:** Borrowed Audience, Borrowed Press Cycle
**Status:** DRAFT MODE — no images generated yet (no image-generation API key available in this cloud environment, per explicit instruction for this run). All prompts below are final and ready for `/justpreneur-finish` to execute locally. **IMAGE NOT YET GENERATED — PROMPT ONLY applies to every slide listed in Section 10.**

**Governing fact constraint (non-negotiable for every slide):** Straker's NYC has not been confirmed open to the public as of this post. No slide or image may depict, or visually imply, a currently open, bustling, actively-serving dining room. No slide may render a recognizable likeness of any real named person (Thomas Straker, Heather Kaminetsky, Cindy Crawford, Serena Williams, Katie Holmes, Emmy Rossum, Nara Smith, Aurora James, Willy Chavarria, Wes Gordon, Christopher John Rogers, Keith McNally, or anyone else). People are represented only through anonymous, faceless, or fully blurred/silhouetted forms, or through objects/props — never through a posed, named, or identifiable portrait. No real brand logos (Net-a-Porter, PORTER, Lucky Strike signage, TikTok/Instagram UI) appear in any generated image.

---

## 1. Overall Creative Concept

Treat this as a quiet, confident business-and-culture editorial explainer — closer to a Business of Fashion or Puck "how the room got built" feature than a restaurant-review or motivational-template post. The visual language sits at the intersection of **SoHo bistro history** (cast-iron facades, marble, brass, warm low light) and **fashion-week press energy** (velvet rope, soft camera-flash bokeh, step-and-repeat glow) — without ever showing a crowd, a finished dining room in service, or a named face.

The carousel is held together by one recurring device: a thin horizontal **gold press-line rail** running along the bottom of every slide, carrying a single small circular **status marker** whose state advances across the ten slides — unlit → lit → bright/flash → lit (steady) → **hollow/outlined** (the one deliberate break, on the honesty slide) → lit again (resolved) — so the "is this confirmed yet?" thread is legible even to someone who only skims the images. This continues the visual family established in the sibling project `restaurant-isnt-open-yet` (same real-world subject, same no-likeness / no-open-dining-room discipline, same bottom-rail-and-marker device), so the two carousels read as part of one coherent JustPreneur visual system even though the angle and slide count differ.

Mood: cinematic, warm, slightly hushed — the feeling of a room lit for a private event rather than a restaurant doing dinner service. Photographic/editorial style throughout (not flat vector), shallow depth of field, practical warm lighting (candlelight, brass sconces, string-light bokeh) rather than flat studio lighting.

## 2. Composition & Focal Hierarchy (applies to all 10 slides unless noted)

Fixed template skeleton, repeated across all 10 slides for carousel cohesion:

1. **Top safe zone (y: 0–100px):** empty/breathing room only. No text here.
2. **Headline/body-copy zone (y: ~110–520px):** the on-slide text line from the approved copy, largest type weight on the slide, left-aligned (centered only on Slides 1 and 10). Length varies by slide — see Section 3 for exact y-ranges per slide.
3. **Image/environment zone (y: ~540–1050px):** the photographic/editorial focal image, generated by image-gen, always a quiet or in-between space (facade, bar detail, empty place setting, doorway) — never an active service scene, never a crowd, never a named face.
4. **Supporting-copy zone (y: ~1060–1220px):** the one-sentence supporting line from the approved copy, steel-gray or warm-white, smaller weight than the headline/body text.
5. **Press-line rail + status marker (y: ~1240–1260px):** thin gold horizontal line spanning the safe text width, with one circular marker whose position/state advances per Section 8.
6. **Brand zone (y: ~1270–1300px):** JustPreneur wordmark, bottom-center, small-but-legible, identical position/size/color on every slide. On Slide 10 only, add "@justpreneur_hq" directly beneath the wordmark.

Focal hierarchy order on every slide: (1) headline/on-slide text → (2) photographic image → (3) supporting sentence → (4) press-line rail/marker → (5) JustPreneur brand mark. Nothing competes with the on-slide text for first-glance attention.

## 4. Typography Direction

- **Headline/on-slide-text font:** bold, high-contrast editorial serif with a business/culture-press feel (e.g., Tiempos Headline / Canela / Freight Display style) — signals premium journalism, not a motivational template. Used for the primary on-slide text line on every slide.
- **Supporting-copy font:** clean grotesk/sans (e.g., Söhne / Inter / Neue Haas style), regular/medium weight, smaller size, generous line-height (1.3–1.4x) for mobile legibility — this pairing (serif headline + sans body) matches the sibling `restaurant-isnt-open-yet` project for cross-post visual consistency on this subject.
- **Slide 5 guest-list line:** same sans family as all supporting copy, but set at the smallest body size used anywhere in this carousel, with extra line-height, so eight names remain legible without feeling like a label or caption block.
- **Slide 9 sidebar text:** same serif/sans pairing as every other slide — no new typeface or "warning" styling is introduced; the only typographic difference is the thin vertical gold rule beside the text block (see Section 3).
- **JustPreneur wordmark:** consistent lockup across all 10 slides — same type treatment, same size, same color (gold on navy), same vertical position from the bottom edge, on every slide including Slide 9 (the wordmark itself never desaturates, even on the caveat slide).
- All exact text (on-slide text, supporting copy, guest names, wordmark, follow handle) is added by the design/layout tool after image generation — per brand policy, the image model is never asked to render legible exact text.

## 5. Color Palette

- **Deep navy** (#0B1326) — dominant background across all 10 slides, carries the "editorial at night" premium tone.
- **Steel gray** (#5B6472) — secondary color (supporting-copy text, muted architectural detail, the full dominant tone of Slide 9's desaturated treatment).
- **Warm white** (#F5F2EC) — headline/on-slide text default, supporting copy on darker image areas.
- **Energy gold** (#D9A94C / #E6B655) — accent/signal color reserved for: the press-line rail and its status marker, the JustPreneur wordmark, and warm practical-light sources within the images (candlelight, brass, string-light bokeh, the single storefront glow on Slide 10). Gold must never spread so widely that it loses its "this is lit / this matters" signaling function.
- **Bistro brass/amber** (#B07D3E) — secondary warm accent used only in the SoHo-history imagery (Slides 2, 3, 6, 7, 8) for brass fixtures, marble-and-wood tones, and candle/lamp glow — keeps the restaurant-history slides feeling like a real, specific room rather than a generic "fashion event" palette.
- **Slide 9 desaturation rule:** on Slide 9 only, pull gold saturation down by roughly 40% and let steel gray/navy dominate the image, leaving a single small warm light source as the only true-gold accent in the frame — the status marker, wordmark, and sidebar rule remain full-strength gold (brand elements never desaturate; only the generated photographic image does).

## 6. Image Treatment

- Photographic/cinematic editorial style throughout — not flat vector, not 3D-rendered, not illustrative. Shallow depth of field, soft practical warm lighting (candlelight, brass sconces, string-light bokeh, camera-flash bokeh) rather than flat studio lighting.
- Consistent facade anchor: the same cast-iron SoHo storefront silhouette (door, window proportions, awning frame) recurs across Slides 1, 3, 9, and 10's background block, so the carousel visually ties back to one real location without ever naming or signing it.
- No photographic depiction of a crowd, a seated/served dining room, or any named individual's face — people appear only as blurred, distant, or fully anonymous silhouettes, or are implied through objects (a place setting, an invitation card, a key).
- No real logos, signage text, or legible UI anywhere in any generated image (Net-a-Porter, PORTER, Lucky Strike, TikTok/Instagram, Resy, etc. are never rendered).
- Every image must leave large, clearly empty negative-space regions (headline zone, supporting-copy zone, rail/brand zone) with low visual noise — no particles, gradients, or clutter bleeding into those zones.

## 7. Instagram Safe-Margin Instructions

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350px.
- Keep all text (on-slide text, supporting copy, guest names, wordmark, follow handle) within a safe margin of **80px from every edge** (top, bottom, left, right) — live text area is roughly 920 x 1190px centered in the canvas.
- Keep the **bottom 150px** visually calm (low graphic density) since Instagram's UI (caption line, profile row on some placements) can crop or overlay near the bottom edge on feed/profile-grid previews — the press-line rail and wordmark sit inside this calm band by design, never competing with dense imagery.
- Nothing load-bearing (on-slide text, supporting copy, guest names, rail, marker, wordmark) may touch or cross the 80px margin at any edge.

## 8. Slide-to-Slide Consistency Rules

1. **Fixed template skeleton** (Section 2) applies to all 10 slides — same zones, same JustPreneur wordmark position/size/color on every slide.
2. **Press-line rail + status marker progression:** unlit (Slide 1) → lit, advancing left-to-right (Slides 2–3) → bright/flash, largest glow (Slide 4) → lit, steady (Slides 5–8) → **hollow/outlined, the one deliberate break** (Slide 9) → lit/solid, resolved at the final rail position (Slide 10). No slide outside Slide 9 may use the hollow marker state, and Slide 9 is the only slide permitted to desaturate its generated image.
3. **Facade continuity:** Slides 1, 3, 9, and 10 share the same cast-iron storefront silhouette/proportions so the carousel reads as one consistent real place across cover, history, caveat, and closing beats.
4. **Caveat-slide distinction without losing premium feel:** Slide 9 is visually distinguished only through (a) the hollow rail marker, (b) the desaturated/cooler image palette with a single remaining warm pilot light, and (c) a thin vertical gold sidebar rule beside its text block (editorial "correction" styling, borrowed from print-magazine fact-check boxes) — never through red/alarm color, exclamation iconography, a stamp, a banner, or any typeface change. The wordmark and type treatment remain identical to every other slide so the carousel's premium tone never breaks.
5. **No real likeness, logos, or open-dining-room imagery on any slide** (Section 1 governing constraint) — this rule is absolute and applies to every image-generation prompt in Section 10.
6. **No slide may introduce icons, labels, dates-as-captions, slide numbers, or production marks beyond what's specified above.**

## 9. Accessibility & Contrast Check

- Warm white (#F5F2EC) headline/body text on deep navy (#0B1326) background: contrast ratio well above WCAG AA (>12:1) — passes for both large and small text.
- Gold (#D9A94C) press-line rail/marker and wordmark on deep navy: contrast ratio ≈ 7:1 — passes AA for large/graphic elements.
- Steel gray (#5B6472) supporting copy must sit only on navy background (not directly on a busy image area) — if a supporting-copy line overlaps any part of the generated image in layout, add a subtle navy scrim behind the text block before placing type.
- Slide 5's eight-name guest list is the single highest text-density slide in the carousel — verify at final export that all names remain legible at thumbnail size (≈300px-wide preview); if any name blurs or crowds, increase line-height or split to a fourth line before increasing font size downward is considered.
- Slide 9's desaturated image must not reduce the contrast of its own warm white on-slide text or gold sidebar rule — verify the text and rail remain full-contrast against the cooler background once the image is in place.
- Verify at final export: on-slide text legible at thumbnail size on every slide — if any word blurs at that size, increase type size before publishing, do not rely on zoom.
