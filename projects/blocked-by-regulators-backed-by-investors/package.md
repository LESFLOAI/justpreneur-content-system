## Facts summary (Approved Facts Block, cleared by Fact Verifier — cleared with qualifications)

In December 2025, Meta agreed to acquire Chinese-founded, Singapore-headquartered AI agent startup Manus (parent company Butterfly Effect, led by CEO Xiao Hong) in a deal reported at more than $2 billion — a price never officially confirmed by either company. In early 2026, China's National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) opened a review of the deal on foreign-investment grounds; Reuters reported (via the Financial Times) that NDRC barred Manus co-founders Xiao Hong and chief scientist Ji Yichao from leaving China during the inquiry. On April 27, 2026, NDRC ordered the deal unwound, prohibiting foreign investment in the Manus project. Reporting (originating with Chinese outlet 36Kr, corroborated in part by Caixin Global) indicates that by around August 2026, a consortium of Manus's original backers — including Tencent, ZhenFund, and HSG (formerly Sequoia China) — bought the company back from Meta at a price reported to be around $2 billion, with Tencent emerging as the largest shareholder after absorbing the stake previously held by Benchmark (which did not participate in the buyback). Manus announced a return to independent operations in the following weeks. On October 8, 2026, Butterfly Effect announced it had raised more than $500 million in a new funding round led by Boyu Capital and IDG Capital, with existing investors Tencent, HSG, and ZhenFund also participating — its first funding round since separating from Meta. The company did not disclose a post-funding valuation; Bloomberg and the Wall Street Journal had previously reported, citing unnamed sources, that the round was targeting a valuation of roughly $4 billion, a figure Butterfly Effect itself has not confirmed.
