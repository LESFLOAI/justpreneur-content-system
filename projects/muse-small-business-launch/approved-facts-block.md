## 6. Missing Context That Could Materially Change Interpretation

- **Trust/privacy controversy running in parallel:** In the days immediately before the Sept 29 SMB launch, Meta was actively managing a Muse trust controversy — reports (AppleInsider, Futurism, Sept 27–28, 2026) that Muse accessed Mac/iPhone Messages without clear user consent, and a Meta Superintelligence Labs executive (David Singleton) publicly apologizing/clarifying how the messaging-access feature worked (Inc.com, Sept 23, 2026; TechCrunch "Can Muse overcome Meta's trust issues?", Sept 27, 2026). The Scout has explicitly and correctly instructed that this story should NOT be angled around that controversy (to avoid duplicating the pipeline's recent "Delegated Authority Isn't Free Access" angle from 2026-09-26). That's a reasonable editorial call — but the Strategist and Editor should be aware the SMB launch landed in the middle of active trust scrutiny, so the piece should avoid a tone that reads as unaware of or dismissive of that context, even while not making it the angle.
- **"Autonomously" framing risk:** Given the parallel trust controversy is specifically about the agent overstepping permissions, describing the SMB tool as acting "autonomously" is not just factually imprecise (see #4 above) — it is also the single word most likely to read as tone-deaf or to invite pushback in the current news environment. Recommend the Strategist avoid "autonomous" language entirely in headline/lede.
- **Meta's own framing is business-development-driven:** Meta explicitly says the SMB product exists because it discovered organic small-business usage of consumer Muse — this supports the Scout's "watch what your users improvise, then build it" angle and is well-supported by Wang's own quote. No correction needed to the angle itself.
