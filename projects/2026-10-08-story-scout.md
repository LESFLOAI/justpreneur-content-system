## CANDIDATE 1 (RECOMMENDED) — Advertising Week New York names Dhar Mann first "Chief Creator Officer," launches $100M Creator Challenge

**Headline:** "Creators Have the Hardest Thing to Get: Trust" — Advertising Week NY Puts a Creator in Charge of $100M in Deals

**What happened:** Advertising Week New York (Oct 5–8, 2026, at The Penn District) appointed YouTube/social creator and studio founder Dhar Mann as its first-ever Chief Creator Officer, and he opened the event with a keynote launching the "$100 Million Creator Challenge" — a push to get brands to commit real budget to creator deals live, on the show floor, rather than just talk about the creator economy on panels. Organizers (global president Ruth Mortimer) reframed the week's creator track from "conversation to commerce," with live matchmaking between creators and brand budget-holders.

**Date of development:** Keynote/launch October 5, 2026; event runs through October 8, 2026 (today).

**Why it's gaining attention now:** The event is literally concluding today, so this is the most live, in-window story available — an industry event built around a dollar figure and a named creator-turned-executive, with trade press (The Drum, NetInfluencer) actively covering it this week.

**Potential JustPreneur lesson:** The scarce resource in the creator economy right now isn't reach, it's trust and deal-infrastructure — advertisers are hiring creators into literal executive roles to broker that trust into booked revenue. Lesson: creators who want real business outcomes need to build (or borrow) matchmaking/sales infrastructure, not just content.

**Best format:** Carousel (3-5 cards: the appointment, the $100M target, the "trust" quote, the lesson) or single post with a strong pull-quote graphic.

**Expected audience response:** Strong engagement from the creator/entrepreneur segment of the audience; good comment bait around "is this real deal flow or a PR number?" and "creators as executives" debate.

**What happened:** MATTE Projects' flagship venue HERO, built inside the former Rockefeller Center Finance Station post office on the Rink Level, opened "reSOUND New York" — a multisensory light/sound exhibit from Korean collective d'strict (creators of the viral digital waterfall/WAVE pieces), running through October 31, 2026. It's the latest in a string of rotating, ticketed, sponsor-backed exhibitions (prior show was Bose-powered) that HERO licenses in rather than producing from scratch each time.

**Date of development:** Recent debut, running now through Oct 31, 2026 (Time Out NY and Rockefeller Center's own site have fresh coverage this week).

**Why it's gaining attention now:** It's a brand-new, currently-running NYC attraction with strong visual assets, plus it's the venue's international debut for a proven Seoul hit (110,000+ visitors, 75 countries), making it a trend piece about importing proven IP rather than betting on originals.

**Potential JustPreneur lesson:** A fixed, expensive piece of real estate (a landmark basement post office) becomes a recurring-revenue business not by owning one show forever, but by operating as a rotating platform for licensed, pre-validated content — de-risking each "season" the way a venue books touring acts instead of commissioning originals every time.

**Best format:** Reel (the visuals are inherently video-native) or carousel pairing visuals with the business-model breakdown.

**Expected audience response:** High saves/shares for visuals; moderate comment engagement on the business angle unless the caption does the work of surfacing the model.

## CANDIDATE 3 — Medellín's foreign-buyer real estate boom: an entire housing product built for digital nomads

**Headline:** Medellín Developers Aren't Building Apartments Anymore — They're Building a Product for 8,000 Monthly Arrivals

**What happened:** Medellín's residential market has seen average middle-class price gains over 17% and rent spikes up to 80% in nomad-heavy neighborhoods, driven by developers building purpose-made "viviendas turísticas" — a hybrid of boutique hotel, co-living, and studio apartment — specifically for the wave of foreign digital nomads and remote workers (one estimate puts arrivals around 8,300/month). Roughly 90 such projects are underway per one local research group.

**Date of development:** Ongoing 2026 trend, most recently synthesized in Nearshore Americas and Nexo.legal pieces dated/updated for 2026; not a single-day news event.

**Why it's gaining attention now:** Tension between foreign-capital-driven development and local affordability/gentrification is a live, recurring LATAM conversation, and the "they built a housing category for a buyer persona" framing is a clean business story.

**Potential JustPreneur lesson:** When a new, well-funded buyer segment shows up in volume, the winning real estate play isn't renting out old inventory — it's building an entirely new product category engineered for that segment's actual behavior (short stays, remote work, community), even if it strains the existing market.

**Best format:** Carousel (data points + the "new category" framing) — this is a numbers-and-context story, not a single striking image.

**Expected audience response:** Likely to spark real debate (gentrification critics vs. real-estate/entrepreneur audience) — good for comments, moderate for passive likes.

**Sources:**
- https://nearshoreamericas.com/nearshore-model-is-shaken-by-medellins-rocketing-property-values/
- https://news.bloomberglaw.com/tech-and-telecom-law/digital-nomads-drive-a-development-boom-in-medellin
- https://nexo.legal/foreign-buyers-medellin-vs-other-colombian-cities-2026/

**Needs verification:** This is a trend, not a dated news event — several key stats (17% price gain, 80% rent spikes, 8,300/month arrivals, ~90 projects) come from single sources (one undated, one from a developer-linked research group with a potential conflict of interest) and must be cross-checked against ACI Medellín or Colombian notary/registry data before publishing any number.
