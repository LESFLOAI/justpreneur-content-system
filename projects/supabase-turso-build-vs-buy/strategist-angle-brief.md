# Strategist Angle Brief — Supabase / Turso

**Selected angle: "Build vs. Buy, Decided by Data"**

Final strategic thesis: Supabase's own usage data revealed a customer segment (AI agents) ahead of the market, and rather than spend time building for it, the company bought the capability and the team that already had it — proving that the fastest response to a validated signal is sometimes acquisition, not construction.

Primary takeaway: When your product's usage data points to an emerging customer type, don't default to "we'll build that" — evaluate whether buying proven capability and talent gets you there faster.

Desired emotional response: Sharp recognition/respect — "that's a smarter move than it looks like at first glance" — not hype, not alarm.

Desired audience action: Save/share the post and reflect on their own usage data for an overlooked customer segment; consider build-vs-buy as an explicit decision point.

Headline options considered:
1. "Supabase Saw AI Agents Taking Over Its Platform — Then Bought the Company That Already Served Them" (selected, lightly tightened)
2. "70% of New Customers Weren't Human. Supabase's Answer Wasn't to Build. It Was to Buy."
3. "The Build-vs-Buy Decision Hiding Inside Supabase's $150M Week"

Format: Carousel, 8 slides — signal → fork-in-the-road → decision → team/talent retained → stacked capital → framework → CTA.

Constraints honored: avoided the "owned vs. rented infrastructure" framing (used 4x recently); chosen as a non-celebrity B2B/funding story to balance recent celebrity-heavy coverage.
