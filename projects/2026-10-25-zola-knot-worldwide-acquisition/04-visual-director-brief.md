## 1. Overall Creative Concept

This is a business-media deal breakdown that happens to live in the wedding-industry orbit — so the design must read like *The Information*, *Axios*, or *Bloomberg Businessweek* covering an M&A story, not like a wedding-planning or lifestyle account. No florals, no pastels, no script fonts, no confetti.

The one controlled nod to the subject matter is a recurring **abstract interlocking-rings / linked-line motif** — two thin, minimal line-drawn rings or loops, rendered in energy gold on navy, used only as a small structural divider or background accent. It quietly echoes "The Knot" and "a deal that links two brands" without becoming a literal wedding graphic. It never competes with text and never appears as a "label" or icon in the small-mark sense the brand rules prohibit — it is a design motif woven into the background/divider geometry, not a logo, icon badge, or caption.

Think: a premium deal-memo aesthetic — navy "paper," gold foil accents, confident condensed display type for headlines, clean grotesk for body copy, generous white space, and one abstract textured/geometric background image per slide (no photography of people, since no approved photo assets exist and the story is about a corporate deal, not individuals' faces).

## 2. Composition & Focal Hierarchy (applies to all slides unless noted)

1. **Headline / primary statement** — top-to-upper-middle third, largest type on the slide, always the first thing the eye lands on.
2. **Supporting copy / body text** — middle band, smaller weight, generous line spacing, left-aligned for an editorial-memo feel (not centered walls of text).
3. **Motif accent** — a single thin gold linked-rings/line glyph used as a divider between headline and body, or as a subtle corner-adjacent background element (never literally in a corner "label" position — it sits inside the safe margin as part of the layout, not as a small production mark).
4. **JustPreneur wordmark** — bottom band, consistently placed, always legible, same size/position every slide (see Section 3).
5. **Background texture** — abstract navy/gradient/geometric field generated by the image model, intentionally built with negative space so no text competes with a busy area.

## 4. Typography Direction

- **Headline / lead-line typeface direction:** a bold, condensed-to-semi-condensed grotesk/sans with high legibility at small mobile sizes and strong presence at large sizes — direction equivalent to **Söhne Kräftig/Breit, Akkurat Bold, or National 2 Condensed Bold**. Use all-caps sparingly (cover headline only); sentence case elsewhere for a more editorial, less shouty feel.
- **Body copy typeface direction:** a clean, warm grotesk for readability at small sizes — direction equivalent to **Söhne Buch, Inter, or Graphik Regular**, set at a size that stays legible on a phone screen (minimum effective body size equivalent to ~34–38px at this canvas resolution, generous 1.3–1.4x line height).
- **Weight hierarchy:** lead line = heaviest weight on the slide; body = regular/medium; the one "punch line" per slide (e.g., "Zola stays Zola," "this deal has not closed") gets a weight bump but not a color change, to avoid looking like a highlighted ad.
- **No script, no serif-romantic, no handwriting fonts** — these would pull the design toward "wedding content" and undercut the business-media register this post needs.

## 9. Accessibility & Contrast Check

- Warm white (`#F6F2EA`) body/headline text on deep navy (`#0B1E33`) background: contrast ratio is very high (well above WCAG AA 4.5:1 for body text), safe for all lead lines and body copy.
- Energy gold (`#C9A24C`) on deep navy: sufficient contrast for short punch-words and the motif glyph, but **gold should not be the primary color for long body paragraphs** — reserve it for lead-line punch words, the motif, and the wordmark, where type is larger/bolder and contrast tolerance is higher.
- Steel gray (`#8A94A6`) on navy: acceptable for secondary/supporting lines only (Slide 6, captions-within-slide) — not for primary lead lines, since contrast is lower than warm white.
- If a warm-white "memo" background variant is used (Slide 2 or 6), flip body text to deep navy for the same high-contrast relationship.
- Minimum effective body text size must remain legible at phone-screen viewing distance; no body paragraph should run longer than ~5–6 lines before a line break for scannability (copy already fits this constraint).
- No motif or background texture element should ever be placed directly behind text without the specified negative-space band — avoids low-contrast collisions.
