## 1. Overall Creative Concept

This is a business-media "the top line lied to you" story, not a restaurant post and not a motivational template. Visual language should sit closer to a financial-press explainer (think Bloomberg, The Information, Axios Markets) than to a QSR/food account — no Wendy's trademarks, no food photography, no fast-food color cosplay (no literal Wendy's red-and-white branding reproduced anywhere).

The single visual thesis carried across the whole deck: **a diverging-line motif** — one line (representing size: store count / revenue / footprint) stays flat-to-slightly-up, confident and steady in warm white or gold; a second line (representing health: store-level EBITDA / profit) breaks away and falls sharply, rendered in a muted signal red. The two lines start close together and visibly separate as the carousel progresses — on Slide 1 and 2 they are nearly overlapping ("looks fine"), by Slide 3 they have clearly split, and by Slide 6 the red line is deep in negative territory. This is the one abstract graphic idea doing all the conceptual work — never literal bar charts with fake axis labels, never fabricated numbers rendered as chart text (exact numbers are set by the layout tool as real type, not left to the image model).

A companion secondary motif for Slide 6 only: a simple **store-count ticker** — a minimal, abstract odometer/tally-style graphic suggesting a number winding downward (closures), no readable digits rendered by the image model (the real numbers go on top via layout).

Slide 7 (The Dispute) is the one deliberate exception: it carries the highest text density in the deck and is flagged by the copywriter for a lighter visual treatment. It drops the diverging-line motif and signal-red accent entirely in favor of a calm, flat, almost-empty background — see Section 4 and the Slide 7 prompt below.

---

## 2. Composition and Focal Hierarchy (applies to every slide unless noted)

1. **Headline** — upper third, largest type, highest contrast, first thing the eye hits.
2. **Diverging-line motif / data accent** — sits as a restrained background/mid-ground graphic behind or beside the copy, never directly behind body text without a scrim; carries the "size vs. health" idea visually without needing to be read as a literal chart.
3. **Supporting body copy / stat blocks** — middle-to-lower band, left-aligned, generous line spacing; on numbers-heavy slides (2, 3, 6, 7) treated as short stat-card lines or a tight list rather than a dense paragraph.
4. **JustPreneur wordmark** — small, fixed position, every slide, never competing with headline.

Reading order every slide: Headline → stat/body → motif (reinforces, doesn't interrupt) → branding.

## 3. JustPreneur Branding — Exact Placement

- Wordmark "JustPreneur" in clean energy-gold or warm-white sans serif, one consistent size across all 8 slides — do not let it grow/shrink slide to slide.
- Position: **bottom-left corner**, inside the safe margin, identical x/y coordinates on every slide.
- Always sits on a solid or gradient-darkened zone for contrast — never placed over high-detail or high-contrast imagery without a scrim behind it.
- No tagline, no handle, no slide-count numeral, no date stamp, no "Chapter 11" case-number citation mark near the wordmark — name only, per brand rule.

- Background: the diverging-line motif enters here at its subtlest — the two lines nearly overlapping, confident, in the lower third, kept dark/low-contrast so it reads as atmosphere, not a stat to parse.

- Visual: this is where the diverging-line motif visibly splits — the size line continues flat/up in warm white, the health line breaks downward in signal red, clearly separating for the first time in the deck.

- JustPreneur wordmark bottom-left.

- Layout: Headline top. "33" set large as a ticking-down numeral treatment (visually implying a counter winding lower, e.g., set against a faint odometer-style horizontal tick-mark strip), in signal red. "Up to 60 more" set as the second-largest stat beneath it, still red-leaning but slightly lower contrast to show "projected, not final." The $18.6M buyer-proposal line set smallest, in steel gray/warm white, clearly visually subordinate since it's a pending, unresolved detail, not a confirmed closure count.

The diverging-line motif's red line should be at its lowest point of the whole deck in the background treatment.

## 5. Typography Direction

- **Headline typeface direction:** bold, high-legibility grotesque/sans serif (direction equivalent to Akkurat Bold, Suisse Int'l Bold, or National 2 Condensed Bold) — confident, financial-press weight, not playful.
- **Body/stat typeface direction:** clean humanist grotesk (direction equivalent to Inter or Graphik Regular/Medium) for all supporting copy and stat lines.
- **Numerals:** headline-weight numerals for the hero stats (48%, 320, $617.7M, 33, 60, $18.6M) — numbers are the stars of this carousel and should always be set larger/bolder than the sentence around them.
- **Color-for-emphasis, not size alone:** negative/declining figures in signal red; stable/size figures in warm white or gold; this pairs with (never substitutes for) the line-motif color logic so the same information is never conveyed by color alone (see Section 9).
- Slide 7 uses a slightly smaller body-type size than the rest of the deck (by design, to carry more lines) but never below a comfortable mobile-legible minimum, and with increased line-height to compensate.

## 6. Color Palette

- **Deep navy** (~#0E1A2B) — primary background.
- **Steel gray** (~#8A94A6) — secondary/supporting text, quiet labels (e.g., "Status: unresolved").
- **Warm white** (~#F5F1E8) — primary body text, "size" line of the motif.
- **Energy gold** (~#C9A24A) — headline accents, hero numerals, CTA line, wordmark.
- **Signal red** (~#B8433A, a muted brick/editorial red, not a neon alarm red) — reserved exclusively for down-trending data: the "health" line of the motif, negative percentages/losses, the Slide 3 down-arrow, the Slide 6 closures count. Never used as a dominant background wash; always an accent on navy, and never present at all on Slide 7.

This is a deliberate, brand-approved exception/extension of the standard navy-gray-white-gold palette, requested by the strategy brief specifically to carry "red/down iconography" on the numbers slides — it stays an accent color only, used consistently and sparingly, never contradicting the base brand system.

## 9. Slide-to-Slide Consistency Rules

1. Same canvas size (1080x1350) and same base palette (navy/steel/white/gold, with signal red as the controlled accent) on every slide except Slide 7, which deliberately drops red and the line motif per its "lighter treatment" flag — this is the one approved exception, not a drift.
2. Same JustPreneur wordmark — size, color, position (bottom-left) — identical across all 8 slides.
3. Same headline/body typefaces across all 8 slides; only color emphasis (white/gold vs. red) shifts intentionally with the data.
4. The diverging-line motif must visibly progress across the deck in a consistent direction — nearly overlapping (Slides 1–2), clearly split (Slide 3), paused for the timeline device (Slide 5), deepest separation (Slide 6), absent (Slide 7), frozen/resolved (Slide 8) — never reset or contradicted mid-deck.
5. Signal red is reserved strictly for declining/negative data and the "health" line — it never appears for a positive or neutral fact, so color always reinforces meaning, never decorates arbitrarily.
6. No slide introduces a new accent color, new font, or new branding position; Slide 7's "lighter treatment" is a reduction (fewer elements), not a new visual system.

## 10. Accessibility and Contrast Check

- Warm white (#F5F1E8) on deep navy (#0E1A2B) and energy gold (#C9A24A) on deep navy both exceed WCAG AA contrast comfortably at headline sizes.
- Signal red (#B8433A) on deep navy is readable at headline/stat size but is intentionally never used for small body-copy text — reserved for large numerals and short labels only, where contrast is strongest.
- Every negative/decline fact is conveyed redundantly by **wording** ("down ~48%," "net loss," "Status: unresolved") and **not by color alone** — safe for color-vision-deficient readers, consistent with brand accessibility rule.
- Every slide must pass the squint test: headline/hero-numeral legible as a shape/contrast block even blurred, enforced by always placing headline and hero stats on a flat navy or gradient-scrim zone, never directly on a busy motif.
- Slide 7's reduced-contrast, red-free treatment is itself an accessibility plus for that slide given its higher text density — calmer background, more line-height, nothing competing with the longer copy block.
