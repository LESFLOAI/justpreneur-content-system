## 1. Story & Facts Summary

**Story:** Meritage Hospitality Group, Inc. (OTC: MHGU) — one of the largest Wendy's franchisees in the U.S. — filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on September 17, 2026, one day after Wendy's franchising subsidiary moved to terminate its franchise agreements. The company is closing dozens of restaurants amid a collapse in store-level profitability, even though its headline revenue stayed large. **Angle:** Unit economics / overexpansion — "scale isn't a business model." Deliberately chosen to diverge from the pipeline's recently overused deal/exit/ownership-control angle cluster.

**Approved Facts Block (ground truth used for all copy):**

> Meritage Hospitality Group, Inc. (OTC: MHGU), headquartered in Grand Rapids, Michigan, is one of the largest Wendy's franchisees in the United States, operating 320 restaurants across 15 states — including 314 Wendy's locations — as of its bankruptcy filing. On September 17, 2026 (a Thursday), Meritage filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Western District of Michigan, one day after Wendy's franchising subsidiary, Quality Is Our Recipe LLC, sent a notice attempting to terminate Meritage's franchise agreements "effective immediately." Meritage disputes that the termination is valid and contends its franchise rights remain part of its bankruptcy estate; the dispute is unresolved. Wendy's has separately objected to some of Meritage's planned store closures.
>
> According to bankruptcy court filings, Meritage reported a net loss of $31.5 million for fiscal 2025, compared with net income of $8.0 million in 2024; revenue fell 7.6% to $617.7 million from $668.8 million. Meritage's CEO attributed the downturn in part to a roughly 48% decline in store-level EBITDA, citing beef-cost inflation and heavier discounting.
>
> Court filings disclose approximately $155 million in secured debt. Separately, Wendy's has asserted in court filings that Meritage owes it approximately $146.9–147 million in total, consisting of about $27.4 million in unpaid royalties, fees, and liquidated damages, plus about $119.5 million in continuing operations fees. A narrower $24.9 million figure (often rounded to $25 million) refers specifically to deferred franchise fees owed to Wendy's franchising entity as listed among Meritage's unsecured creditors — this is a subset of, not the total of, Wendy's claimed debt.
>
> As of October 20–21, 2026, Meritage had closed 33 restaurants since late September 2026 under its bankruptcy restructuring. Under a court-approved cash-collateral agreement, Meritage may be required to close an additional 20 to 60 restaurants by November 13, 2026. A buyer — Hiren Patel, owner of Divine Investors (operator of Dunkin' and KFC locations) — has proposed to acquire 42 Wendy's restaurants located across Arkansas, Tennessee, Mississippi, and Missouri (referred to in filings as Meritage's "Tennessee business") for $18.6 million as a going concern; the sale is pending court approval and could close as early as late November 2026. Meritage says it intends to continue operating its restaurants and paying its roughly 9,000 employees throughout the Chapter 11 process.

**Important framing constraints (carried through every slide/caption):** This is an active, unresolved legal/bankruptcy matter. No slide or caption states a final outcome. Disputed claims are attributed to the correct party throughout. The story is scoped to one franchisee's unit economics — it does not imply Wendy's the brand is struggling nationally.

## 4. Final Caption

Meritage Hospitality Group operated 320 restaurants across 15 states — 314 of them Wendy's — and pulled in $617.7 million in revenue. It's one of the largest Wendy's franchisees in the country. It also just filed for Chapter 11.

The headline numbers didn't collapse first. Store-level EBITDA did — down roughly 48%, driven by beef-cost inflation and heavier discounting, according to the company's CEO. Revenue dipped only 7.6%. The size stayed visible long after the profitability didn't.

The company has now closed 33 restaurants since late September, with up to 60 more possibly closing by November 13 under its restructuring plan. A buyer has offered $18.6M for 42 of the locations — still pending court approval. Separately, Meritage and Wendy's are disputing a franchise termination notice and competing debt claims; that fight is unresolved, and we're not calling it either way.

The takeaway isn't about this one franchisee, or about Wendy's as a brand. It's about what "big" can hide. Revenue growth, store count, headcount — none of them tell you if the unit economics underneath are holding up.

If you run a business with repeatable units — locations, clients, SKUs, territories — when's the last time you checked the profit on one of them, not the average across all of them?
