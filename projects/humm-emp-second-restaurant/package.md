## Facts Summary (Approved Facts Block, cleared with qualifications by Fact Verifier)

Daniel Humm, the three-Michelin-star chef behind Eleven Madison Park, is opening a new restaurant called **Humm** at 435 Hudson Street in Manhattan's West Village, in the former En (Japanese) Brasserie space. Multiple September 2026 outlets (Wine Spectator, Haute Living) report an opening date of **September 22, 2026**. The restaurant is an entirely à la carte, **modern American** concept built around a wood-fired hearth and shareable plates, with a wine program featuring Mediterranean bottles led by wine director Michael Duffy — Mediterranean applies to the wine program only, never the cuisine. The space was designed with Pierre Yovanovitch, with a ceiling mural by Francesco Clemente.

Separately, Humm made Eleven Madison Park fully plant-based in 2021; the shift proved polarizing with some longtime guests (attributed sentiment, not a quantified/documented event), and in August 2025 Humm announced EMP would reintroduce meat and fish (effective October 14, 2025) alongside its plant-based menu, citing business pressure and personal reflection.

**No confirmed quote exists** of Humm linking the new restaurant to the EMP reversal as a "reinvention." Any such connection is JustPreneur's own editorial interpretation, explicitly labeled as such (Slide 6, caption). Both restaurants operate concurrently — EMP is not replaced or deprioritized. ## Final Caption

Daniel Humm isn't undoing the plant-based era at Eleven Madison Park. He's building a new restaurant instead.

In 2021, EMP went fully plant-based — a bold bet that reporting suggests split even some of its longtime guests. In August 2025, Humm announced meat and fish would return to the menu starting October 14, 2025, citing business realities and personal reflection.

Around that same window: a second restaurant. Humm opens September 22, 2026 at 435 Hudson Street in the West Village — an entirely à la carte, modern American concept built around a wood-fired hearth, with a wine program from Michael Duffy featuring Mediterranean bottles. The space was designed with Pierre Yovanovitch, with a ceiling mural by Francesco Clemente.

Here's our read: the correction didn't happen inside EMP. It happened in a new address. EMP and Humm now run concurrently — one isn't replacing the other.

The lesson for founders: when a core bet stops serving your business, you don't always need to rewrite the original. Sometimes the smarter move is building a new vehicle — one that protects what you already built while still meeting the market where it's moved.

Save this if you're forcing one product to carry two different audiences.
