## Facts Summary (Approved Facts Block, cleared with qualifications by Fact Verifier)

Nu (Nubank) founder and CEO David Vélez — a Medellín-born entrepreneur often described as leading the world's largest digital bank — announced at the Latam Fintech Market 2026 conference in Barranquilla (September 17–18, 2026) that Nu Colombia plans to invest approximately COP $5 trillion ("5 billones de pesos") — roughly US$1.5–1.6 billion at prevailing 2026 exchange rates — in Colombia between 2026 and 2030. Vélez publicly called Colombia's legal interest-rate cap ("tasa de usura") "the greatest obstacle to financial inclusion in Colombia" and urged its elimination or restructuring, arguing (his own claim, not independently audited data) that the cap leaves roughly 80% of Colombians without real access to formal bank credit and pushes them toward informal "gota a gota" lenders, where he says rates can reach 500%–2,000% annually. Separately, a cited 2025 ANIF/Colombia Fintech report found more than 65% of Colombian adults lacked formal credit — a distinct, independently-sourced figure that must never be merged with Vélez's own estimate. On September 18, 2026, newly inaugurated President Abelardo de la Espriella (in office since August 7, 2026, with Vice President José Manuel Restrepo) named Vélez as an unpaid ("ad honorem") principal adviser to lead an artificial-intelligence and state-digital-transformation strategy — a role separate from financial regulation, but one that places Vélez inside the same administration he is now publicly pressuring on the usury-cap issue. ANIF has separately warned that fully eliminating the cap would also remove its associated criminal penalty, reducing legal protection against predatory informal lending. Around September 22, 2026, in a separate event in New York (Council of the Americas), Vice President Restrepo presented Colombia's broader ~US$150 billion, 176-project investment roadshow — a distinct occasion from Vélez's Barranquilla remarks, not a joint appearance.

## Final Caption

David Vélez, founder and CEO of Nu (Nubank) — often described as leading the world's largest digital bank — just committed roughly COP $5 trillion (~US$1.5–1.6B) to Colombia through 2030.

That's the headline. It's not the story.

At the Latam Fintech Market 2026 conference in Barranquilla (Sept 17–18), Vélez also called Colombia's legal interest-rate cap "the greatest obstacle to financial inclusion in Colombia," arguing — by his own estimate, not independently audited — that it locks ~80% of Colombians out of formal credit and pushes them toward informal "gota a gota" lenders charging 500%–2,000% annually. (For context: a separate 2025 ANIF/Colombia Fintech report put the share of adults without formal credit at 65%+ — a distinct, independently sourced figure.)

One day later, in a separate move, President Abelardo de la Espriella named Vélez an unpaid ("ad honorem") principal adviser to lead Colombia's AI and state-digital-transformation strategy — a role outside financial regulation, but one that places him inside the same government he's now publicly pressuring on the usury cap.

Worth noting for balance: ANIF has warned that fully removing the cap would also strip its criminal penalty, weakening protection against predatory lending.

(Separately, around Sept 22 in New York, VP José Manuel Restrepo presented Colombia's broader ~$150B, 176-project investment roadshow at the Council of the Americas — a different event, different city, same general week.)

The lesson isn't the capital commitment. It's that Vélez built himself proximity to power at the exact moment he needed it — and he didn't charge for the seat.
