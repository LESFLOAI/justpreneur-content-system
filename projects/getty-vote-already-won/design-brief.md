## 1. Overall Creative Concept

Concept: **"Sealed Before the Curtain Opens."** A single recurring glyph — an abstract wax-seal/stamp mark — anchors the cover and reappears, transformed, through the deck. On the cover it is shown fully pressed and closed, sitting in front of a still-closed curtain-fold texture: the decision is already sealed before the public "show" (the bankruptcy announcement) ever opens. On the private-deal slides (2–4) the same closed, solid-edged visual register continues — signature lines, an ownership key, a ledger checkmark — all rendered crisp and locked-down, because these events were already final. On the backstory slide (5) the register shifts to a forked-path motif, visualizing the regulatory fork that led here. On the public-record slides (6–7) the curtain motif reopens — bright, documentary, matter-of-fact — because this is the part everyone was finally allowed to see. Slide 6 specifically breaks from any "closed/calm" visual comfort: it is rendered in a deliberately muted, gold-free palette so the shareholder wipeout never reads as a minor footnote.

A second, text-based device carries the same "private vs. public" thesis without relying on color alone: every slide 2–7 carries a short status-tag pill (solid pill = settled/confirmed register; dashed pill = causal backstory) printed with the literal words "PRIVATE," "BACKSTORY," or "PUBLIC RECORD" plus a date where relevant — so the distinction between what happened behind closed doors and what the public timeline shows is legible even to a fast-swiping thumb, and even in grayscale.

No logos (Getty Images, Koch Industries/Koch Equity Development, Shutterstock, NYSE), no courthouse signage, no photographic likeness of any named executive, and no real building renderings appear anywhere. All corporate, legal, and financial concepts are rendered through abstract geometric motifs only — consistent with JustPreneur's existing policy on named real companies.

## 4. Typography Direction

- **Headline / display type:** a bold, modern grotesque or geometric sans at display weight (e.g., Inter Bold / Neue Haas Grotesk Bold / Söhne Breit as stylistic reference) — confident and editorial, consistent with financial-media headline treatment. Used for Slide 1's headline and Slide 8's pull-quote opening line.
- **Body / supporting copy:** a clean grotesque sans at regular-to-medium weight (same family as headline, lighter weight), for all body lines on Slides 2–8.
- **Status tag text:** small caps or all-caps, letter-spaced (+40–60 tracking), smallest text in the deck but never below the accessibility floor in Section 9 — a label, not a body line, so it must stay short (2–4 words plus an optional date) and never carry essential information alone.
- **Slide 5 (densest slide) hierarchy — three strict tiers, enforced to protect mobile readability:**
  - Tier 1 (bold, largest body size): "July 2026: Getty walks from a $3.7B merger with Shutterstock."
  - Tier 2 (medium weight, one step down): "The UK regulator would only clear it if Shutterstock sold its editorial business. Getty said no. The US DOJ had already cleared the deal, no conditions."
  - Tier 3 (lighter weight or steel-gray, smallest body size but never below the body floor): "Cost of the chase: $100M+ in sunk professional and financing fees over 18+ months." Do not label this a "breakup fee" anywhere in layout — the approved copy is explicit that this figure is sunk costs, not a breakup fee.
  - Generous line spacing (≥1.5x) between tiers on this slide specifically, since it carries the most information in the deck.
- **Slide 6 (shareholder wipeout) copy treatment:** render every line at full body weight and full warm-white contrast — do not step any line down to the lighter "caveat" treatment used elsewhere in the deck. This is a stated fact with real consequence, not a qualifier, and must never visually read as less important than the slides around it.
- **Minimum sizes:** headline ≥ 64px effective at 1080px width; body copy ≥ 40px effective; status tag text ≥ 28px effective. Err larger — this is a swipe-and-glance medium.
- **Line length:** keep headline lines to short mobile-friendly wraps (aim ≤ 32 characters per line at display scale); body copy wraps naturally but should avoid orphaned single words, especially on Slide 5.

## 9. Accessibility and Contrast Check

- Warm white `#F5F1E8` text on deep navy `#0B1A2B`: contrast ratio ≈ 13–14:1, comfortably exceeds WCAG AAA (7:1) for body and headline text at all sizes used.
- Steel gray `#5A6472` on navy: lower-contrast by design for the Slide 5 tier-3 line only — confirm it sits at ≥ 40px effective mobile size and is always paired with full-contrast warm-white copy nearby (the tier-1 and tier-2 lines above it), never the sole carrier of essential meaning. Slide 6 does NOT use this lower-contrast treatment for any line (see Section 4) — every Slide 6 line stays at full warm-white contrast.
- Energy gold `#D9A441` on navy: ≈ 6.5–7:1, meets AA and is near AAA — acceptable for tag-pill borders/text and the seal motif, not for long body paragraphs.
- The private/backstory/public distinction is never color-dependent alone: every tagged slide carries the printed word ("PRIVATE," "BACKSTORY," "PUBLIC") plus a distinct pill fill/outline treatment (solid vs. dashed) and a distinct border color (gold vs. steel) — redundant signals, so a colorblind reader or a reader on a low-color-fidelity screen still gets the distinction from text and shape alone.
- Minimum type sizes in Section 4 ensure legibility at typical in-feed mobile viewing scale (~350–400px effective display width).
- Specific dignity/accessibility check for Slide 6: because this slide intentionally drops the gold accent, double-check in finishing that the warm-white-on-navy contrast and type size are, if anything, slightly more generous than the deck average — the muted palette must never be allowed to read as "muted importance."
