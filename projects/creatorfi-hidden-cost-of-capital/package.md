## Facts Summary (Approved Facts Block, cleared with qualifications by Fact Verifier)

CreatorFi, a New York-based fintech company founded by Billy Huang (Co-Founder and CEO), announced on September 2, 2026 that it had secured $45 million in combined debt and equity financing, with capacity to scale to as much as $100 million more. The equity portion was led by EV3 (Escape Velocity), with participation from Uncorrelated Ventures, Protagonist, and other investors including Aptos Foundation, Pluto 11.11, and P2 Ventures, alongside angel investors described as finance and media executives from firms including State Street, J.P. Morgan Chase, and Periscope. The debt portion was led by VerisFi Capital as senior lender, with Intrinsic Capital and Kamui Finance providing mezzanine capital.

CreatorFi advances capital — typically in amounts from $500,000 to $5 million per deal — against a customer's recurring platform income (such as YouTube AdSense, Spotify and streaming royalties, TikTok Shop sales, and Roblox/Fortnite in-game earnings), repaid out of that same revenue stream, rather than taking an equity stake or requiring creators to give up their IP.

CreatorFi originated from Insomnia Labs, which raised $12 million and launched CreatorFi in July 2025 as a stablecoin-based credit product built on the Avalanche blockchain; CreatorFi took an additional $2 million strategic investment from Aptos Foundation in November 2025 to expand stablecoin credit infrastructure. The September 2026 $45 million round is a separate, larger, subsequent financing event — not a re-announcement of the earlier $12 million facility — though it shows continuity with CreatorFi's crypto-native investor and lender base (e.g., Kamui Finance and Aptos Foundation both recur). Public-facing framing of the September 2026 raise emphasizes "independent media" and revenue-based financing rather than crypto/stablecoin branding, but the underlying blockchain-based infrastructure has not been reported as discontinued. This piece deliberately omits the crypto/blockchain origin story rather than risk mischaracterizing it — an editorial choice, not a factual gap.

**On the 50%/life-insurance detail:** According to interview commentary attributed to Billy Huang (not stated in CreatorFi's official press release), some CreatorFi financing deals involve the company taking roughly a 50% share of a customer's specified platform revenue, sometimes paired with an obligation to produce new IP (e.g., new music releases) by a deadline, and in cases involving significant key-person risk, CreatorFi may require the customer to carry a life insurance policy. This is always attributed on-slide to Huang/interview coverage in the copy below — never presented as verified from CreatorFi's own official materials, and never generalized as CreatorFi's universal deal structure.

## Final Caption

Capital access isn't the same as cheap capital.

CreatorFi just raised $45M in combined debt and equity (Sept 2, 2026) — with capacity to scale to as much as $100M more — to advance money against creators' recurring platform income: AdSense, streaming royalties, TikTok Shop, in-game earnings. No equity stake. No IP handover. On paper, that sounds like the founder-friendly option.

But per interview commentary from CEO Billy Huang (not CreatorFi's official materials), some deals carry a revenue share of roughly 50% — sometimes paired with a deadline to ship new IP, or a life insurance requirement when key-person risk is high.

None of that makes CreatorFi's model wrong. It makes the pitch incomplete unless you ask the follow-up questions yourself.

Before you take revenue-based financing — or any deal that markets itself as "no equity, no dilution" — calculate what you're actually giving up: what percentage, for how long, and under what conditions. The absence of a term sheet clause is not the absence of a cost.

Save this post as your reference framework for the next financing conversation you have.
