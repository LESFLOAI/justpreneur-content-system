**Slide 7 — The Reframe**
No equity ≠ cheap.

**Slide 8 — The Framework: Before You Sign**
Ask four questions:
1. What % of revenue am I giving up?
2. For how long — until repaid, or indefinitely?
3. What obligations come attached (deadlines, insurance, exclusivity)?
4. What happens if I miss a target?

## 5. Final Caption

Capital access isn't the same as cheap capital.

CreatorFi just raised $45M in combined debt and equity (Sept 2, 2026) — with capacity to scale to as much as $100M more — to advance money against creators' recurring platform income: AdSense, streaming royalties, TikTok Shop, in-game earnings. No equity stake. No IP handover. On paper, that sounds like the founder-friendly option.

But per interview commentary from CEO Billy Huang (not CreatorFi's official materials), some deals carry a revenue share of roughly 50% — sometimes paired with a deadline to ship new IP, or a life insurance requirement when key-person risk is high.

None of that makes CreatorFi's model wrong. It makes the pitch incomplete unless you ask the follow-up questions yourself.

Before you take revenue-based financing — or any deal that markets itself as "no equity, no dilution" — calculate what you're actually giving up: what percentage, for how long, and under what conditions. The absence of a term sheet clause is not the absence of a cost.

Save this post as your reference framework for the next financing conversation you have.
