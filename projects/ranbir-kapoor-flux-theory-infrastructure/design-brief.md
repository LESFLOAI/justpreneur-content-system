## 0. Hard constraints carried into every visual decision

These come directly from the assignment brief and Fact Verification, and override the general brand defaults wherever they conflict:

1. **No real-person likeness — of anyone.** Ranbir Kapoor, Ashni Biyani, and Advay Jhunjhunwala are all named real people. No prompt in this brief asks an image generator to render any of their faces or a recognizable likeness. "The face" side of the visual system is represented through anonymized rim-lit silhouette, pure stage-light/spotlight imagery, or abstract mirror/prism motifs — never a posed portrait a viewer could mistake for an attempt at Kapoor's likeness. This is a deepfake-avoidance rule, not a stylistic preference — flag it as non-negotiable to whoever runs generation locally.
2. **No real trademarks reproduced.** Flux Theory is a real, currently-shipping brand. No prompt asks for the Flux Theory logo, wordmark, or real bottle/label design. Any product shown is a generic, unbranded body-wash bottle silhouette — plain surface, no readable text, no logo. Likewise, no Amazon/Flipkart/Myntra/Zepto/Tira/Instamart/D2C-site logos, no BusinessToday/Storyboard18/Entrepreneur India mastheads — retail platforms are represented as abstract unbranded network nodes; press coverage is represented as generic unbranded press-clipping/column-rule texture, never a fabricated real outlet logo.
3. **Not a takedown — tone stays premium editorial, not tabloid.** This piece does not accuse Kapoor of wrongdoing and must not visually imply the brand is fake, cheap, or that he "did nothing." No gotcha iconography: no magnifying glass over a name, no red flags, no gavel/courtroom imagery, no crossed-out text, no smirking or mocking visual tone, no tabloid red/yellow color intrusions. The visual register throughout is calm, confident structural analysis — think a business-desk feature (Bloomberg Businessweek, The Ken, Fortune India), not a gossip-account callout graphic.
4. **No 1:1 pairing between the five film characters and the five product variants.** Barfi/Bunny/Jordan/Ayan/Shiva and Lover/Rebel/Dreamer/Sport/Sage must always render as **two independent parallel fives** — two separate columns/rows/lists that never connect via arrows, lines, numbering, or spatial alignment that would suggest character 1 = variant 1, etc. Where both lists appear near each other (Slide 2), keep a visible gap and asymmetric arrangement between them, and never present them as a two-column matched table.
5. **No fabricated data.** No source discloses Kapoor's equity stake, investment amount, or deal terms — do not visualize placeholder numbers, percentages, pie charts, or "deal terms" documents that would imply disclosed figures exist. Slide 3's visual should communicate "the label exists, the number doesn't" through absence (e.g., a clean press-style layout with a deliberately blank/redacted-feeling gap), not through invented statistics.
6. **No product-claim visuals.** Do not render Flux Theory's actual scent/ingredient claims or packaging copy; bottle silhouettes stay abstract and unlabeled.

## 1. Overall creative concept

The whole carousel runs on **one recurring visual system with two opposing "worlds,"** paying off the copy's central argument that "Founder" now has two layers — the face and the infrastructure:

- **Front-of-House World** (the face, the campaign): glossy, warm, cinematic spotlight — the visual register of a premium FMCG launch film. Rich amber-gold key light, soft haze, a mirror/prism motif standing in for "you contain multiple versions of yourself," anonymized silhouette rather than a likeness. This is deliberately *attractive* — the piece never undercuts it or makes it look fake, because the whole point is that the face and the campaign are real and well-made.
- **Back-of-House World** (the infrastructure): clean navy blueprint/schematic diagram treatment — thin gold linework on deep navy, grid, network nodes, exploded-diagram feel, like a page out of an operations engineering deck. Cool, precise, unglamorous by design, but never sloppy or "cheap-looking" — it should read as *impressive in its own right*, because the copy's thesis is that this layer is the one worth studying.

The carousel opens (Slide 1) and pivots at its thesis (Slide 6) by placing both worlds side-by-side in a single deliberate split composition — this is the visual signature of the whole piece and should feel unmistakably intentional, not like two mismatched templates. Slides 2–3 live entirely in the Front-of-House world (the visible campaign story, then the caveat about what isn't disclosed). Slides 4–5 live entirely in the Back-of-House world (the infrastructure that had to already exist, and who supplied it). Slide 7 resolves both worlds into a calm navy-gold JustPreneur brand field for the CTA.

This is modern business-media visual language — closer to a Bloomberg or Fortune India explainer graphic than a fan-page or gossip carousel.

## 2. Composition and focal hierarchy

- **Standard interior slides (2, 3, 4, 5):** top ~42–45% of the frame is clean negative space for kicker + body copy; the generated visual occupies the lower ~55–58%, full-bleed to the side edges. JustPreneur wordmark sits small and centered in the bottom safe margin on every slide.
- **Split slides (1 cover, 6 thesis):** frame divides into two visual halves along a clean seam (vertical seam preferred on Slide 1, horizontal seam preferred on Slide 6, to avoid the two split slides looking identical) — Front-of-House glossy half on one side, Back-of-House blueprint half on the other. Headline/kicker text sits in a clean band that bridges both halves without visually favoring one side over the other (no larger type over one half than the other) — the split itself is the point, not a hierarchy between the two.
- **CTA slide (7):** full-bleed calm navy-gold abstract field, no split, no literal bottle/diagram elements — a deliberate bookend of quiet resolution after six slides of contrast.

## 4. Typography direction

- **Headline/cover typeface:** bold, semicondensed grotesk (JustPreneur's standing family — keep consistent with prior carousels). Sentence case for the long cover headline and the Slide 6 thesis lines; short kicker words (THE VISIBLE STORY, THE CAVEAT, WHAT HAD TO EXIST FIRST, WHO SUPPLIED IT, THE REFRAME) in all-caps, letter-spaced, small size, gold.
- **Body copy:** warm white, regular weight, generous line-height. Slide 2, 3, and 6 run slightly longer paragraphs than a typical JustPreneur carousel (the copy itself is longer/denser here) — hold body type at a size that still clears mobile legibility (no smaller than roughly 34–36px cap-height equivalent at 1080px width) even if it means the copy block runs a touch taller into the visual zone; do not shrink type to fit.
- **List treatment (Slide 4):** the four infrastructure items set as a clean vertical list with a thin gold rule or dash bullet per line (not baked into the generated image) — mirrors the blueprint diagram's node labels without duplicating them.
- **Quoted term treatment:** the word "Founder" is set in a distinguishing treatment (e.g., quotation marks kept, optionally a subtly different weight) everywhere it appears in quotes in the copy (cover headline, Slide 1 hook, Slide 6) to consistently flag it as the term under examination — never presented as JustPreneur's own editorializing label.
- No script fonts, no tabloid-style condensed-bold headline treatment, no red "breaking news" banner styling.

## 5. Color palette

**Brand base (unchanged):** deep navy, steel gray, warm white, energy gold — used for all text, kickers, wordmark, and UI chrome throughout.

**Story-specific accent layer (background art only, not text):**
- **Front-of-House world:** warm amber-gold spotlight glow, soft haze, warm highlight on prism/glass surfaces — premium campaign-film lighting.
- **Back-of-House world:** deep navy blueprint field, thin energy-gold schematic linework, cool steel-gray structural nodes — engineering-diagram precision.
- Avoid: tabloid red/yellow, neon or saturated "hype" colors, any color combination that could read as a specific real retail-platform brand palette (e.g., Amazon's blue/orange, Flipkart's blue/yellow).

## 6. Image treatment

- Front-of-House visuals: cinematic, glossy, shallow depth of field, soft haze/bloom around light sources — the visual quality of an actual premium FMCG campaign still, minus any identifiable person or real product label.
- Back-of-House visuals: flat-lit, precise, high-contrast thin-line schematic rendering on a clean navy field — the visual quality of an operations/engineering deck page, not a cluttered infographic.
- No text baked into any generated image anywhere — all copy, kickers, and lists are added as type layers after generation.
- No fake logos of any kind (Flux Theory, Amazon, Flipkart, Myntra, Zepto, Tira, Instamart, Think9, or any press outlet), no watermarks, no small corner icons, no fabricated production slates or citation marks.
- No human faces or recognizable likenesses anywhere in the set — silhouette/light-only representation of "the face" throughout, per §0.1.

## 7. Instagram safe-margin instructions

**Carousel (1080x1350):** Minimum 72px margin on all sides for any text or wordmark. Interior slides (2–5): keep the top ~42–45% of the frame clean and low-detail for kicker + body copy. Split slides (1, 7's near-split treatment on the seam band, and 6): keep a clean band of at least 120px around the seam itself free of high-detail texture so kicker/body text reads clearly regardless of which half it overlaps. JustPreneur wordmark centered in the bottom safe margin on Slides 2–6 (small, consistent size/position); enlarged only on Slide 7 per §3.

## 8. Slide-to-slide consistency rules

- The Front-of-House glossy spotlight motif (Slides 2, 3, top-half of 1 and 6) must use the same light color temperature, haze density, and prism/mirror treatment throughout so it reads as one consistent "world," not four different photo styles.
- The Back-of-House blueprint motif (Slides 4, 5, bottom-half of 1 and 6) must use the same navy field, gold line-weight, and node-icon style throughout — Slide 5's hub diagram should look like a zoomed continuation of Slide 4's diagram, not a new diagram style.
- Kicker style (all-caps, gold, small, letter-spaced, same position) is identical across Slides 2–6.
- JustPreneur wordmark size/position/opacity is identical across Slides 2–6, then scales up only on Slide 7.
- The word "Founder" keeps the same quoted-term type treatment (§4) everywhere it appears.
- No slide introduces a visual element (icon style, color, texture) that doesn't recur or connect to the Front-of-House/Back-of-House system established on Slide 1.

## 9. Accessibility and contrast check

- Warm-white text (#F5F3EE-equivalent) on a navy scrim (#0B1E32-equivalent) at ≥70% opacity comfortably clears WCAG AA contrast for body-copy-size text at carousel scale.
- Gold kicker text (energy-gold, e.g., #D4A017-equivalent) is reserved for short, large kicker labels and the quoted "Founder" emphasis only — never for full paragraph body copy, since gold-on-navy body text at small sizes drops below comfortable mobile contrast.
- On Front-of-House slides (2, 3, top of 1/6), body copy sits on a navy scrim over the glossy visual, not directly on the amber haze, to protect contrast — the scrim should feel like a deliberate editorial treatment (soft vignette), not a flat box.
- On Back-of-House slides (4, 5, bottom of 1/6), body copy sits on the navy field directly (already high-contrast by design) with the blueprint linework kept clear of the text zone.
- Slides 2, 3, and 6 carry longer body copy than usual — cap max line length at roughly 34–38 characters per line at the chosen type size and confirm no paragraph exceeds 4–5 lines before it starts crowding the visual zone; if it does, allow the clean-space band to extend to 48–50% of the frame on that slide rather than shrinking type.
