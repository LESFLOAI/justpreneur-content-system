## Final Caption (QC corrections applied: chronology reworded, hedges restored)

Target just opened a new store in Bridgehampton — and left its own signature color at the door.

The ~90,000-sq-ft location sits in Bridgehampton Commons, in the space of what's widely reported as the last full-size Kmart in the continental U.S. (closed Oct. 20, 2024). A 2024 unscientific local poll by Greater Long Island had found 49% of readers wanted Target there — more than Walmart and Costco combined. It opened to the public Oct. 6, 2026, with an official grand-opening celebration on Oct. 11.

But there's no red bullseye out front. According to Target and local reporting, Southampton's design guidelines and review by the town's Architectural Review Board shaped a different look: gray shingles, white trim, gray bollards, and a white bullseye lit from above instead of glowing from within — built to fit Bridgehampton's character.

The name didn't change. The model didn't change. The logo's shape didn't change. What changed was color and light source — the parts of the brand that were negotiable — in exchange for access to a market that doesn't bend for anyone.

That's not a compromise. That's a strategic skill: knowing in advance what you'd trade for access, and what you'd never touch.

Before you expand into a market with its own rules — do you know which parts of your brand are fixed, and which are just decoration?

Save this. Then go audit your own brand's "non-negotiable."
