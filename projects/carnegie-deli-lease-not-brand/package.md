## Facts Summary (Approved Facts Block, cleared by Fact Verifier)

Carnegie Deli, the New York pastrami institution founded in 1937, reopened its Manhattan flagship on September 15, 2026 (a Tuesday) at 834 Seventh Avenue in Midtown — the former home of its one-time rival, the Stage Deli. The 110-seat restaurant is led by Sarri Harper, third-generation owner and CEO, who is the granddaughter of Milton Parker (who bought the deli with partners in 1976) and the daughter of Marian Harper Levine, who ran the business until its 2016 closure. The Manhattan flagship had been closed since December 31, 2016, when Marian Harper Levine announced the shutdown citing a combination of personal and legal difficulties, including a prior 2015 Con Edison illegal-gas-line dispute (which had already been resolved with a February 2016 reopening), an ongoing divorce, and lawsuits. During the roughly nine-and-a-half-year hiatus, the Harper family kept the Carnegie Deli brand alive through licensing deals, including counters at Madison Square Garden, The Mirage in Las Vegas (opened 2005), and Sands Casino Resort Bethlehem, PA (opened 2009), plus wholesale and packaged-product sales. Only the Madison Square Garden counter remains open today; the Mirage location closed around February 2020, and the Sands Bethlehem location closed at the end of 2017.

**Hard constraints honored in all copy below:** no "Con Edison forced the closure" framing; no implication the full licensing network is still active; Milton Parker never called "founder"; reopening date is September 15 (not the 16th); no implication that the 2016/Mirage/Sands closures were deliberate strategic triage.
