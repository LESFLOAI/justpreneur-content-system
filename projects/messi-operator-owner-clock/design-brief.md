## 1. Overall Creative Concept

The story is about a *structural rule*, not a celebrity moment — Messi is the hook, but the point is universal: you cannot hold the "operator" seat and the "owner" seat on the same clock until a defined transition point opens. The visual system should feel like premium sports-business editorial (think The Athletic's business desk, Sportico, or a Bloomberg sports-finance explainer) — not a fan-page graphic and not a generic motivational carousel.

**Important creative constraint:** This package deliberately avoids generating a literal, photorealistic likeness of Lionel Messi, any real Inter Miami crest/kit branding, or real MLS marks. Image models handle real-athlete likeness unreliably (distorted faces, refusals, legal/personality-rights exposure) and literal team logos risk trademark issues and "fake logo" clutter the brand guidelines prohibit. Instead, the series uses an **abstracted, unbranded athlete silhouette + stadium/boardroom environment + a recurring "two clocks / one gate" motif** to carry the story visually, while the copy (added as text, not as image-generated lettering) carries the specific facts, names, and dates. This keeps the design premium, legally safer, and far more reliable to actually generate.

**Recurring motif across all 8 slides: "Two Clocks, One Gate."**
A stylized stadium scoreboard/clock face is split into two overlapping dials — one labeled visually (via distinct color temperature and position, not literal text) as the *Operator Clock* (cool steel-blue, pitch-side, in motion) and one as the *Owner Clock* (warm gold, boardroom-side, stationary until triggered). Between them sits a glowing **gate / turnstile line** — the literal "transition point." Early slides show the gate closed with one clock dominant; later slides show the mechanism itself; the CTA slide shows the gate cracked open, inviting the reader to find their own trigger. This single mechanical image — two dials, one gate — repeats in different crops and lighting states across all 8 slides the same way a logo lockup would, giving the carousel a strong swipe-through identity without needing repeated text labels.

## 2. Composition and Focal Hierarchy

Every slide follows the same three-layer stack, in priority order:

1. **Headline / key line (top ~40% safe zone)** — largest, boldest text, warm white on a dark navy scrim, always legible at thumbnail size.
2. **Supporting copy (lower-middle third)** — smaller weight, steel-gray or warm-white, placed on a darker gradient scrim so it never sits directly on busy image detail.
2a. **JustPreneur wordmark** — small, consistent lockup, bottom-left corner on slides 1–7; moves to bottom-center (slightly larger, paired with the CTA) on slide 8, matching the brand's established pattern of a deliberate CTA-slide lockup shift.
3. **Imagery / motif (background, full bleed)** — supports but never competes with text; all generated images must leave a clear negative-space band (dark, low-detail) behind wherever headline/body text will land.

Focal hierarchy is always: **headline > motif gate/clock element > supporting copy > wordmark.** No slide should ask the eye to read fine imagery detail and dense body copy at the same time.

## 3. Exact Text, Imagery, and Branding Placement (per slide)

All slides: **1080 × 1350 px portrait.** Safe margins: minimum **72 px** from every edge; keep all critical text out of the bottom **150 px** (Instagram UI overlap zone) and top **40 px**.

### Slide 1 — Cover
- Headline (top, lines broken for mobile): **"Messi Can't Own The Team He Plays For — Yet."** — largest type on the carousel, 2–3 line break, centered or left-aligned in the top 40%, on a navy scrim over the stadium image.
- Subhead directly below, smaller weight: **"There's a rule behind that. It applies far beyond soccer."**
- Imagery: dusk stadium pitch, two-clock/gate motif glowing center-low, unbranded athlete silhouette mid-stride near the gate.
- JustPreneur wordmark: bottom-left, small, gold on navy chip.

### Slide 2 — The retirement that happened
- Copy block (upper-middle, on scrim): **"On Aug. 31, 2026, Lionel Messi retired from Argentina's national team. That announcement was about international play only."**
- Imagery: the "Operator Clock" side dimming/powering down — represents the international-play lane closing, club lane untouched.
- Wordmark bottom-left.

### Slide 3 — The retirement that didn't happen
- Copy block: **"He did not retire from club soccer. Messi remains an active Inter Miami player, reportedly under contract through 2028."**
- Imagery: the opposite clock face — pitch-side lane brightly lit, active, contrasting with slide 2's dimmed dial, visually paired as a matched set.
- Wordmark bottom-left.

### Slide 4 — The actual question
- Copy block: **"So why can't he own part of the team he plays for? Club officials say current MLS rules don't allow an active player to hold equity in a club."**
- Imagery: the full "two lanes, one gate" composition — operator lane (pitch, cool light) and owner lane (boardroom suite, gold light) shown side by side, separated by the glowing closed gate.
- Wordmark bottom-left.

### Slide 5 — Where this comes from
- Copy block: **"Inter Miami's chief business officer, Xavier Asensi, told Marca in an October 2026 interview: Messi is expected to join the club's ownership group once he's no longer an active player — not before."**
- Imagery: an owner's-box / glass boardroom overlooking the pitch at dusk, a single generic unbranded executive silhouette gesturing toward the gate below — this slide sources the claim, so it should feel like an interview/office setting rather than the pitch.
- Wordmark bottom-left.

### Slide 6 — The reframe
- Copy block (can run larger/bolder — this is the thesis line): **"Not merit. Not money. Timing. You can't be the operator and the owner on the same clock — not until a defined transition point."**
- Imagery: tight macro-style shot of the gate/turnstile mechanism itself, the two clock hands clearly offset from one another — this is the "mechanism reveal" slide, most abstract and graphic of the set.
- Wordmark bottom-left.

### Slide 7 — Final Takeaway
- Copy block: **"Every operator building toward equity runs into this same structure somewhere. The work clock and the ownership clock often don't run together — until something specific triggers the switch."**
- Imagery: wide pull-back shot — multiple generic, diverse, unbranded professional silhouettes each standing at their own small gate across a gridded plaza/field, generalizing the motif beyond soccer into business broadly.
- Wordmark bottom-left.

### Slide 8 — Call to Action
- Copy block: **"Before you build toward equity in something you operate — find the actual trigger language in your deal."**
- CTA line, visually distinct (bolder/boxed): **"Then comment: where are you right now — operator clock, or owner clock?"**
- Imagery: single gate/turnstile cracked open just slightly, warm gold light spilling through — inviting, hopeful, generous clean negative space reserved for the two text blocks.
- JustPreneur wordmark: bottom-center, slightly larger CTA lockup (deviation from slides 1–7, consistent with brand pattern of emphasizing the handle on the final slide).

## 4. Typography Direction

- **Headlines:** Bold geometric sans, heavy weight — Montserrat ExtraBold / Black, Clash Display, or Archivo Black. Tight letter-spacing, generous leading between wrapped lines so the headline never feels cramped on a phone screen.
- **Supporting body copy:** Clean humanist sans at medium weight — Inter, Work Sans, or HK Grotesk Regular/Medium — for maximum legibility at small size.
- **Accent/number treatment:** Where years or clock-style numerals appear in design (e.g., any small scoreboard-style digit treatment within the motif graphic itself), use a digital-scoreboard or monospace numeral style (e.g., Space Mono, DSEG-style) strictly as a *graphic texture element inside the generated image* — never as the primary readable copy, which is always set with the design tool afterward, not generated as image text.
- All actual slide copy (headline, subhead, body, CTA) is added with a design tool after image generation — never relied upon from the image model.

---

## 5. Color Palette

- **Deep navy** `#0B1B2B` — primary background / scrim base, "operator clock" cool tone anchor.
- **Steel gray** `#8A94A6` — secondary text, dial rims, pitch shadow tones.
- **Warm white** `#F5F1E8` — headline type, key highlight light.
- **Energy gold** `#C9A227` (richer `#D4AF37` for the gate-light glow and "owner clock" dial) — the ownership/equity signal color throughout; used sparingly and consistently so the eye learns "gold = ownership side" across the carousel.
- **Secondary accent (restrained):** a desaturated coral/pink glow (`#E8768A`, used only as a faint rim-light or floodlight-color cue on pitch scenes) — a subtle nod to Inter Miami's real-world pink identity without using their logo, wordmark, or exact brand hex, keeping it a background mood cue rather than a trademark reference.

---

## 6. Image Treatment

- Cinematic dusk/night stadium lighting across the pitch-set slides (1, 2, 3, 4, 7); warm interior gold lighting for the boardroom/owner's-box slide (5); tight graphic/product-shot lighting for the mechanism slide (6); warm inviting glow for the CTA gate (8).
- Consistent lens language: slightly low, dramatic angles for pitch shots; clean architectural symmetry for the boardroom shot; macro/graphic framing for the mechanism shot.
- Desaturated, premium color grade — avoid saturated "sports poster" clichés (no lens flare bursts, no confetti, no trophy/medal imagery).
- No literal faces rendered in detail — all human figures are silhouettes, rim-lit or backlit, generic build, unbranded clothing, no visible team crest, no jersey numbers, no readable signage.
- Every image must include a clear low-detail negative-space zone (dark gradient, minimal texture) sized and positioned per the per-slide placement notes above, so text overlays stay legible.

---

## 7. Instagram Safe-Margin Instructions

- Canvas: 1080 × 1350 px for all 8 slides.
- Minimum 72 px clear margin on all four edges — no text or critical motif detail crosses this line.
- Keep all text out of the bottom 150 px (profile/caption UI overlap) and top 40 px (notification bar overlap on some devices).
- Headline block must sit fully within the top 40% of the frame on every slide so it reads at Instagram's carousel-grid thumbnail size.
- Wordmark lockup stays within the bottom-left 72 px margin box (slides 1–7) or bottom-center 150 px-high band (slide 8) — never overlapping body copy.

---

## 8. Slide-to-Slide Consistency Rules

- The **two-clocks/gate motif** must appear, in some crop or state, on every one of the 8 slides — this is the carousel's visual "logo" and the primary swipe-through recognition cue.
- Color logic stays fixed: **cool steel-blue/navy = operator/active-play lane; warm gold = owner/equity lane** — never swapped slide to slide.
- Scrim treatment (dark gradient behind text) must match in darkness/opacity across all 8 slides so the carousel doesn't flicker in brightness as the user swipes.
- Wordmark position, size, and treatment stay identical on slides 1–7; the single intentional deviation is slide 8's bottom-center CTA lockup.
- No slide introduces a new color, a literal team crest, a literal player likeness, or a new unrelated motif — every slide is a variation on the same stadium/boardroom/gate world.

---

## 9. Accessibility and Contrast Check

- Headline text (warm white `#F5F1E8`) on navy scrim (`#0B1B2B` at ~70–80% opacity over the image) targets a contrast ratio well above WCAG AA (4.5:1) for body text and comfortably exceeds the large-text AA threshold (3:1) for headlines.
- Supporting copy (warm white or light steel-gray) stays on the same dark scrim band, never directly on busy image texture — contrast is controlled by the design tool's scrim layer, not by the generated image's natural brightness.
- Gold accents are used only for motif/graphic elements and short emphasis words, never for full body-copy paragraphs, since gold-on-navy body text would fail contrast at small sizes.
- No color-only meaning: the operator/owner distinction is reinforced by *position* (pitch vs. boardroom, left lane vs. right lane) as well as by color, so the story still reads for color-blind viewers.
- Type sizes: headline minimum ~60–72pt equivalent at 1080px width; body copy minimum ~36–40pt equivalent — both well above comfortable mobile-thumbnail legibility thresholds.
