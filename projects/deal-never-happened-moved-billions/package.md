## 1. Facts Summary (Approved Facts Block, verbatim from Fact Verifier)

> On October 8, 2026, the Financial Times reported — citing people familiar with the matter — that Starbucks had worked with outside advisers in recent months on a possible takeover proposal for Chipotle Mexican Grill. The FT said it was unclear whether a formal offer had been made or whether any deal would proceed. The rumor drew attention in part because Starbucks CEO Brian Niccol previously served as Chipotle's chairman and CEO from March 2018 until August 31, 2024 — roughly six years — before being named Starbucks' CEO on August 13, 2024, and starting in that role on September 9, 2024.
>
> On Monday, October 12, 2026, a Starbucks spokesperson told reporters, "As a matter of policy, we don't comment on rumors and speculation, but we want to be clear we are not buying Chipotle," adding that the company remains focused on its "Back to Starbucks" turnaround plan. Chipotle did not publicly comment on the rumor or the denial.
>
> Following the October 8 report, Chipotle shares rose and Starbucks shares fell; reported figures vary by source and by intraday-vs-closing timing, with Chipotle commonly cited as up roughly 6%-7% and Starbucks down roughly 3% (some intraday reports showed larger swings in both directions). After the October 12 denial, the moves partially reversed: Chipotle was commonly reported down roughly 3%-4% and Starbucks up roughly 1%. These figures should always be attributed to a specific outlet and presented as approximate, not exact.
>
> Some analysts and commentators noted that a completed Starbucks-Chipotle deal would have surpassed Burger King's 2014 acquisition of Tim Hortons (valued at roughly $11 billion) as the largest restaurant-industry M&A transaction on record — this is a hypothetical comparison only, since no deal occurred. Chipotle's market capitalization at the time of the report was commonly cited near $39 billion, though published figures ranged from roughly $38 billion to $42 billion depending on timing and methodology; present as an approximate, attributed figure. One analyst (D.A. Davidson's Matt Curtis) was reported as putting the odds of a completed deal at around 20% before the denial — an attributed opinion, not fact.
>
> No evidence a formal offer was ever made or that either company's board considered a deal; talks (if they happened at all) were described as banker-driven and never advanced to a formal offer. Separately, Chipotle had reportedly hired its own advisers earlier in October, possibly related to defending against activist investors — a separate thread not used in this package.

**Mandatory hedges honored throughout this package:**
1. The deal is never framed as real, near-complete, or board-considered — every slide/caption reinforces "no offer, no deal, rumor-and-denial."
2. Stock-move figures are always ranges with "roughly"/"reported," never single exact percentages.
3. "Largest restaurant M&A" / Chipotle's ~$39B valuation are presented only as hypothetical, attributed comparisons — never stated as fact. (Neither figure appears on-slide; the comparison was deliberately left out of visible copy to avoid hype risk.)
4. The denial quote is attributed to "a Starbucks spokesperson," never to Niccol or any named individual, and is reproduced in full, exact wording (QC caught and fixed a dropped opening clause — see Section 9).
5. Niccol's career timeline (Chipotle Mar 2018–Aug 31, 2024; named Starbucks CEO Aug 13, 2024; started Sep 9, 2024) stated exactly, never rounded or altered.
6. Caption's claim of "four trading days" was factually wrong (only three U.S. trading days separate Oct 8 and Oct 12) and was corrected by QC to "within days."
7. The analyst's ~20%-odds figure is presented as a reported opinion, not fact.
8. No Starbucks/Chipotle logos, storefronts, or any likeness of Brian Niccol appear anywhere in the visual design — abstract motifs only.

## 7. Visual Design Brief Summary

Financial/business-media editorial register (Bloomberg/Axios/The Information-adjacent), not a generic motivational template. Core concept: "The Record vs. The Rumor" — a recurring top-center status-tag pill on Slides 2–6 explicitly labels each slide's claim type (VERIFIED RECORD, REPORTED — UNCONFIRMED, MARKET REACTION, ON THE RECORD), paired with a second nonverbal signal: edge quality. Verified/on-record slides render crisp and sharp; the one rumor slide (Slide 3) renders with a deliberate haze/blur vignette and dashed-outline tag — visually "unconfirmed" before it's even read. Stock-reaction slides (4, 6) use neutral abstract up/down chart motifs with no numerals baked in. No Starbucks/Chipotle logos, storefronts, or any likeness of Brian Niccol anywhere — all motifs are abstract/geometric (corporate-tower silhouettes, career-timeline steps, quotation-mark glyph, balance scale, bookmark ribbon). Palette: deep navy, steel gray, warm white, energy gold; no red/green used for stock direction — direction is always carried by words. All exact text (headline, body lines, the denial quote, approximate ranges, status-tag words/dates) is added in a layout/finishing pass with real typography, never relied upon from the image model.
