## 1. Approved Facts Summary

On October 7, 2026, Tishman Speyer and The Cooper Union announced they had finalized a 150-year ground lease agreement covering the Chrysler Building (405 Lexington Avenue, Manhattan), with the Public Sector Pension Investment Board (PSP Investments) named as lead co-investor among other institutional partners. The companies state the deal and planned restoration will be financed with $235 million in equity, with no new debt — a claim attributed to the companies' own statements, not independently audited.

Cooper Union, a nonprofit educational institution, has owned the underlying land since the early 1900s. The new lease follows the eviction of prior lessee RFR Holding (with Signa), which fell more than $21 million behind on ground rent; Cooper Union regained control through court action in 2024–2025 and put the leasehold back on the market in May 2025. Tishman Speyer previously held the Chrysler Building's ground lease from 1997 until fully divesting by 2019.

Planned restoration work includes the building's Art Deco facade and crown, plus modernization of mechanical, electrical, elevator, and air-handling systems. Announced new amenity space includes a 61st-floor indoor/outdoor lounge with food-and-beverage service and a terrace reported as open to all building tenants, plus a separate fitness/wellness/meeting amenity suite in the underground arcade. New ground-rent payment terms under the lease have not been disclosed.

**Qualifications carried through this entire package (do not drop in finishing):**
- This is a **ground lease**, not a sale — never call Tishman Speyer the "buyer" or "owner."
- "$235M equity, no new debt" is **company-stated**, not independently audited.
- The deal is **signed/announced/finalized** — never say "closed."
- **NY Attorney General approval status is unconfirmed** and is omitted entirely from this package by design.
- Only the confirmed **$21M+ back-rent arrears** figure is used — no invented/old RFR-era rent terms are presented as the new deal's terms.

- 2019 exit is described without asserting an unconfirmed "voluntary, no default, no scandal" characterization (corrected per QC below).
