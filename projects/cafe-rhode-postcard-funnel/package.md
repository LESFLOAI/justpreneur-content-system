## Story / Facts Summary

Rhode — the skincare and color cosmetics brand founded by Hailey Rhode Bieber and owned by e.l.f. Beauty — launched at Sephora across continental Europe on September 30, 2026, becoming available online and in more than 500 Sephora stores across 19 markets (Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Czech Republic, Denmark, France, Germany, Greece, Italy, Luxembourg, Monaco, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Serbia, Spain including the Canary Islands, Sweden, Switzerland, and Turkey). This followed Rhode's earlier staggered Sephora rollouts in the US and Canada (September 4, 2025) and the UK (November 10, 2025), after roughly three years as a direct-to-consumer-only brand (founded June 2022). The brand's official stylization is lowercase, "rhode."

To mark the Europe launch, trade press (Cosmetics Business, Mercado Negro, and lareclame.fr/PremiumBeautyNews) reported that Rhode opened "Café Rhode," a reservation-based pop-up bistro on Rue Bonaparte in Paris's 6th arrondissement, running September 30–October 4, 2026. Per these reports, guests received a branded postcard and "metro card" that could be presented at a participating Paris Sephora location (reportedly including Champs-Élysées) for a skincare sample with purchase, while supplies lasted.

**Not asserted anywhere in this package:** an unqualified "first-ever European retail presence" (UK, launched Nov 2025, is also Europe — correctly scoped to "first rollout across continental Europe"); any Europe-specific sales/performance figures (none exist, none used); a flat, undisputed US sales figure (the disputed $10M/2-day vs. $15M/24h stat was deliberately left out of the published copy entirely); any direct/verbatim quote from Hailey Bieber (none are confirmed verbatim; none used); the Café Rhode pop-up as company-confirmed (it is attributed to named trade press throughout, on every slide that mentions it and in the caption).

## Angle

**"The Pop-Up Had a Job to Do"** — A pop-up is not a marketing expense if it's built like a funnel. Café Rhode's reported postcard/metro-card-to-Sephora mechanic shows how to turn a short-lived physical activation into trackable, revenue-channel foot traffic — a discipline most brand experiences skip in favor of pure aesthetics. Primary takeaway: design the redemption mechanic before you design the aesthetic. This angle deliberately avoids funding/valuation and M&A/ownership-control framing (e.l.f. Beauty's ownership of Rhode is background only) and does not reuse any of the ~19 angle families already on cooldown from the last two weeks of the pipeline's output.
