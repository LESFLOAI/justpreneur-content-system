## 0. Hard constraints carried into every visual decision

These override the general brand defaults wherever they conflict. All come from the approved copy's own attribution and trademark exposure, not from new restrictions I'm inventing:

1. **No real-person likenesses.** Hailey Bieber is a named public figure (rhode's founder, tagged account). No prompt asks an image generator to render her face or a recognizable likeness. Any human presence in generated art is hands/forearms only, fully anonymized, or omitted entirely.
2. **No fabricated rhode or Sephora logos, wordmarks, or packaging.** rhode's wordmark, its signature phone-case-style product bottle silhouette, and Sephora's name/logo/signature striped branding are real trademarks. Every prompt below specifies generic, unbranded minimalist skincare-bottle silhouettes and generic, unbranded retail environments — no readable text, no logo marks, no reproduced label design.
3. **No overclaiming unconfirmed details as documentary photography.** Slides 3 and 4's copy is explicitly attributed to trade press ("not company-confirmed"). The matching visuals (bg-carousel-3, bg-carousel-4) are written as atmospheric/conceptual renderings of "a Paris café pop-up" and "a postcard redemption mechanic" in general — not as a purported real photo of the actual event. This keeps the image honest about what it is: an editorial illustration of the story, not proof of it.
4. **No readable postcard/metro-card text.** The postcard and "metro card" motifs appear as blank, unmarked, or softly patterned cards — no rhode wordmark, no real Paris transit-system (RATP) branding, no invented copy. Exact wording, if any is ever added to these shapes, is a type-layer decision made later by a human, not something the image generator renders.
5. **No Eiffel-Tower-postcard cliché.** Lean into Rue Bonaparte's real 6th-arrondissement café-row character — narrow stone facades, café awnings, bistro chairs, warm morning light — rather than generic tourist-postcard Paris landmarks. This matches the actual boutique, insider aesthetic the story describes, not a souvenir-shop look.

## 1. Overall creative concept

The story's thesis is a reframe: everyone noticed the pretty café, almost no one noticed the mechanic hiding inside it. The visual system should mirror that reveal — open on café-world beauty, then let a recurring **blank postcard/metro-card motif** quietly take over the frame as the slides progress, so the viewer's eye gets pulled from "nice room" to "the thing that actually moves money" exactly as the copy does.

Visual language: **modern business/culture editorial** — think a trade-press feature on retail strategy (Business of Fashion, Glossy) crossed with a Paris lifestyle shoot, not a generic motivational-quote template and not a rhode ad. Clean, warm, minimal clutter, confident negative space — rhode's own minimalist aesthetic is a legitimate reference point for mood and restraint, but the piece stays in JustPreneur's own navy/gold/warm-white editorial system, not a rhode-branded pastel world.

Two recurring visual threads, set in deliberate contrast (same technique as prior JustPreneur carousels):
- **The Café World** — warm, textured, inviting: cream stone facades, marble café tables, rattan chairs, soft Parisian morning light. Carries the "pretty room" / hook beats.
- **The Card World** — a single blank postcard/metro-card object, lit cleanly and deliberately, almost product-shot in its stillness. Carries the "mechanic" / point beats.

The piece resolves on the JustPreneur wordmark with the blank card motif glowing softly in gold-edged light — pulling the recurring object back into brand territory for the CTA.

## 4. Typography direction

- **Headline/Header typeface:** bold, condensed-to-semicondensed grotesk (JustPreneur's established carousel family — keep consistent with prior posts). Sentence case, not all-caps.
- **Slide 1 headline:** two-line centered set, generous leading, cover-weight size.
- **Interior Headers (Slides 2–6):** bold, large, sits directly above its Body copy, left-aligned within the safe margin (matches prior JustPreneur layouts better than centered for longer header lines like these).
- **Body copy:** warm white, regular weight, generous line-height, max 3 lines per slide given the longer attribution-qualified sentences on Slides 2–4 — size down slightly from prior shorter-body carousels if needed to keep every word intact without paraphrasing, but never below comfortable mobile legibility (see §9).
- **CTA slide (7):** Header bold, Body regular, same family, no special treatment beyond what's used on Slides 2–6.
- No script fonts, no decorative "café menu" chalkboard lettering — despite the Paris/café subject, avoid bistro-chalkboard or French-script cliché type. Stay in JustPreneur's own premium-editorial voice.

## 5. Color palette

**Brand base (unchanged):** deep navy, steel gray, warm white, energy gold — used for all text, wordmark, and UI chrome across every slide.

**Story-specific accent layer (background art only, never text):**
- Warm cream / sand / aged-stone — Paris café facades, marble tabletops (the "Café World")
- Muted terracotta / dusty rose — café awning and light accents, used sparingly, never as a dominant color
- Soft dove gray — card stock, marble veining, keeps the "Card World" feeling clean and deliberate rather than warm/inviting (a quiet contrast to the café world, echoing the copy's "hook vs. point" reframe)
- A restrained touch of rhode-adjacent "glazed" pastel (soft milky blush/putty) is allowed only as a tiny accent within the blank postcard's edge-lighting on Slides 4, 6, and 7 — never enough to read as a rhode-branded palette takeover
- Avoid: any reproduction of rhode's actual brand palette as the *dominant* story palette, any black-and-white striped motif that could be read as a direct Sephora trademark reference

## 8. Slide-to-slide consistency rules

- Every slide uses the same top-45%-clean / bottom-visual convention, the same grotesk type family, and the same JustPreneur wordmark size/position (bottom-center safe margin) — no switching mid-carousel.
- The Café World ↔ Card World visual contrast (warm cream/terracotta vs. clean dove-gray/card-stock) must stay consistent every time each world reappears (Slides 1, 3, 5 vs. Slides 4, 6, 7), so a viewer recognizes which "world" they're in without rereading the text.
- Slide 2's retail-world visual is a deliberate third beat (neither full Café World nor full Card World) — it should feel like a clean bridge between them: more neutral/premium-retail than either extreme.
- Header type size and weight stay identical across all 6 non-cover slides; Body type size stays identical across Slides 2–7.

## 9. Accessibility and contrast check

- Warm-white text (~#F5F3EE-equivalent) on a navy scrim (~#0B1E32-equivalent) at ≥70% scrim opacity clears WCAG AA contrast for large text at carousel scale.
- Gold is reserved for short emphasis only (e.g., isolating "a postcard" or "the register" within a header line, if the finishing designer wants that treatment) — never for full paragraph body copy, since gold-on-navy body text at small sizes drops below comfortable mobile contrast, and Slides 2–4 already carry longer body sentences that need maximum legibility.
- No text is ever placed directly over high-frequency background detail (café-table marble veining, card-stock texture, retail-shelf blur) without a navy scrim between them — scrim is mandatory on every slide, not optional, especially given the longer attribution sentences on Slides 2–4.
- Minimum body-copy size should render no smaller than is comfortably legible on a phone at arm's length; if the full attribution sentence on Slides 2–4 can't fit above that minimum at 3 lines, flag it back to Copy rather than silently trimming the "not company-confirmed" qualifier.
