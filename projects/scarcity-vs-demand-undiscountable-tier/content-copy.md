## Caption

Manhattan's office market just handed entrepreneurs a pricing lesson hiding in plain sight.

Q3 2026: Class A asking rents hit $85.45/sf — an all-time high, beating pre-pandemic levels. Demand backs it up: tenants leased over 10M sf for the fourth straight quarter, a streak not seen since 2002. Availability fell to 13.4%, the tightest since 2020. (Colliers, via The Real Deal, Oct 1, 2026.)

But look at the trophy tier. In April 2026, HBeyond signed a 10-year lease at 9 West 57th St for $327.50/sf — at the time, the highest office rent on record in Manhattan, topping One Vanderbilt's $320/sf (The Real Deal / Commercial Observer). By June 2026, the landlord was seeking as much as $400/sf for what's left in the building — an ask, not a done deal (The Real Deal).

Same city. Same window of time. $85 vs. $327.50 closed vs. $400 asked.

That gap isn't about demand — the whole market has demand. It's about scarcity engineered within a defined tier. Trophy space can't be substituted, so it never has to discount.

Your pricing power works the same way. If you're competing in a crowded, interchangeable tier, the market sets your ceiling. If you build toward being the only option in a narrow, defined category, you set it yourself.

Audit your offer this week: are you discountable, or are you the trophy floor?
