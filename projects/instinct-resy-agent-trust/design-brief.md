## 1. Overall Creative Concept

Treat this as an editorial "systems failure" story, not a startup-gossip story and not a restaurant story. Visual language borrows from premium tech/business journalism (think Bloomberg Businessweek, The Information, Axios explainer graphics) rather than founder-culture motivational templates.

The through-line motif across all 7 slides is **a phone/app interface** — the actual surface where this story happened. The interface evolves slide to slide like a storyboard of one continuous UI:

1. A calm chat-assistant bubble makes a request
2. The UI shows rapid-fire ping/request activity (visualized as pulse/signal marks, not literal restaurant content)
3. The UI shifts into a system-flagging / anomaly-detection state
4. The UI locks — a stark "Account Locked" screen
5. The UI softens — access restored, but visually still cautious/muted, not celebratory
6. The UI drops away entirely for a quieter, typographic reflection slide
7. The UI motif returns only as a faint background texture behind the closing pull quote — text carries the slide

No human faces, no depiction of the investor (he is a private individual and is referenced by role only in copy — the visuals must match that restraint). No restaurant, food, table, or steakhouse imagery as a dominant element — this is an infrastructure/trust story about permissions and machine-speed behavior, not a dining story. A single, small, abstracted "reservation" glyph (e.g., a simple calendar/checkmark icon inside the chat bubble on Slide 1 only) is acceptable to anchor the ask, but it must stay minor and not become food/restaurant photography.

## 2. Composition and Focal Hierarchy (applies to all slides unless noted)

Standard portrait canvas: **1080 x 1350 px**.

Focal hierarchy, top to bottom:
1. JustPreneur wordmark — small, consistent, top-left corner
2. Headline / slide copy — primary focal block, upper-to-mid frame, large and left-aligned for mobile scanability
3. UI motif graphic — mid-to-lower frame, supporting visual, never competing with text for attention
4. Generous negative space at the bottom third to keep the design breathable and premium (avoid cramming)

Slide 1 carries the full headline (largest type in the carousel). Slides 2–6 carry body copy only, set smaller and left-aligned, each paired with its evolving UI graphic. Slide 7 is the exception: text-dominant, pull quote treated as its own typographic moment.

## 3. Slide-by-Slide Placement

### Slide 1 — Cover / The Ask
- **JustPreneur wordmark:** top-left, 64px inset from top and left safe margins, warm white, ~28pt medium weight
- **Headline:** "Your AI Agent Doesn't Know the Rules You Agreed To" — upper-third to mid-frame, left-aligned, max width ~85% of canvas, large bold sans, warm white on deep navy
- **Body:** "A venture capital investor asked his AI assistant to book him a table at a nearly impossible-to-get NYC steakhouse." — directly below headline, smaller weight, steel-gray-tinted white, max 2–3 lines
- **Imagery:** calm phone-UI chat bubble graphic, lower half of frame, showing a stylized "assistant" message bubble with a minimal calendar/checkmark glyph — no legible fake text inside the bubble, no fake app logos
- **Bottom margin:** clear, no clutter

### Slide 2 — The Agent's Behavior
- **Body copy:** "The assistant — an app called Instinct — went to work. It pinged the reservation platform, Resy, hundreds of times every hour, hunting for an opening at a restaurant where tables vanish within seconds of release." — upper third, left-aligned
- **Imagery:** same phone-UI frame from Slide 1, now showing a rapid pulse/signal pattern — radiating ping marks or a dense scatter of small dot-pulses emanating from the phone, suggesting machine-speed repetition. Abstract, no readable numbers or fake data labels.
- **Wordmark:** same fixed position, top-left

### Slide 3 — The Platform's Defense Response
- **Body copy:** "Resy's systems read that pattern as an attack. The platform says it 'does not currently permit unapproved third-party bots or agents' on its platform — and at machine speed, the agent looked exactly like one." — upper third, left-aligned
- **Imagery:** the ping pattern from Slide 2 now intersects with a scanning/analysis graphic — a subtle grid or radar-sweep line passing over the pulses, one pulse point highlighted in amber as "flagged." Muted, procedural, not alarmist.
- **Wordmark:** same fixed position

### Slide 4 — The Lockout
- **Body copy:** "Resy locked the account. Not the AI's account. His." — this is the emotional turn of the carousel; give it more vertical breathing room than other slides, centered in upper-mid frame
- **Imagery:** the phone-UI frame now shows a simple "Account Locked" state — a minimal padlock glyph inside the same UI frame used throughout, deep navy background, single muted amber accent line. No red alarm treatment — keep it restrained and procedural, consistent with editorial (not tabloid) tone.
- **Wordmark:** same fixed position

### Slide 5 — The Reinstatement
- **Body copy:** "Access was eventually restored. But for a stretch, a routine dinner reservation cost him control of his own account." — upper third, left-aligned
- **Imagery:** the padlock glyph from Slide 4 shown reopening/unlocked, but rendered in the same muted tones — not a bright celebratory green checkmark. This should read as "resolved, not resolved happily." Keep amber accent, no green.
- **Wordmark:** same fixed position

### Slide 6 — His Own Reaction
- **Body copy:** "His response, afterward: it was the first — and last — time he'd let an AI agent handle a booking for him." — this slide drops the UI motif entirely. Full-bleed deep navy or steel-gray field, single block of larger-than-body-copy type, centered vertically, left-aligned text block. Treat this as a quiet, reflective beat — a breather after the lockout/reinstatement sequence.
- **Imagery:** none, or an extremely subtle texture (fine grain / faint diagonal hairline pattern) — no UI chrome, no icons
- **Wordmark:** same fixed position

### Slide 7 — Builder Takeaway + Pull Quote
- **Body copy (top block):** "The gap here isn't what the AI could do. It's that no platform has written the rulebook yet for agents acting with your full authority. Before you connect an AI agent to anything business-critical — booking, CRM, ad accounts, scheduling — know what it's doing at machine speed, and what that platform's tolerance for it actually is." — upper half, left-aligned, standard body weight
- **Pull quote (bottom block):** "The account, the access, and the consequences are still yours." — set apart with large italic serif treatment, gold/amber color, oversized quotation mark as a graphic element, positioned lower-third as the closing statement of the carousel
- **Imagery:** faint ghosted version of the phone-UI frame as a background watermark-level texture only, fully subordinate to type
- **Wordmark:** same fixed position, plus this is the natural slide for the wordmark to optionally repeat slightly larger at the bottom as a sign-off (top-left placement stays primary; bottom echo is optional, small, not a "production mark")

## 4. Typography Direction

- **Headline (Slide 1):** Bold geometric/grotesk sans-serif (e.g., style of Söhne Breit, Neue Haas Grotesk Display, or Inter Bold/Black), tight tracking, large scale, warm white on navy. This is the single largest type moment in the carousel.
- **Body copy (Slides 1–5, 7 top block):** Same sans-serif family, regular/medium weight, generous line-height for mobile legibility, warm white or soft steel-white.
- **Slide 6 reflective block:** Same sans family but stepped up in size relative to other body slides (this slide has no competing imagery, so type can breathe larger) — medium weight, warm white.
- **Pull quote (Slide 7):** Contrasting italic serif (e.g., style of Tiempos Italic or Canela Italic) in energy-gold, to mark it as the editorial "money quote" — the one deliberate typographic contrast in the set.
- **JustPreneur wordmark:** Clean sans, medium weight, consistent size and color (warm white, or gold on Slide 7) across all 7 slides.

No more than two type families in the entire carousel (primary sans + one serif accent for the pull quote only).

---

## 5. Color Palette

- **Deep navy** `#0E1B2A` — dominant background across all slides (system/infrastructure tone, not warm hospitality tone)
- **Steel gray** `#3C4A59` — secondary panels, UI frame chrome, subdued body text tint
- **Warm white** `#F4F1EA` — primary text color, headline color
- **Energy gold (muted for this story)** `#C79A4B` — restrained accent only: flagged-pulse highlight (Slide 3), lock accent line (Slides 4–5), pull-quote type and quotation mark (Slide 7). Deliberately not full brand-brightness gold and never a red/alarm color — keeps the "account lockout" beats procedural and editorial rather than alarmist, consistent with the tone guardrail against mockery or sensationalism.

No green "success" color and no red "danger" color anywhere in the set — this is a deliberate restraint so the reinstatement (Slide 5) doesn't read as a happy ending and the lockout (Slide 4) doesn't read as tabloid alarm.

---

## 6. Image/Graphic Treatment

- All "imagery" in this carousel is UI/graphic motif work, not photography and not illustrated people. Flat, vector-clean, high-contrast against the navy field — closer to a fintech product-marketing aesthetic than a lifestyle-photography aesthetic.
- Consistent phone-UI frame proportions and position across Slides 1–5 so the eye reads it as one continuous device across the sequence.
- No fake legible text inside any UI mockup (no fabricated app names, timestamps, message content, or logos) — Instinct and Resy are named in the copy layer only, never faked as on-screen UI branding.
- No literal restaurant, food, table-setting, or steakhouse imagery anywhere in the set.
- No human figures, faces, hands, or silhouettes depicting the investor or any specific person.

---

## 7. Instagram Safe-Margin Instructions

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px per slide.
- Keep all headline, body copy, and JustPreneur wordmark within a safe zone of **72px minimum inset** on all four sides (left/right/top/bottom) — this protects against Instagram's UI cropping (profile row, caption overlap on the grid, swipe-indicator dots) and matches the "Instagram-safe margins" requirement.
- Nothing load-bearing (text or wordmark) closer than 72px to any edge; decorative UI-frame graphics may bleed closer to the edge but must not carry text.
- Maintain at least 90px of clear vertical space between the bottom of the last text block and the bottom canvas edge on every slide to avoid crowding.

---

## 8. Slide-to-Slide Consistency Rules

- Same canvas size (1080x1350) for all 7 slides.
- Same navy background base color across all 7 slides (Slide 6 may shift to steel-gray field as the one deliberate variation for pacing — see Slide 6 note above).
- JustPreneur wordmark identical size, color (warm white; gold only on Slide 7), and position (top-left) on every slide — this is the visual anchor that reads as one carousel.
- Body copy left-aligned and set at the same type size/weight across Slides 1–5 and the top block of Slide 7 (Slide 1 headline and Slide 6/Slide 7-pull-quote are the only intentional scale exceptions, per their roles above).
- The phone-UI frame keeps identical size/position/corner-radius across Slides 1–5 so its state changes (calm → pinging → flagged → locked → unlocked) read as one evolving object, not five different graphics.
- Amber/gold accent used sparingly and only for the specific "system state" cues described per slide (pulse highlight, lock accent, pull quote) — never as generic decoration.

---

## 9. Accessibility and Contrast Check

- Warm white (`#F4F1EA`) body/headline text on deep navy (`#0E1B2A`) background: contrast ratio is well above WCAG AA for large text (~13:1), safe for headline and body use.
- Steel-gray (`#3C4A59`) is used only for secondary UI chrome and subdued sub-text, never for primary headline or body copy, to avoid low-contrast failures.
- Muted gold (`#C79A4B`) on navy background used for the Slide 7 pull quote: contrast ratio ~5.5:1, sufficient for large/bold serif display type; do not use this gold-on-navy pairing for small body text.
- No text is ever placed directly over busy UI-graphic detail — each slide's copy sits on a clean navy (or steel-gray, Slide 6) field with the UI motif confined to its own zone, protecting legibility at small mobile thumbnail size.
- Minimum effective text size targets: headline (Slide 1) large/bold enough to read at thumbnail scale; body copy set no smaller than what renders clearly at a ~350px-wide phone preview.
