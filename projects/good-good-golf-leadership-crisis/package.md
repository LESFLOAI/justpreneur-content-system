## 1. Facts Summary (Approved Facts Block, verified by Fact Verifier)

In August 2026, Good Good Golf and Callaway released a co-branded driver advertisement in which Good Good co-founder Garrett Clark shoved colleague and brand influencer Alexis Miestowski to the ground to stop her from touching the driver — a scene Clark and then-CEO Matt Kendrick said was meant to parody the film "Obsession." The ad, released in late August 2026 (reported by WWD as Aug. 21) and removed roughly a day later, drew widespread criticism for trivializing violence against women.

According to multiple reports, Callaway had pre-approved the ad; Callaway subsequently ended its partnership with Good Good and pledged $1 million to organizations supporting victims of, and working to prevent, violence against women. The PGA Tour parted ways with Good Good as title sponsor of a new FedEx Cup Fall event (tournament week Nov. 9–15, 2026, at Omni Barton Creek Resort & Spa's Fazio Canyons Course in Austin, Texas); Good Good announced on Aug. 27, 2026 that it was "stepping away" from that sponsorship — reporting is inconsistent on whether this was voluntary or PGA Tour-initiated, so no cause is asserted — and the event is temporarily listed on the PGA Tour's site as the "Austin Championship" pending a new title sponsor. Golf Channel separately shelved the debut season of its "Big Break x Good Good" reality-show reboot, stating the show's PGA Tour prize exemption no longer existed, though it still plans a new "Big Break" season in 2027. Retailers including Dick's Sporting Goods, Golf Galaxy, and PGA Tour Superstore pulled Good Good–Callaway merchandise.

On or about Sept. 2–3, 2026, Good Good CEO Matt Kendrick and President Joe Flannery left the company, per an internal memo from CFO/COO Alex Puchala. Good Good co-founder and early investor Nahid Giga became interim CEO. Garrett Clark, who appeared in the ad, has not been reported to have left the company and posted a video apology stating, "This is not, at all, what we stand for."

## 4. Final Caption (QC-corrected)

A co-branded campaign has two authors — and one insurance policy.

In late August 2026, a Callaway x Good Good ad went live. According to multiple reports, Callaway had approved it before launch. When the fallout came, it landed almost entirely on Good Good: it stepped away from its PGA Tour title sponsorship (the event is now listed as the Austin Championship, pending a new sponsor), a shelved TV debut season (Golf Channel has a 2027 season planned), pulled retail distribution, and two executive exits. Callaway pledged $1M to anti-violence-against-women organizations and moved forward. Co-founder Garrett Clark remains with the company and posted a video apology.

Approval isn't protection. If you're building a co-branded campaign, negotiate who owns the crisis — not just who owns the creative.

Save this before your next partnership conversation.
