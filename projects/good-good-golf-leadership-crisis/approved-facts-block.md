## 2. Required Corrections / Softenings (all 5+1 flagged by the assigning editor)

1. **Vague ad date** — Do not state a single precise date as unqualified fact. Use: "released in late August 2026 (reported by WWD as Aug. 21) and taken down roughly a day later."
2. **"Austin Championship" renaming** — Do not say the event was "renamed the Austin Championship." Say it is "temporarily listed" that way on the PGA Tour's site while a new title sponsor is sought; no permanent new name or sponsor has been confirmed as of this check.
3. **2025 funding round timing** — Do not place the $45M raise near the 2026 crisis timeline. It was announced March 20, 2025 — over a year before the ad controversy — and must be described as unrelated background, not part of the crisis narrative.
4. **Giga's title attribution** — Nahid Giga should only be described as "Good Good co-founder and early investor, now serving as interim CEO." No source supports any additional or permanent title.
5. **Partial-quote framing for Kendrick's line** — The "coordinated media blitz... covers it up" quote must be attributed explicitly to Kendrick as his allegation/opinion, not presented as a verified account of Callaway's actions. Consider using an ellipsis-free, fully attributed block quote rather than paraphrasing fragments, to avoid stripping context.
6. **VP dismissal detail (Lefkovits)** — Single-sourced in this session; flag for confirmation before use, or omit.

## 3. Fairness / Balance Context Required in Any Downstream Copy

- **Callaway pre-approved the ad** (per multiple reports) before the backlash — this complicates a narrative that places responsibility solely on Good Good/Clark.
- **Callaway pledged $1 million** to organizations supporting victims of, and working to prevent, violence against women — a mitigating/responsive action, reported alongside the partnership termination.
- **Golf Channel's "Big Break x Good Good" reboot season was shelved**, not permanently cancelled as a franchise — Golf Channel has stated it intends to air a new "Big Break" season in 2027.
- **Garrett Clark, the co-founder shown in the ad, remains with the company** and personally apologized on video ("This is not, at all, what we stand for"), distinguishing his outcome from Kendrick's and Flannery's departures.

Any narrative that frames this purely as "Good Good's scandal" without these four points risks being materially misleading by omission.

## 4. Approved Facts Block (verbatim — paste into downstream copy as-is; do not alter wording without re-verification)

> In August 2026, Good Good Golf and Callaway released a co-branded driver advertisement in which Good Good co-founder Garrett Clark shoved colleague and brand influencer Alexis Miestowski to the ground to stop her from touching the driver — a scene Clark and then-CEO Matt Kendrick said was meant to parody the film "Obsession." The ad, released in late August 2026 (reported by WWD as Aug. 21) and removed roughly a day later, drew widespread criticism for trivializing violence against women. According to multiple reports, Callaway had pre-approved the ad; Callaway subsequently ended its partnership with Good Good and pledged $1 million to organizations supporting victims of, and working to prevent, violence against women. The PGA Tour parted ways with Good Good as title sponsor of a new FedEx Cup Fall event (the "Good Good Championship," tournament week Nov. 9–15, 2026, at Omni Barton Creek Resort & Spa's Fazio Canyons Course in Austin, Texas); Good Good announced on Aug. 27, 2026 that it was "stepping away" from that sponsorship, and the event is temporarily listed on the PGA Tour's site as the "Austin Championship" pending a new title sponsor. Golf Channel separately shelved the debut season of its "Big Break x Good Good" reality-show reboot, stating the show's PGA Tour prize exemption no longer existed, though it said it still plans a new "Big Break" season in 2027. Retailers including Dick's Sporting Goods, Golf Galaxy, and PGA Tour Superstore pulled Good Good–Callaway merchandise. On or about Sept. 2–3, 2026, Good Good CEO Matt Kendrick and President Joe Flannery left the company, per an internal memo from CFO/COO Alex Puchala. Before departing, Kendrick posted a social-media statement alleging that Callaway asked Good Good to make the ad, approved it, then "asks us to take the fall," drops the company "in a coordinated media blitz," and "covers it up by giving a million dollars away" — an allegation attributed to Kendrick, not an independently verified account of Callaway's conduct or motives. Good Good co-founder and early investor Nahid Giga became interim CEO; Alex Puchala remains COO/CFO overseeing daily operations. Garrett Clark, who appeared in the ad, has not been reported to have left the company and posted a video apology stating, "This is not, at all, what we stand for." Separately, and unrelated in timing to the 2026 crisis, Good Good Golf raised a $45 million funding round led by Creator Sports Capital, announced March 20, 2025 — more than a year before the ad controversy — with investors including Peyton Manning's Omaha Productions, Manhattan West Private Equity, and Sunflower Bank. As of this check on Sept. 27, 2026, Nahid Giga remains interim CEO with no permanent successor announced, and no new PGA Tour title sponsor has been named for the Austin event.
