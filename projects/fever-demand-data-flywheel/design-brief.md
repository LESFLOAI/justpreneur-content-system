## 1. Overall Creative Concept

Treat this as a premium business-media explainer, in the register of The Information, Bloomberg Businessweek, or a16z's own explainer decks — not a startup-hype template and not a generic "here's a funding round" motivational post. The reader should feel like they're getting an analyst's read on a deal, not a congratulations post.

The through-line visual motif across the carousel is a **flywheel / closed loop** connecting two nodes:
- **Marketplace** (ticket transactions — Fever's DICE-powered resale/ticketing business)
- **Owned Experiences** (Fever's self-produced live events, referenced by category only — "owned experiences" — never rendered as the real Candlelight logo or real event photography)

The loop is built progressively across the slide sequence, so the carousel itself performs the thesis: the reader watches the flywheel assemble slide by slide, exactly as the argument builds. By Slide 7, the loop is fully closed and rendered as the hero graphic behind the money-quote.

No real logos, no fabricated logos, no depiction of Fever, DICE, Candlelight, EQT, Baillie Gifford, Point72, Atresmedia, or F1 as literal brand marks, cars, drivers, tracks, or event photography anywhere in the generated imagery. All company and platform names live in the copy layer only (added in the design tool), never faked as on-screen branding, trademarks, or logos in the AI-generated backgrounds. This protects against misrepresenting real companies' actual marks and keeps every image legally and editorially safe.

## 2. Composition and Focal Hierarchy (applies to all slides unless noted)

Standard portrait canvas: **1080 x 1350 px**.

Focal hierarchy, top to bottom:
1. JustPreneur wordmark — small, consistent, top-left corner, every slide
2. Headline / body copy — primary focal block, upper-to-mid frame, left-aligned for mobile scanability
3. Flywheel/loop motif graphic — mid-to-lower frame, supporting visual, evolving slide to slide, never competing with text for attention
4. Generous negative space in the bottom third to keep the design breathable and premium

Slide 1 carries the full headline (largest type in the carousel). Slide 2 is copy-and-numbers-dense and gets special typographic handling (see Section 3 and the critical constraint below). Slides 3–6 carry body copy paired with the evolving flywheel graphic. Slide 7 is the thesis/pull-quote slide — text-dominant, with the completed flywheel as its hero backdrop. The optional Slide 8 (CTA) is quiet and typographic, echoing Slide 1's restraint.

## 3. Slide-by-Slide Placement

### Slide 1 — Cover
- **JustPreneur wordmark:** top-left, 64px inset from top/left safe margins, warm white, ~28pt medium weight
- **Headline:** "The Real Product Fever Is Selling Isn't Tickets — It's Demand Data" — upper-third to mid-frame, left-aligned, max width ~85% of canvas, large bold sans, warm white on deep navy. This is the single largest type moment in the carousel.
- **Subtext:** "A $250M raise. The real story is what happens between the transactions." — directly below headline, smaller weight, steel-gray-tinted white, max 2 lines
- **Imagery:** the flywheel motif in its earliest, quietest state — two faint unconnected nodes (simple circles/rings) in the lower half of frame, not yet joined by a visible loop-arrow. This plants the visual idea without explaining it yet.
- **Bottom margin:** clear, no clutter

### Slide 2 — The Raise (numbers + hedges — critical slide)
- **Body copy, set as a sequence of short editorial lines, not a single dense paragraph** (break the approved copy into visually distinct beats so the qualifiers get their own breathing room, not compressed into footnote treatment):
  1. "Fever — the platform behind DICE — just raised $250M, led by EQT, with Baillie Gifford, Point72, and existing shareholders joining."
  2. "Fever calls it the largest-ever financing round for a live-entertainment tech company — Fever's own characterization, not an independently audited benchmark."
  3. "Trade press estimates a ~$5.2B valuation based on a separate Atresmedia share sale; Fever hasn't confirmed a new valuation alongside this round."
- **Imagery:** the two flywheel nodes from Slide 1, still unconnected, now smaller and pushed to the lower-right corner as a quiet continuity marker — this slide is about the numbers, not the motif, so the graphic recedes.
- **Wordmark:** same fixed position

### Slide 3 — Fever Also Builds Product
- **Body copy:** "Fever isn't only a resale marketplace. It also owns and runs its own experiences — like Candlelight concerts. Most marketplaces just take a cut. Fever also builds product." — upper third, left-aligned
- **Imagery:** the two flywheel nodes now visually distinguished for the first time — the "Marketplace" node rendered as a cool steel-gray/navy ring with small ticket-stub-like tick marks around its edge; the "Owned Experiences" node rendered as a warm gold-glow circle (candle-glow abstraction — soft radial warm light, no literal candle, no literal concert stage). Nodes remain unconnected, positioned side by side in the lower half of frame.
- **Wordmark:** same fixed position

### Slide 4 — Every Ticket Is a Data Point
- **Body copy:** "Every ticket sold through Fever's marketplace is a data point — what sells out, what stalls, what price holds, what city wants what and when. That's raw demand signal, generated just by running the marketplace." — upper third, left-aligned
- **Imagery:** the "Marketplace" node (steel-gray ring) now shown actively generating small particle/dot marks that drift inward toward the center of the frame — visualizing raw transactional exhaust accumulating. The "Owned Experiences" (gold-glow) node sits quietly to the side, not yet receiving anything.
- **Wordmark:** same fixed position

### Slide 5 — The Signal Feeds Back
- **Body copy:** "That signal doesn't stay on the marketplace side. It feeds decisions about Candlelight — what to build, where, and for whom. The transactional exhaust from one business becomes the R&D input for the other." — upper third, left-aligned
- **Imagery:** the loop closes for the first time — a single clean curved arrow/ribbon connects the "Marketplace" node to the "Owned Experiences" node, carrying the drifting particles from Slide 4 into the gold-glow node, which brightens slightly on contact. This is the visual "aha" moment of the sequence.
- **Wordmark:** same fixed position

### Slide 6 — The Loop Scales
- **Body copy:** "A pure rake-taking marketplace earns a fee and moves on. Fever earns the fee — and keeps the signal. The marketplace keeps scaling too: in June 2026, F1 named Fever its Official Ticketing Supplier for the 2027–2031 seasons. The platform hasn't launched yet, but once live, that's years more transaction data feeding the same loop. More volume on one side, better product decisions on the other — that compounds." — upper half, left-aligned, slightly smaller type to accommodate the longer copy block while staying within safe margins
- **Imagery:** the same closed loop from Slide 5, now with a second, larger inbound arrow feeding into the "Marketplace" node from off-frame (representing new/future volume, rendered as an abstract widening funnel of particles — no F1 branding, no car, no track, no checkered flag, no red/racing color cues). The loop itself renders visibly thicker/brighter than in Slide 5 to convey compounding scale.
- **Wordmark:** same fixed position

### Slide 7 — The Moat Thesis (pull quote)
- **Body copy (top block):** "This isn't 'marketplaces should launch a second product.'" — upper third, left-aligned, standard weight
- **Pull quote (lower block):** "It's this: if your marketplace data isn't feeding an owned decision somewhere, you're only collecting a rake — not building a moat." — set apart with large italic serif treatment, energy-gold color, oversized quotation mark as a graphic element, lower-half of frame as the closing statement
- **Imagery:** the fully closed flywheel loop from Slide 6, now rendered as the hero graphic — larger, centered, both nodes bright, the connecting loop-arrow complete and glowing — positioned behind/around the pull quote at low-to-mid opacity so it reads as a backdrop, not a competitor to the text.
- **Wordmark:** same fixed position, optionally echoed slightly larger at the bottom in gold as a closing sign-off (top-left stays primary)

### Slide 8 — Optional CTA
- **Body copy:** "Look at your own model. Are you capturing a signal, or just a fee?" — upper-to-mid frame, left-aligned, larger reflective type (no competing graphic on this slide, similar treatment to a quiet "breather" slide)
- **CTA line:** "Save this to revisit when you're scoping your next product decision." — directly below, smaller weight, steel-gray-tinted white
- **Imagery:** a single faint, ghosted low-opacity version of the completed flywheel loop as a background watermark-level texture only — fully subordinate to type, no particles, no motion marks
- **Wordmark:** same fixed position

## CRITICAL CONSTRAINT — Slide 2 Hedge Language (do not compress)

Per Fact Verifier / Copywriter instruction: the qualifying lines on Slide 2 — "Fever's own characterization, not an independently audited benchmark" and "Trade press estimates ... Fever hasn't confirmed a new valuation alongside this round" — **must render at the same type weight and size as the surrounding factual lines on that slide.** They are not disclaimers, footnotes, fine print, or small captions — they are load-bearing parts of the claim itself.

Concretely for the design tool pass:
- Do not shrink these two lines relative to the $250M/EQT line.
- Do not set them in a lighter/lower-contrast color than the rest of Slide 2's body copy (no gray-on-gray footnote treatment).
- Do not push them to the bottom margin or a corner as if they were a citation — keep them in the same left-aligned body flow, directly under the fact they qualify, at conversational reading distance (no more than one line of vertical gap).
- If Slide 2 needs to break into two visually stacked groups for breathing room, keep each fact + its qualifier together as one visual unit rather than separating all facts from all qualifiers.
- Never render "$5.2B valuation" as a standalone large/bold pull-number on this slide without its qualifying sentence visible in the same frame at legible size. If any type hierarchy is applied to Slide 2 at all, the qualifier sentence should if anything be the same size as the number line, not smaller.

Flag to Quality Control: this is the single highest-risk slide in the set for accidental "confirmed fact" framing — check the rendered layout literally, not just the copy file, before this goes out.

## 4. Typography Direction

- **Headline (Slide 1):** Bold geometric/grotesk sans-serif (style of Söhne Breit, Neue Haas Grotesk Display, or Inter Bold/Black), tight tracking, large scale, warm white on navy.
- **Body copy (Slides 1–6, top block of 7, both lines of 8):** Same sans-serif family, regular/medium weight, generous line-height for mobile legibility, warm white.
- **Slide 2 numbers vs. qualifiers:** same family and same size for both fact-lines and qualifier-lines per the critical constraint above — no separate "footnote" type style introduced anywhere in this carousel.
- **Pull quote (Slide 7):** Contrasting italic serif (style of Tiempos Italic or Canela Italic) in energy-gold, marking it as the editorial "money quote" — the one deliberate typographic contrast in the set.
- **JustPreneur wordmark:** Clean sans, medium weight, consistent size and color (warm white; gold only on Slide 7) across all slides.

No more than two type families in the entire carousel (primary sans + one serif accent reserved for the Slide 7 pull quote only).

## 6. Image/Graphic Treatment

- All "imagery" in this carousel is abstract flywheel/node/particle motif work, not photography and not illustrated people or real events.
- Flat, vector-clean, high-contrast against the navy field — closer to a fintech/data-product marketing aesthetic than a lifestyle or concert-photography aesthetic.
- Consistent node size, position, and proportion across Slides 1–7 so the eye reads the "Marketplace" and "Owned Experiences" nodes as the same two objects evolving state-by-state (unconnected → distinguished → generating → connected → scaling → complete).
- No fake legible text, numbers, logos, or UI chrome inside any graphic — Fever, DICE, Candlelight, EQT, Baillie Gifford, Point72, Atresmedia, and F1 are named in the copy layer only, never faked as on-screen branding.
- No literal concert, stage, candle, ticket-scanner, racetrack, or car imagery anywhere in the set — everything stays at the level of abstract node/particle/loop graphics.
- No human figures, faces, hands, or silhouettes anywhere in the set.

## 7. Instagram Safe-Margin Instructions

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px per slide.
- Keep all headline, body copy, pull quote, and JustPreneur wordmark within a safe zone of **72px minimum inset** on all four sides — protects against Instagram's UI cropping (profile row, caption overlap on the grid, swipe-indicator dots).
- Nothing load-bearing (text or wordmark) closer than 72px to any edge; decorative loop/node graphics may bleed closer to the edge but must never carry text.
- Maintain at least 90px of clear vertical space between the bottom of the last text block and the bottom canvas edge on every slide.
- Slide 2 specifically: given the higher copy density, confirm in layout that all three fact/qualifier lines still sit fully inside the 72px margins with no line touching the safe-zone boundary — do not shrink margins to fit more text; shrink type slightly instead, keeping fact and qualifier lines at equal size to each other.

## 8. Slide-to-Slide Consistency Rules

- Same canvas size (1080x1350) for all 8 slides.
- Same navy background base color across all slides.
- JustPreneur wordmark identical size, color (warm white; gold only on Slide 7), and position (top-left) on every slide.
- Body copy left-aligned and set at the same type size/weight across Slides 1–6 and the top block of Slide 7 (Slide 1 headline and Slide 7 pull quote are the only intentional scale exceptions).
- The two flywheel nodes keep identical size/position/relationship across Slides 1–7 so their state changes (unconnected → distinguished → generating → connected → scaling → complete) read as one evolving diagram, not seven different graphics.
- Gold accent used sparingly and only for the "Owned Experiences" node glow and the completed-loop states (Slides 3–7) plus the pull quote — never as generic decoration, and never introduced before Slide 3.

## 9. Accessibility and Contrast Check

- Warm white (`#F4F1EA`) body/headline text on deep navy (`#0E1B2A`) background: contrast ratio ~13:1, well above WCAG AA for both large and body text.
- Steel-gray (`#3C4A59`) reserved for the Marketplace node graphic and subdued sub-text only, never for primary headline or body copy, to avoid low-contrast failures.
- Energy gold (`#C8A24A`) on navy for the Slide 7 pull quote: contrast ratio ~5.2:1, sufficient for large/bold serif display type; not used for small body text anywhere.
- No text is ever placed directly over the densest part of the particle/node graphic — each slide's copy sits on a clean navy field with the motif confined to its own zone, protecting legibility at mobile thumbnail size.
- Slide 2 specifically checked for equal-contrast treatment between fact lines and qualifier lines (see critical constraint) — this is an accessibility/trust issue as much as a legal one, since a visually "quieter" hedge is a misleading-by-design-choice risk even if the words are technically present.
