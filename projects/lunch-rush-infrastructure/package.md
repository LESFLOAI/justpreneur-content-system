## 1. Facts Summary (Approved Facts Block, verbatim from Fact Verifier)

> According to data Rappi reported on October 20, 2026, orders for corrientazo (Colombia's classic fixed-price lunch) and executive-lunch sets on its platform grew approximately 160% year-to-date in 2026 compared with the same period in 2025 — rising from roughly 250,000 to over 660,000 orders. Rappi said growth was strongest in mid-sized cities rather than Bogotá: Villavicencio (+533%), Ibagué (+414%), and Santa Marta (+346%), compared with Bogotá's +239% (other cities Rappi cited: Cartagena +306%, Valledupar +238%, Barranquilla +228%). Rappi did not disclose absolute order volumes by city, so these are percentage-growth comparisons, not comparisons of total order counts. Rappi reported more than 2,000 restaurants offer corrientazo/executive-lunch options on the platform, more than 800 of which price a full lunch under COP $19,900 (as of the October 2026 reporting date, Colombia's official exchange rate was approximately COP 3,150–3,200 per US dollar, making COP $19,900 roughly equivalent to US$6, not US$5). Rappi also reported that 84% of these orders are placed between 11:00 a.m. and 2:59 p.m., with a peak around noon. All figures are company-reported by Rappi and relayed by Colombian outlets (La República, Portafolio, Blu Radio, Pulzo); none have been independently audited by a third party or confirmed via a Rappi primary press release/newsroom post. The claim that "Rappi reached profitability in Colombia" is unconfirmed, out of scope, and excluded from this block.

**Mandatory framing rules honored throughout this package:**
1. Never "$5" or bare "~$5" anywhere — price always reads "COP $19,900 (~US$6, per Rappi, Oct. 2026)." The Story Scout's original working headline ("The $5 Lunch...") was rejected by Fact Verifier and is not used anywhere in this package.
2. Every stat attributed to Rappi ("Rappi reported," "per Rappi") — never stated as independently confirmed or third-party audited.
3. Time window always reads "11:00 a.m.–2:59 p.m." or "just before 3 p.m." — never a clean "3pm"/"3:00 PM," in copy or in the clock-arc visuals.
4. No claim or visual implies mid-size cities out-order Bogotá in absolute terms — city figures are growth-RATE percentages off undisclosed bases only; no bar/height chart comparing cities by volume.
5. No mention or implication anywhere of Rappi "profitability" in Colombia — fully excluded per Fact Verifier.
6. No Rappi logo or branding used in a way implying partnership or official endorsement — this is third-party commentary on publicly reported data, not sponsored content.

## 4. Final Caption

Rappi reported that corrientazo and executive-lunch orders on its platform grew about 160% year-to-date in 2026 vs. 2025 — from roughly 250,000 to over 660,000 orders. Growth was strongest in mid-sized cities like Villavicencio, Ibagué, and Santa Marta compared with Bogotá, per Rappi — these are growth-RATE percentages off undisclosed bases, not order-volume comparisons. More than 2,000 restaurants offer corrientazo on the platform; over 800 price a full lunch under COP $19,900 (~US$6, per Rappi, Oct. 2026).

But the number that matters most: Rappi reported 84% of these orders land between 11:00 a.m. and 2:59 p.m., peaking around noon.

That's not a lunch story. That's a demand-engineering story. A recurring, time-boxed, habitual window — four hours, same hours, day after day — is the kind of predictability most businesses never get and rarely design around.

Predictability is the asset. Not the price.

What's the four-hour window in your business — and have you actually built operations around it?

(All figures as reported by Rappi, October 2026, relayed via Colombian outlets; not independently audited.)
