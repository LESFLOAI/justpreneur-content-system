## 2. Slide-by-Slide Copy

### Slide 1 — Cover / Hook
**Headline:** THE CORNER STORE ISN'T COPYING ITSELF IN VEGAS — IT'S SYSTEMIZING ITSELF
**Kicker (optional, small):** A look inside the real playbook for scaling a hospitality brand.

### Slide 2 — Lesson Framing
**Headline:** Most expansions fail one of two ways.
**Body:** Improvise everything, and you lose what made the brand work in the first place. Copy-paste everything, and you ignore the market you're entering.

### Slide 3 — The Constant Core
**Headline:** What doesn't change, by design.
**Body:** The Lobster & Caviar Rolls. The Five Cheese Pizza Rolls. The Wagyu French Dip. The Rockwell Group design language. The service identity that made the SoHo original one of the toughest reservations in Manhattan.

### Slide 4 — The Vegas-Only Flex
**Headline:** What's built to flex, on purpose.
**Body:** A Vegas-exclusive Butcher's Counter steak program — Rosewood Ranch Wagyu Tomahawk, Wagyu Porterhouse. A Vegas-only Dirty Martini, served alongside the Tomato and Sour Cream & Onion Martinis carried over from SoHo.

### Slide 5 — Design Continuity
**Headline:** Even the architecture repeats the system.
**Body:** Rockwell Group designed the original SoHo space — and designed the Las Vegas location too. Inside The Cosmopolitan's third floor: a Martini Room, a train-car-inspired dining room.

### Slide 6 — The Takeaway
**Headline:** This is the real lesson.
**Body:** Scalable brands don't run on improvisation or rigid copy-paste. They run on a fixed, non-negotiable core — plus deliberate, limited localization.

### Slide 7 — CTA / Steal This Framework
**Headline:** Steal this framework.
**Body:** Before you expand: define what never changes. Then decide — on purpose — what's allowed to flex. The Corner Store's Las Vegas location is set to open November 9, 2026, inside The Cosmopolitan.

## 5. Final Caption

The Corner Store is bringing its SoHo hit to Las Vegas — but it's not copying the restaurant. It's replicating a system.

Set to open November 9, 2026, inside The Cosmopolitan of Las Vegas, the new location keeps the non-negotiables: the Lobster & Caviar Rolls, the Five Cheese Pizza Rolls, the Wagyu French Dip, and the Rockwell Group design that helped make the SoHo original (475 West Broadway, opened September 2024) one of the toughest reservations in Manhattan — drawing visits from celebrities including Taylor Swift.

Then it localizes on purpose: a Las Vegas-exclusive Butcher's Counter steak program (Rosewood Ranch Wagyu Tomahawk, Wagyu Porterhouse), a Vegas-only Dirty Martini, and a Martini Room and train-car-inspired dining room inside the former Blue Ribbon American Grill & Oyster Bar space.

This is the brand's first location outside New York — announced in partnership with MGM Resorts, extending a relationship between Catch Hospitality Group and MGM that dates back to 2018's Catch at ARIA. Catch Hospitality Group was co-founded by Eugene Remm and Mark Birnbaum in 2011; Tilman Fertitta has been a 50% stakeholder in the group since 2017.

The lesson for anyone scaling a brand: don't improvise everything, and don't copy-paste everything. Fix the core. Flex the rest — deliberately.

Reservations are open now via The Cosmopolitan's website or (702) 675-9828.
