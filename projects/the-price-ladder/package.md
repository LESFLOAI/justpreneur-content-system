## Facts Summary (Approved Facts Block — verified by Fact Verifier, status: Cleared with qualifications)

On September 22, 2026, Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5, priced at $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens — a 20% reduction from Claude Opus 5's $5/$25 list price. Anthropic says the new model also generates output more than 30% faster than Opus 5, and separately claims that, once caching and efficiency gains are included, typical workloads cost about 40% less to run (a different figure from the 20% list-price cut). Anthropic also raised five-hour usage limits by 20% for Pro, Max, Team, and seat-based Enterprise plans and added a temporary, one-time "rate limit reset" feature — it did not eliminate usage caps.

Approximately 90 minutes later that same day, OpenAI released two new models, GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna. GPT-6 Sol is priced at $2/$10 per million input/output tokens, down from GPT-5.6 Sol's $4/$20 (a 50% cut). GPT-6 Luna is priced at $0.10/$0.50 per million tokens, down from GPT-5.6 Luna's $0.20/$1.20 (a 50% cut on input, roughly 58% on output). An OpenAI spokesperson told VentureBeat this pricing is permanent rather than promotional. GPT-6 Sol's pricing undercuts Anthropic's newly announced Opus 5.5 on both input and output cost.

GPT-6 Luna's input price is below some competing budget models, including Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.6-Flash, but Luna's output-token price is higher than MiMo-V2.6-Flash's, and other low-cost models (including a DeepSeek "flash" tier) remain cheaper than Luna on a pure per-token basis. Luna should not be described as priced below "nearly every" frontier-class model.

These releases came roughly ten days after Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published a call to "pace the frontier" (slow the release cadence of the most capable models), which OpenAI CEO Sam Altman publicly endorsed. Multiple news outlets (CNBC, Fortune, Axios, Yahoo Finance) editorialized that the price cuts undercut or contradicted that pledge — this is press interpretation, not a statement from either company, and is not used in this piece's copy.

**QC note (applied):** Slide 3 / caption originally used "OpenAI answered," which implied the launch was reactive to Anthropic's move. The facts only establish ~90-minute timing, not causation — corrected to neutral chronological phrasing below.

## Final Caption

Sept 22, 2026: Anthropic cut Claude Opus 5.5 to $4 / $20 per million tokens — a 20% list-price drop from Opus 5. About 90 minutes later, OpenAI followed with two launches at once: GPT-6 Sol (frontier tier, $2 / $10) and GPT-6 Luna (commodity tier, $0.10 / $0.50) — pricing OpenAI says is permanent.

The headline is the price war. The real story is the ladder.

One company cut a single price line. The other shipped two tiers simultaneously — a premium option and a stripped-down, high-volume option — landing in front of more buyers at once, same day.

If your offer stack has one price point, you're exposed on one front. If it has tiers, you're harder to out-position.

Save this and audit your own ladder: where's your premium tier? Where's your accessible one?
