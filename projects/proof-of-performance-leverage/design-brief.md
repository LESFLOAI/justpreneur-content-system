## 0. Hard constraints carried into every visual decision

These override general brand defaults wherever they conflict. They come directly from the approved copy's hedging and the subjects involved:

1. **No real-person likenesses.** Byron Allen, Jimmy Fallon, and Jimmy Kimmel are real, named public figures and the copy explicitly hedges disputed figures attributed to them. No image-generation prompt asks for any of their faces or a recognizable likeness. Any human presence in generated art is hands-only, silhouette, or fully abstracted icon/figure — never a posed portrait. Names are added as plain text in the design tool after generation, never baked into AI images.
2. **No real network/show logos or trade dress.** CBS, NBC, "The Tonight Show," and "Comics Unleashed" are real trademarked properties. No prompt requests their logos, wordmarks, or brand-specific color systems. Any broadcast/studio reference in generated art uses a generic, non-trademarked abstract emblem or a neutral "TV/scoreboard" motif, never a literal mark.
3. **Disputed and unconfirmed figures must read as visually hedged, not confirmed.** The $40M CBS loss figure, the $15–20M Allen figure, and the "early, unconfirmed streaming talks" must each get a visibly provisional treatment (muted/desaturated marker color, dashed or bracketed container, or similar) wherever they appear as graphic elements — mirroring how the copy itself never states them as settled fact. This is a factual-accuracy constraint, not a style preference.
4. **No invented data points.** Only the approved numbers and language from `copy.md` appear anywhere (as exact text added in layout). No image prompt asks for additional statistics, dates, or labels beyond what's approved. No slide counters, timestamps, or citation marks are added.
5. **AI images supply mood, motif, and negative space only — never exact numerals, dollar figures, or body copy.** Every number and word of copy in this carousel (1.175M, 1.073M, 18–49, $40M, $15–20M, Oct 16, all headlines and body lines) is added afterward as a precise text layer in the design tool, per brand convention of never relying on image generation to render exact figures reliably.

## 1. Overall creative concept

Treat this as a **business-news data explainer**, visually closer to a premium financial/sports-business graphics package (think Sportico or Bloomberg's "numbers behind the headline" segments) than a motivational template — fitting a story about which number actually gets you paid.

The visual spine is the **Scoreboard vs. Ledger** device, set up explicitly in the cover headline:
- **Scoreboard register (steel gray, cool light):** the flashy, loud, easy-to-report number — total viewers, the "winner" headline. Rendered via stadium-scoreboard / TV-ratings-crawl motifs. Visually impressive but intentionally a little hollow — bright surface, little depth.
- **Ledger/Leverage register (energy gold, warm light):** the quiet number that actually pays bills — the demo win, the deal money, the leverage point. Rendered via ledger-book, target-medallion, balance-scale, and fulcrum/lever motifs. Less flashy, more weight and depth — the "real" number.

This single color-coded device carries the whole carousel: every slide's background visibly belongs to one register or the other (or, on the disputed-money slide, shows tension between them), so a viewer tracks "is this the headline number or the real number" without rereading text. Slides 6 (lesson) and 7 (CTA) strip the device away entirely for a flat, high-contrast, text-forward treatment — the "cleared board" moment where the thesis and the ask land with maximum clarity after five slides of visual build-up.

## 2. Composition and focal hierarchy

- Full-bleed background graphic per slide (flat, graphic-design-forward motion-graphics-style plate — not photographic/cinematic; this is a data explainer, not a lifestyle moment).
- A short eyebrow/kicker line (ALL CAPS, small, gold or steel per register) sits above the main headline on slides 2–5, matching the approved copy's slide labels ("THE NUMBER THE HEADLINES USE," etc.).
- The main headline/number statement for each slide sits in the **upper-to-mid third** of the frame, within the grid-crop-safe zone (see §7), so it survives both the full carousel view and the profile-grid square crop.
- Supporting body copy sits directly beneath the headline, in a tighter, lighter weight, never crowding the lower edge.
- JustPreneur wordmark: small, steady, top-safe-zone corner on slides 2–6 (consistent position/opacity); large and centered as a closing lockup on slide 7 only, paired with the CTA question — matching the brand's standard "small watermark throughout, large sting at the end" convention.
- Slide 1 (cover) is the one exception: JustPreneur wordmark sits small at the top (same position as interior slides) so brand presence is established from frame one, while the split Scoreboard/Leverage headline dominates the center.

## 4. Typography direction

- **Headlines (all slides):** bold grotesk/sans, the family already established across JustPreneur carousels (Neue-Haas-Grotesk-Condensed-style heavy weight), sentence case on body lines, ALL CAPS on eyebrows and the cover headline.
- **Cover headline split-color treatment:** "HE LOST THE SCOREBOARD." in steel gray (muted, slightly desaturated — it's the hollow number), "HE WON THE LEVERAGE." in energy gold (warm, saturated — it's the real number). This word-level color coding previews the whole deck's register system in the very first slide.
- **Eyebrow/kicker lines (slides 2–7):** small, letter-spaced ALL CAPS, colored to match that slide's register (steel on slide 2, gold on slides 3/5, neutral warm white on slide 4's tension treatment, warm white on slides 6–7 since those are register-neutral).
- **Numbers inside body copy (1.175M, 1.073M, 18–49, $40M, $15–20M):** set in a slightly larger or bolder numeral style than surrounding body text so they read as the visual anchor of each sentence, matching each slide's register color — except the disputed $40M/$15–20M figures on slide 4, which use a hedged, slightly lower-contrast gray-gold tone rather than full-saturation color, visually reinforcing "disputed."
- **Slide 6's lesson line:** heaviest available weight, largest optical size used anywhere in the deck — this is the thesis statement and should dominate the frame, warm white, centered.
- **Slide 7's CTA:** question line in warm white, the two action lines ("Build toward winning that number." / "Save this for your next pitch.") in a slightly smaller weight directly beneath, with "Save this" doing double duty as a natural save-prompt without needing an icon.
- No script fonts, no decorative serif treatments — subject matter calls for clean, premium editorial grotesk throughout, same as prior business-news-style JustPreneur carousels.

## 5. Color palette

**Brand base (unchanged):** deep navy, steel gray, warm white, energy gold — used for all text, wordmark, and UI-style elements throughout.

**Story-specific register layer (background art only):**
- **Scoreboard register (slide 2, left half of slide 1):** cool steel-gray dominant on a navy field — stadium-scoreboard/ratings-crawl linework, bright but slightly flat lighting (intentionally "loud, little depth").
- **Leverage register (slides 3 and 5, right half of slide 1):** warm energy-gold dominant on a navy field — ledger/medallion/fulcrum linework, deeper, warmer lighting (intentionally "quiet, more weight").
- **Tension/disputed register (slide 4 only):** muted, partially desaturated blend of steel and gold on navy, with a neutral mid-gray reserved specifically for the dashed "disputed figure" bracket containers — distinct from both the Scoreboard steel and the Leverage gold so disputed numbers never get mistaken for a confirmed register.
- **Slides 6–7 (flat minimal):** deep navy base only; slide 6 has zero gold/steel accents (maximum "cleared board" contrast for the thesis line); slide 7 reintroduces one restrained gold glow behind the centered JustPreneur lockup, matching the brand's standard closing-sting treatment.

## 6. Image treatment

- Flat, graphic-design-forward, premium motion-graphics-plate treatment throughout — not photographic, not cinematic. This is a numbers/data explainer, so backgrounds should read like high-end broadcast business-news graphics, not lifestyle photography.
- Motifs (scoreboard/ratings-crawl, ledger book, target medallion, balance scale, fulcrum/lever, dashed bracket containers) rendered as simple, bold, line-based or softly-lit 3D-icon graphics — never more than 2–3 elements visible at once per slide, generous negative space always preserved for the text layer.
- No text, logos, numerals, or real identifiable people/faces generated inside any AI image — every number, name, and word of copy is added in the design tool afterward per this brief. AI-generated backgrounds supply motif, register color, lighting, and negative space only.
- No real network/show logos, no literal CBS/NBC trade dress, anywhere in any generated image (see §0.2).

## 7. Instagram safe-margin instructions

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350. Keep all text within x: 80px–1000px.
- **Grid-crop safety:** Instagram's profile grid displays portrait carousel posts cropped to a centered 1080x1080 square, trimming roughly the top 135px and bottom 135px. Every headline, eyebrow, and the JustPreneur wordmark must sit within y: 135px–1215px so the post still reads correctly as a grid thumbnail, not just in the full carousel view.
- Within that safe band, keep primary headline/body text further inset to y: 220px–1100px to leave breathing room above (profile/avatar overlap is minimal on static feed posts but a top margin still reads cleaner) and below (room for the swipe-dot indicator Instagram renders near the bottom of carousel images).
- Slide 1's split-seam headline should sit roughly centered at y: 500–850 so it reads clearly straddling both registers.
- Slide 6's centered thesis line and slide 7's centered CTA/wordmark lockup should sit at the true vertical center of the safe band, roughly y: 550–950.

## 8. Slide-to-slide consistency rules

- Every slide uses the same navy base, the same grotesk type family, and the same eyebrow-kicker treatment (small, letter-spaced, ALL CAPS, top-left) — no font or layout-system switching mid-carousel.
- The Scoreboard (steel) ↔ Leverage (gold) register coding must be visually identical every time each register reappears (slides 1, 2, 3, 5) so a viewer tracks "which number is this" instantly without rereading text.
- The disputed-figure dashed/bracketed marker treatment on slide 4 uses a single consistent gray value and container style — if it needs to reappear conceptually (it doesn't elsewhere in this deck), it must match exactly.
- JustPreneur wordmark position, size, and opacity stay identical (small, top-center/top-right) across slides 1–6, then scale up only for slide 7's closing lockup.
- Slides 6 and 7 share the same flat-navy base treatment, reinforcing that they are the deck's two-part landing (thesis, then ask) after five slides of register-coded build-up.

## 9. Accessibility and contrast check

- Warm-white text (#F5F3EE-equivalent) on navy background (#0B1E32-equivalent) comfortably clears WCAG AA contrast for both headline and body sizes.
- Energy-gold text (e.g., #D4A017-equivalent) is reserved for short eyebrows, numeral emphasis, and the slide-1/3/5 headline treatment only — never full paragraph body copy at small sizes, since gold-on-navy body text drops below comfortable contrast on compressed mobile screens.
- Slide 4's muted gray-gold "disputed" numeral treatment is intentionally lower-contrast as a design signal (hedged/uncertain), but must still sit on a dark-enough navy backdrop with a subtle scrim behind the numeral itself to remain independently legible, not just gray-on-gray.
- No text is ever placed directly over high-frequency motif linework (scoreboard crawl texture, ledger ruling lines, etc.) without a soft navy scrim (~65–75% opacity) between background art and text — mandatory on every slide, not optional.
- Minimum body-copy size renders at no less than ~44px cap-height equivalent at 1080px width; headlines and the slide-6 thesis line are substantially larger, legible on a phone at arm's length mid-scroll.
- Byron Allen, Jimmy Fallon, and Jimmy Kimmel's names (added as plain text in layout) use the same warm-white body treatment as surrounding copy — no color-coding singles out a named person in a way that could read as editorializing beyond the approved copy's own hedging.
