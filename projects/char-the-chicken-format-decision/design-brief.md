# Visual Design Brief — "He Has 7 Million Followers. He Still Chose the Boring Menu." (8-slide format-decision carousel)

## 1. Overall creative concept

This is a business-decision explainer, not a food post and not a creator-hype post. The chicken is the visual hook (fire, char, fat, craft); the actual story is a counter-service-vs-full-service operating decision. Treat it like a Bloomberg/Puck/Eater-business-desk breakdown of why a 7-million-follower creator picked the "boring" format. Keep every slide disciplined and editorial — no neon, no meme energy, no generic "hustle" motivational styling.

The carousel has three visual movements:
- **Slides 1–3 (Hook + Setup + Product):** fire, char, chicken fat, craft — sensory and premium.
- **Slides 4–5 (The Decision):** a deliberate, mirrored two-panel comparison — "Full-Service" vs "Counter-Service" — shot with the *same camera angle, same depth, same lighting style* so the swipe itself reads as "two ways this could have gone." This is the structural heart of the carousel.
- **Slides 6–8 (Credibility + Takeaway + CTA):** calmer, text-forward, closing the loop on why the "boring" choice was actually the informed one.

A single recurring graphic device ties all eight slides together: a thin horizontal gold line along the bottom edge of every slide carrying one small circular marker. The marker's position/state tracks the decision arc: unlit far-left (cover, before the decision is revealed) → lit, moving right (setup, product) → **two markers shown side by side at equal height on Slide 4** (both options still live/possible) → **one marker fades to hollow/unlit while the other brightens and moves right on Slide 5** (decision resolved) → single steady marker, fully lit, continuing right through Slides 6–8 to a final resting position on Slide 8. This is the same thin-line/marker motif used on prior carousels, adapted so its one moment of divergence (Slide 4) and resolution (Slide 5) does the heavy lifting of visualizing "two paths, one chosen" without needing icons, labels, or charts.

## 2. Composition and focal hierarchy

Every slide follows the same top-to-bottom hierarchy:
1. Headline or body-copy block — upper portion of frame
2. Photographic focal environment — lower half to two-thirds of frame
3. Thin gold line + marker(s) — fixed bottom-edge position, state advancing per slide as described above
4. JustPreneur wordmark — small, fixed bottom-center position, identical placement across all eight slides (sitting just above the gold line)

Slides 4 and 5 add a second-level hierarchy rule (see Section 3) governing how the comparison reads as one continuous beat across a swipe.

## 4. Typography direction

- Headline typeface (Slides 1, 7): a bold, high-contrast editorial serif with a business/culture-press feel (Tiempos Headline / Canela / Freight Display style) — premium journalism, not a motivational template.
- Short label headlines (Slides 4, 5, 7's "The Takeaway"): a heavier-weight grotesk or the same serif at a condensed/bold cut, set noticeably smaller than full headlines so they read as section markers, not competing headlines.
- Body copy typeface (Slides 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8): a clean grotesk/sans (Söhne / Inter / Neue Haas style) for maximum mobile legibility.
- No kicker labels, no slide numbers, no date tags added as separate small text anywhere — all facts (7 million, mid-October 2026, six years, 2020) appear only within the approved copy sentences themselves.
- Numeral callout "7M+" on Slide 2: extra-bold, gold, noticeably larger than body copy, never overlapping the body paragraph.
- Headline sizing: no line should exceed ~34–38 characters at the chosen weight; target minimum ~64px effective headline size on the 1080-wide canvas.
- Body copy sizing: minimum ~40–44px effective size at 1080 width, line-height ~130–140%, left-aligned (never justified, except Slide 8's centered sign-off treatment).
- Slides 4 and 5 must use identical body-copy point size, line-height, and label position so the pair reads as a matched set across the swipe — this consistency is as important as the imagery contrast.

## 5. Color palette

- Deep navy (#0B1220–#10172A range) — dominant background/shadow tone, identical across all eight slides
- Steel gray (#4B5563 range) — secondary surfaces (counters, stone, kitchen metal)
- Warm white (#F5F1E8 / #FAF7F0) — primary text color on dark backgrounds
- Energy gold / fire-amber (#C9A24B–#D4AF37, with warm ember tones #E8A33D permitted only within the fire/chicken imagery itself) — timeline line + markers, numeral callout, "Boring" accent word on Slide 1, "The Takeaway" label, wordmark
- No red, no additional accent colors beyond the amber-within-gold-family allowance for fire/char imagery — keep the palette disciplined so the carousel reads as one continuous brand set despite the fire-heavy early slides and the cooler flashback slide (6)

## 6. Image treatment

- All backgrounds graded toward navy/gray/gold so fire and amber tones in Slides 1, 3, 5, 7, 8 feel like warm highlights within the brand palette rather than a different color story.
- Apply a subtle dark navy gradient overlay (~40–55% opacity) behind every text zone on every slide to guarantee contrast regardless of the underlying photo.
- No text, logos, signage, or real identifiable faces generated inside the AI images themselves — all copy is added in a design tool afterward per this brief. Matt Broussard is referenced only through the approved copy text, never through a rendered likeness.
- Slide 6 ("the past chapter") should read 10–15% cooler/more desaturated than the rest of the set — the only deliberate grade shift in the carousel, used specifically to signal flashback without a split-screen.
- Slides 4 and 5 must match exactly in camera height, lens feel, and lighting style — the *density and scale of the room* (full of tables and a server vs. a lean counter and open floor) is the entire visual argument; do not let unrelated style differences dilute that contrast.

## 7. Instagram safe-margin instructions

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 (portrait) for all eight slides, consistent throughout the carousel.
- Keep all text and the wordmark within a safe zone of at least 96px from every edge. Instagram's feed UI can crop into the very top ~90px and very bottom ~250px of the preview — keep headline/body anchor points starting no higher than ~150px from the top, and keep the wordmark comfortably above ~120px from the absolute bottom edge (the gold line itself can sit lower, in the ~1290–1310px band, since it is a thin graphic device rather than text).
- Keep body copy line length under ~860px wide (leaving ≥110px margins each side) for comfortable mobile reading, with extra care on Slides 2, 4, 5, and 6 where body copy runs longest.

## 8. Slide-to-slide consistency rules

- Identical canvas size (1080 x 1350) on all eight slides.
- Identical thin gold line position along the bottom edge on every slide; marker state advances in this exact order: unlit far-left (1) → lit, moving right (2) → continuing right (3) → two markers side-by-side, both lit (4 — the fork) → left marker fades hollow, right marker brightens and advances (5 — the resolution) → single steady marker continuing right (6, 7) → final resting position, fully lit, slightly more prominent (8).
- Identical JustPreneur wordmark size/position/color (bottom-center, just above the gold line) on every slide.
- Identical color grade (navy/gray/gold) across all eight backgrounds except the deliberate 10–15% cooler shift on Slide 6 only.
- Identical body-copy typeface, size range, and left-alignment convention on Slides 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8; Slides 1 and 7 share the headline/serif treatment.
- Slides 4 and 5 must share identical camera angle, framing height, and label position — the matched-pair rule is the single most important consistency rule in this set.
- No slide may include a rendered likeness of Matt Broussard or any real named person.

## 9. Accessibility and contrast check

- Warm white (#F5F1E8) text over the navy-gradient-overlaid backgrounds targets a contrast ratio well above WCAG AA (4.5:1) for normal text — confirm the gradient overlay is opaque enough behind every line of text, especially over the firelit chicken imagery on Slides 1, 3 and the candlelit dining room on Slide 4.
- Gold (#C9A24B) is reserved for large display text (numeral callout, "Boring" accent word, "The Takeaway" label, the gold line/markers) on dark backgrounds only — do not set gold body copy at small sizes, where contrast drops.
- Minimum text size on mobile: no body text should render below ~40px effective size on a 1080px-wide canvas.
- Slide 2's body paragraph and Slide 6's body paragraph are the longest in the set — prioritize a slightly heavier gradient overlay behind text on these two slides, and on Slide 4's candlelit, higher-detail dining room background.
