## 1. Overall Creative Concept

This is a business-media explainer, not a motivational quote card. The visual argument IS the content: a rented, broken pipeline (Slide 2) versus an owned, working pipeline (Slide 3). Everything else — cover, evidence slide, takeaway — frames that contrast. Treat this like a Bloomberg/Axios/The Information infographic card: dense information, confident restraint, no decoration for its own sake. The reader should be able to grasp "rented vs. owned infrastructure" from Slides 2–3 alone, even with sound off and a half-second glance.

Visual through-line: a strikethrough/X motif on Slide 2 that visually resolves into a clean arrow/checkmark structure on Slide 3. That resolution — broken chain becomes built chain — is the single visual idea carrying the whole carousel.

## 4. Typography Direction

- **Eyebrow headers (Slides 2–5):** condensed or semi-condensed grotesk, all caps, tight tracking increased (+40–60), small size (28–34px), warm white or steel gray, sits above a thin 2px rule in gold (Slide 3, 4, 5) or muted slate (Slide 2).
- **Slide 1 headline:** bold/black-weight grotesk or modern serif-grotesk hybrid (e.g., a Söhne/Neue Haas Grotesk Display or GT Sectra-adjacent feel), 64–76px, tight leading (0.95–1.0), warm white on navy. No script, no condensed-for-drama distortion — legibility first.
- **Slide 1 sub-line:** same family, regular weight, 32–36px, steel gray, generous leading.
- **Diagram node labels (Slides 2–3):** medium-weight grotesk, all caps or title case, 26–32px, uniform across all nodes — no node should be visually louder than another except the single source node on Slide 3.
- **Body copy (all slides):** regular/medium grotesk, 30–36px, 1.3–1.4 line height, warm white or light steel on navy backgrounds; never below 28px anywhere in the carousel (mobile legibility floor).
- **Stat numerals (Slide 4):** oversized display weight, 96–120px, gold, tabular figures, with small caption label (24px) directly beneath each ("in funding", "from a16z").
- **CTA line (Slide 5):** "Save this." set in gold, bold, same size as body copy but weight-differentiated, not color-alone-differentiated (accessibility).
- Use no more than two typefaces total across the carousel (one display/headline face, one body/UI face) to keep the "premium media" feel rather than a template-stacked look.

## 5. Color Palette

- **Deep navy** (#0B1526 approx) — dominant background across all 5 slides for series consistency.
- **Steel gray** (#8A94A6 approx) — secondary text, node outlines on the "old pipeline" slide, muted supporting elements.
- **Warm white** (#F5F1E8 approx) — primary headline/body text for contrast and warmth (avoids clinical pure-white).
- **Energy gold** (#D9A441 approx) — accent only: rules under eyebrow headers on resolution slides (3, 4, 5), the single source node outline on Slide 3, stat numerals on Slide 4, "Save this." on Slide 5, JustPreneur wordmark accent.
- **Alert/broken tone (Slide 2 only):** a desaturated red-orange (#B8543F approx) or, if a single-accent-color brand rule is preferred, use steel gray strikethroughs instead of introducing red — recommend testing both; red-orange reads faster as "broken/rejected" on a business-media diagram and is a common, expected convention (not decorative), so it is the primary recommendation, with slate-only as the safe fallback if brand guidelines require exactly one accent hue per post series.
- Gold is reserved exclusively for "owned/resolved" signals (Slides 3–5) — never appears on Slide 2 — so the palette itself tells the story arc.

## 6. Image Treatment

This carousel is diagram/typography-led, not photo-led. No photographic subjects, portraits, or scenes are required or appropriate — introducing stock photography of "students" or "offices" would dilute the infographic clarity and risks generic-template feel. Recommended treatment:

- **Slides 1, 4, 5:** solid or very subtle gradient navy background (e.g., a soft radial gradient from #0F1B30 center to #0B1526 edges, or a faint architectural/grid linework texture at 4–6% opacity) — generated as abstract textured backgrounds only, with all text added afterward in a design tool.
- **Slides 2, 3:** background stays the same subtle navy texture for series consistency; the diagram (boxes, arrows, X marks) can be generated as part of the background art OR built entirely in the design tool as vector shapes. Recommended: generate a clean abstract background only, and build the literal diagram (nodes, arrows, strikethroughs, the 10 partner-name boxes) natively in the design tool — this guarantees the partner names and X-marks are pixel-accurate and spelled correctly, which image generation cannot reliably guarantee for 10 distinct proper nouns in a grid.
- No people, no faces, no logos of Anduril/Anthropic/Coinbase/Google/Meta/Nvidia/OpenAI/Palantir/Replit/Stripe should ever be rendered by the image model (trademark/fake-logo risk) — partner names appear as plain set type only, added in the design tool.

## 9. Accessibility and Contrast Check

- Warm white (#F5F1E8) on deep navy (#0B1526): contrast ratio ~13:1 — passes WCAG AAA for body text.
- Energy gold (#D9A441) on deep navy (#0B1526): contrast ratio ~7.8:1 — passes AA/AAA for large text (stat numerals, "Save this."); verify at final render that any gold text below 24px still clears 4.5:1 (recommend keeping gold text uses at 28px+ per Section 4 floor, which is already the brief's minimum).
- Steel gray (#8A94A6) on deep navy: contrast ratio ~5.2:1 — acceptable for secondary/supporting text at 24px+, not for body copy below that size.
- Alert red-orange (#B8543F) on deep navy: contrast ratio ~4.6:1 — borderline AA for the X-mark/strikethrough graphic elements (non-text, so WCAG text ratios don't strictly apply, but keep strikethrough strokes bold/thick, not hairline, to remain visible in low-light phone viewing).
- Do not rely on color alone to distinguish "broken" (Slide 2) from "owned" (Slide 3) — the X-mark/strikethrough shape and the arrow/checkmark-style connector shape are the redundant non-color signal, satisfying accessibility best practice.
- No text is ever placed over uncontrolled busy imagery — all backgrounds are flat or near-flat navy per Section 6, so text-on-image contrast risk is minimal by design.
