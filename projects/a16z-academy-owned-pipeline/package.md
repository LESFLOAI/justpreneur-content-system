## Facts Summary (Approved Facts Block, cleared with qualifications by Fact Verifier)

On September 22, 2026, venture capital firm Andreessen Horowitz (a16z) announced the launch of the Horowitz Andreessen Academy, a tuition-free, unaccredited one-year fellowship program based in San Francisco for a small founding class (reported at roughly 50 students) of ambitious 16-to-22-year-olds, positioned by its founders as a selective alternative path alongside — not a replacement for — traditional college. Applications opened immediately; the first cohort is set to begin in fall 2027. The Academy is reported to be a legally separate entity from the a16z venture fund, led by Udemy co-founder Gagan Biyani as CEO, with a16z co-founder Marc Andreessen and general partner Erik Torenberg reported to sit on its board. Funding totals $42 million, including $35 million from a16z, with additional undisclosed individual investors. Ten founding corporate partners have been named: Anduril, Anthropic, Coinbase, Google, Meta, Nvidia, OpenAI, Palantir, Replit, and Stripe. The program awards no degree, is not accredited, does not accept federal student aid, and does not guarantee a job or paid internship. A separate, tuition-charging two-year program is planned for fall 2028 pending regulatory approval, with tuition expected to be "similar to an elite private university" — this future paid track should not be confused with the current free founding program and is not referenced in this package.

## Final Caption

a16z didn't build a school to disrupt education. They built one because they got tired of renting someone else's talent pipeline.

On September 22, Andreessen Horowitz announced the Horowitz Andreessen Academy — a tuition-free, unaccredited one-year fellowship for a reported founding class of roughly 50 students, ages 16–22.
