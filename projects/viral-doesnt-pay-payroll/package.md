## Story
Chip City Cookies — viral NYC cookie chain abruptly closed all remaining stores amid an unresolved founder-investor lawsuit.

## Approved Facts Block (Fact Verifier, cleared with qualifications)
Chip City, an oversized-cookie chain, was co-founded by Peter Phillips and Teddy Gailas, who opened their first shop in Astoria, Queens, in 2017 and grew the brand into a multi-state chain. In October 2026, Chip City abruptly closed all of its remaining stores — reported at 22 locations — at the close of business on October 1, 2026. According to reporting citing an internal email (via Gothamist), company president Nicolas Baizan told staff that "we no longer have the funding required to operate this business"; a separate company statement issued around October 2, 2026, cited "significant macroeconomic headwinds." Reports vary on the chain's peak size, with figures ranging from 38–39 locations (as of mid-2026) to a reported 45 at an earlier point (2024) — these numbers are not reconciled across sources and should be attributed individually, not presented as a single settled figure. The closure came days after Peter Phillips — Chip City's co-founder and former CEO — filed a lawsuit on September 28, 2026, in New York Supreme Court against Chip City, investor Enlightened Hospitality Investments (EHI, affiliated with Danny Meyer's Union Square Hospitality Group), company president Nicolas Baizan, and Fred LeFranc, alleging breach of contract, unlawful wage withholding, and retaliation. Per the complaint as reported, Phillips stepped down as CEO on March 2, 2026, under an agreement guaranteeing a nine-month consulting role through December 2, 2026, with a prorated $157,500 consulting salary plus promised severance and family health coverage; he alleges this pay stopped on September 18, 2026, and that restoration was conditioned on his transferring web domains and signing SBA-loan-related paperwork. EHI invested $10 million in Chip City in 2022 and an additional $7.5 million in 2024 (total ~$17.5 million), and a March 2026 recapitalization reportedly (per Nation's Restaurant News, single-sourced) gave EHI a majority stake for $4 million. The company separately raised questions about roughly $900,000 in pre-closing expenses, some of which it said went to businesses owned by Phillips and to the former CFO's personal account — allegations Phillips disputes as "manufactured." As of verification (October 20, 2026), no on-record public response from Danny Meyer, Union Square Hospitality Group, or EHI addressing the lawsuit's specific allegations has been found; all allegations in Phillips's complaint are unproven and unadjudicated, and the lawsuit's connection to the store closures has not been established by any source — the two events are reported as close in time but not confirmed as causally linked.

## Strategic Angle
**"Viral Growth vs. Survivable Unit Economics."** A viral product and a growing footprint mean nothing if the business can't survive without continuous outside capital. Primary takeaway: audit whether your growth is funded by real, retained revenue or by a capital runway with an expiration date. Tone: sober vigilance and self-recognition, not schadenfreude.
