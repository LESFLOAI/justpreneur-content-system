## 1. Overall Creative Concept

This is a business-journalism explainer, not a takedown and not a hype reel. The reader should feel like they're swiping through a tight, sober piece of trade-press reporting — closer to Bloomberg, The Information, or Axios than to a founder-quote Instagram template. The visual system is built around a single device: **the ledger page**. Every interior slide (2–8) opens with a consistent masthead (JustPreneur wordmark + a thin gold hairline rule) that reads like the top of a financial statement, and the content below is treated like entries on that statement — dates, figures, quotes, and caveats laid out with the restraint and precision of a disclosures page, not the visual punch of a motivational quote card.

The deck deliberately avoids two traps: (1) making this feel like schadenfreude toward the founder, investor, or employees — no imagery of distress, no red "failure" color-coding, no mocking tone in type treatment; and (2) visually asserting disputed facts as settled — the 38–45 store range, the "majority stake" language, and the stake-value figure are all rendered with the same caveat typography used elsewhere in the brand system (smaller, steel-gray, explicitly paired with the sourcing language already present in the approved copy), never blown up into a hero numeral the way a confirmed fact would be.

Most importantly: slide 2 (the timeline) and slide 7 (the caveats) must never visually suggest that the September 28 lawsuit filing caused the October 1 closures. No arrows, no connecting lines, no color-coded "trigger" styling between those two entries anywhere in the deck — see Sections 3 and 8 for the specific guardrails.

The thumb-stop hook on slide 1 is the contradiction in the headline itself ("viral doesn't pay payroll") paired with a quiet, unglamorous image: a half-lowered security gate at dusk. It is restrained rather than dramatic — the story's tension is financial, not visual, and the cover should promise a sober explainer, not a scandal reel.

## 2. Composition and Focal Hierarchy

Across all 8 slides, the hierarchy is: (1) the slide's ALL-CAPS headline (taken verbatim from the approved copy's header line) — largest, highest-contrast element; (2) the approved supporting body lines — mid-size, high legibility, broken onto short lines; (3) the JustPreneur wordmark — small, fixed top-left position, identical every slide; (4) imagery or the ledger-texture background — supports mood, never competes with text for attention, and never occupies the top third where a scrolling thumb's eye lands first.

Slide 1 (Cover) breaks from the ledger-masthead treatment deliberately — it's a full-bleed photographic hook, no visible ledger rule, to keep the opening frame as clean and arresting as possible. Slides 2–8 all carry the masthead + hairline-rule device, which is what makes the carousel read as one consistent "statement" rather than eight separate graphics.

Slide 2 (timeline) and Slide 7 (caveats) are the two data-dense slides and get list/row treatments rather than big display type — this is a deliberate, acknowledged exception to "headline-sized everything," justified because these two slides are reference/data beats within the swipe, not emotional beats. Slides 3–4 are photographic-plus-scrim beats. Slides 5, 6, and 8 are minimal, type-only exhale/CTA beats, echoing each other to bookend the deck with calm.

## 4. Typography Direction

- **Headline typeface:** bold geometric/grotesque sans (Inter Bold / Neue Haas Grotesk Bold family) — confident and editorial. No scripts, no condensed "impact" display faces, nothing that reads as a motivational-quote template.
- **Body/supporting copy:** clean grotesque, regular/medium weight, same family — Inter Regular/Medium.
- **Caveat/sourcing text** (e.g., "figures vary by source, not reconciled," "single-sourced, per Nation's Restaurant News," any disputed-claim qualifier): consistently set smaller, steel-gray, italic, across every slide where it appears — this is a fixed typographic code for "this is a caveat," used identically on slides 2, 3, 4, and 7 so the reader learns the pattern once and reads it instantly everywhere else.
- **Dates/numerals (slide 2):** bold gold tabular numerals, financial-statement style, visually distinct from body text but never styled as a "hero numeral" oversized display — this deck intentionally does not use giant standalone numerals (unlike the hero-numeral treatment used on other JustPreneur decks), because the disputed figures in this story (store count, stake size, stake percentage) must never read as settled, bold-stamped facts.
- **Minimum sizing for mobile legibility:** headline text no smaller than ~64–72px effective at 1080px width on slides 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8; caveat/steel-gray text no smaller than ~32px effective anywhere it appears, even on slide 2's data-dense rows — this is the deck's accessibility floor and should not be undercut even under layout pressure.
- **Exception, stated explicitly:** slide 2's six timeline rows and slide 7's four caveat items may run smaller than the deck's hero-headline minimum (date labels ~32–36px, row/caveat body ~34–38px) because these are reference/list beats, not emotional beats — but they must never drop below the 32px caveat floor, and line-height must stay generous enough that no row feels cramped.
- **Line length:** short line breaks throughout (aim 5–8 words per line at 1080px canvas width) so no slide ever feels like a wall of text on a phone screen.

## 5. Color Palette

- **Deep navy** (#0B1A2B) — primary background for the cover, slides 2, 5, 6, 8, and the scrim over slides 3–4's photography.
- **Steel gray** (#5A6472) — hairline rules throughout, caveat/sourcing text color, secondary dividers.
- **Warm white** (#F7F3EC) — primary text color on navy backgrounds; becomes the dominant *field* color (not just text) on slide 7 only, as the deliberate "disclosures page" palette inversion.
- **Energy gold** (#D9A441 / #E8B84B) — accent only: masthead hairline rule, timeline dots/guide-line and date numerals on slide 2, quote-block border on slide 4, the single underline emphasis on slides 5 and 8. Gold must never dominate a field — it stays a thin accent throughout, which is what keeps this feeling like premium trade-press design rather than a celebratory or neon treatment (appropriate restraint for a story about a closure, not a win).
- Slide 7 is the one intentional palette inversion (warm white dominant, navy text, steel-gray hairlines only, no gold) — signaling "you are now reading the fine print," without introducing any color outside the existing brand palette.

## 6. Image Treatment

- Photography appears only on slides 1, 3, and 4 (storefront exterior, queue exterior, desk still-life); slides 2, 5, 6, 7, 8 are type-and-texture/graphic only — this keeps the deck from over-illustrating a story that is fundamentally about numbers and documents, not scenery.
- All photographic imagery is color-graded toward a consistent deep-navy, slightly desaturated mood so it reads as one unified editorial brand rather than stock photography — warm gold is reserved for single light sources (a lamp, a gap under a gate, golden-hour sun), never a general color wash.
- No real Chip City signage, logos, or trademarks are depicted in any generated image — all storefronts and shopfronts are generic/unbranded, with no readable text anywhere in the photography.
- No named real individuals (Peter Phillips, Nicolas Baizan, Fred LeFranc, Danny Meyer, or any EHI/USHG executive) are depicted or implied in any image — this story involves an active, unadjudicated lawsuit and real people, and depicting their likeness via AI generation risks an inaccurate or unauthorized portrayal. All human presence in the deck is limited to anonymous, non-identifiable figures shown from behind or in motion-blur silhouette (slide 3's queue only).
- No imagery anywhere in the deck visually connects or juxtaposes the lawsuit and the closures (no split-frame, no before/after pairing, no shared visual motif linking slide 2's Sep 28/Oct 1 rows or slide 7's items) — each caveat and timeline entry is visually self-contained.
- No malformed hands, no duplicate people, no clutter, no small decorative icons (no gavels, no "closed" stamp graphics, no crime-scene-tape visual language — nothing that tips the mood toward scandal).

## 7. Instagram Safe-Margin Instructions

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 px (portrait) for all 8 slides.
- Keep all text and the JustPreneur wordmark within a 72px safe margin from every edge, to avoid feed/profile-grid cropping.
- Slide 1: keep the headline block within roughly y=850 to y=1150 so it clears both the top status-bar zone and bottom caption/UI overlap zone on-device.
- Slides 2–8: the masthead (wordmark + hairline) sits at a fixed y≈96–190 zone on every slide; content zones stay within 72px of the left/right edges and at least 100px clear of the very bottom edge.
- Slide 2's six-row list must fit entirely between y≈320 and y≈1260 — if any row risks pushing past that zone, reduce line-height/row padding slightly before reducing text below the 32px caveat floor (Section 4).
- Do not place any critical text in the outer ~8% bleed on any edge.

## 8. Slide-to-Slide Consistency Rules

- JustPreneur wordmark: identical size, color, and top-left position on all 8 slides (slide 7 flips its color to navy for contrast on the inverted palette, but keeps the same position/size).
- The gold masthead hairline rule: identical weight, color, and y-position on slides 2–8; omitted only on slide 1.
- Caveat/sourcing typographic treatment (smaller, steel-gray, italic) is identical wherever it appears — slides 2, 3, 4, and 7 — so the reader recognizes it as one consistent "this is unconfirmed/disputed" signal across the whole deck.
- **Hard rule, no exceptions:** nowhere in the deck — not slide 2's timeline, not slide 7's caveats, not any transition device — may an arrow, connecting line, shared color code, or directional graphic link the September 28, 2026 lawsuit filing to the October 1, 2026 store closures. Every date/claim in the deck is rendered with equal visual weight to its neighbors; causation is not implied visually anywhere, matching the explicit disclaimer in the approved copy.
- The disputed-figures rule (38–45 stores, "majority stake," $4M valuation) applies consistently: these numbers are never set in hero/display-numeral size anywhere in the deck, and are always paired visually with their caveat-styled sourcing language on the same slide.
- Navy is the shared base across 6 of 8 slides; slide 7's warm-white inversion is the one deliberate exception and should feel like a clearly-signaled "turn to the fine print" moment, not an inconsistency.
- Typography family, weight steps, and minimum sizes per Section 4 are identical across all 8 slides.

## 9. Accessibility and Contrast Check

- Warm white (#F7F3EC) on deep navy (#0B1A2B): ~14:1 contrast — exceeds WCAG AAA for body text at any size used here. Used on slides 1, 2 (descriptions), 3, 4, 5, 6, 8.
- Energy gold (#D9A441) on deep navy: ~6.5–7:1 — meets AA and is near AAA; used for slide 2's dates, the masthead hairline, and short emphasis/underline moments only, never for long body paragraphs.
- Steel gray (#5A6472) on deep navy: lower-contrast by design, used only for caveat text and hairlines — confirmed at 32px-or-larger effective size per Section 4, and never the sole carrier of meaning (every steel-gray caveat line states its qualification in words — "reportedly," "disputes," "not reconciled," "unproven" — so meaning never depends on color alone for colorblind readers).
- Slide 7's inversion: navy (#0B1A2B) text on warm white (#F7F3EC) field is the same ~14:1 ratio read in reverse — fully accessible. Steel-gray hairlines on warm white are used strictly as dividing rules, never as the color carrying caveat text on this slide (all four caveat items are set in full-contrast navy, per Section 3, specifically to avoid any contrast dip on the one slide carrying the deck's most legally sensitive language).
- Slide 1's natural dusk-shadow scrim is mandatory for headline legibility over the photograph — do not ship slide 1 without it.
- No information in this deck depends on color alone anywhere — gold marks "accent/emphasis," steel-gray marks "caveat," and both are redundantly confirmed by the words themselves.
