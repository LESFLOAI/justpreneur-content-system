## 0. Hard constraints carried into every visual decision

1. **No trademarked characters or show artwork.** "KPop Demon Hunters" is a real, copyrighted Netflix title with a specific character aesthetic. No image-generation prompt may request those characters, their likenesses, the show's poster art, or any visual that reads as a copy of that IP. The attraction is represented only through abstract light, color, and architecture-free atmosphere — never through figurative character art.
2. **No real logos or brand marks.** No Netflix wordmark/"N" mark, no Mastercard brand mark (the two-overlapping-circles symbol or any card numerals), no Netflix House signage, no Galleria Dallas or King of Prussia Mall architecture/signage. These names appear only as typography added in the design tool from the approved copy — never as AI-rendered logos, signage, or recognizable buildings.
3. **No real people depicted.** No faces, portraits, or identifiable figures of any kind — including no implied "fans," "VIP guests," or "Netflix employees." Where a sense of "the public" is needed (Slide 4), use only soft, distant, non-identifiable abstract silhouettes/particles, never a composed crowd of people with faces or clothing detail.
4. **Primary visual metaphor: a three-gate access strip.** The spine of this carousel is a clean, abstract horizontal strip holding three evenly spaced turnstile/gate icons — one per access tier (partner, loyalty, public). The strip is cumulative and never regresses: each slide shows the identical strip in the identical position, with only the gate-lighting state changing (bright/open = "this tier's turn," dimmed/settled = "already opened," closed/outline = "not yet"). This single device carries the entire editorial logic — a deliberately sequenced funnel — without a literal calendar, ticket-booth, or app-screen illustration.
5. **Tier color code stays inside brand palette.** Partner tier = energy gold (exclusivity/status), loyalty tier = steel-gray/silver (relationship/membership), public tier = warm white (universal, fully open). This is a strict reuse of the existing brand palette assigned by tier — not a new palette, and not a value judgment (gold isn't "better," it's "first"; white isn't "generic," it's "everyone").
6. **Dense factual copy (Slide 4 pricing) must not compromise legibility.** Slide 4 carries the longest body copy in the set (general admission + VIP pricing + the "prices are variable" caveat). Reserve extra clean navy space on that slide and use a strict two-tier hierarchy (bold lead sentence, smaller supporting pricing lines) — wording is never trimmed or paraphrased, only sized.
7. **No invented labels.** Use only the eyebrow date lines exactly as given in the approved copy ("Sept. 22, 2026," "Sept. 29, 2026," "Oct. 1, 2026," "Nov. 12 & Nov. 20, 2026") on Slides 2–5. Slides 1 and 6 carry no eyebrow in the approved copy, so none is added — no invented chyron labels, no slide-count badges, no extra dates or citations beyond the approved text.
8. **JustPreneur wordmark visible and correctly spelled** on every slide, small, steady, identical position (top-left) and size across all 6 slides.
9. **No fabricated numbers.** All figures ($39, $34, $89, $99, the dates) render only as approved text added in the design tool — never as a different number invented by an image model's background art, and never as readable numerals baked into any generated graphic.

## 1. Overall creative concept

This is a premium business/culture-media explainer — closer to a Puck/Axios/Bloomberg-style "here's the mechanism" graphic than a generic motivational template. The thesis is that Netflix didn't pick one on-sale date, it designed an order. The visual system should physically demonstrate that order, not just illustrate "tickets" generically.

The carousel runs on one visual spine: a **horizontal three-gate access strip** that appears in the same position on Slides 2–5. As the carousel is swiped, the strip builds left to right and never resets: Slide 2 opens Gate 1 (gold/partner); Slide 3 keeps Gate 1 dimmed and opens Gate 2 (steel/loyalty); Slide 4 keeps Gates 1–2 dimmed and opens Gate 3 (warm white/public) — the strip is now fully populated; Slide 5 shows all three gates open simultaneously, now paired with a radiant doorway of light representing the real-world venue opening — the physical payoff of the funnel. This gives the reader a literal, swipeable experience of "watching access cascade in order," which is the entire editorial point.

**Slide 1 (Cover)** teases the device without revealing it — three faint, closed/outline gates in the lower third, establishing "there's a sequence coming" before any tier color appears.

**Slide 6 (Framework + CTA)** clears the board to a flat, high-contrast navy field so the thesis statement lands with maximum clarity, with one small callback: a tiny three-gate icon divider (gold/steel/white, unlit-to-lit left to right) beneath the headline — added as a design-tool layer, not baked into the AI image — so the sequencing motif is never fully abandoned even at its quietest moment.

- **Headlines (Slides 2–6):** bold grotesk, noticeably larger than body copy — the first thing the eye reads after the eyebrow/motif.
- **Body copy (Slides 2, 3, 5):** same grotesk family, regular/medium weight, generous line-height (≥1.3x) for comfortable phone scanning.

## 5. Color palette

**Brand base (unchanged):** deep navy, steel gray, warm white, and energy gold — used for all text, the wordmark, and eyebrow labels throughout.

**Story-specific tier code (reuses the brand palette, assigned by access order — not a new palette, not a value judgment):**
- **Energy-gold** = partner tier (Slide 2's gate, eyebrow underline, dimmed/settled appearances on Slides 3–6) — exclusivity/status, first in line.
- **Steel-silver** = loyalty tier (Slide 3's gate, eyebrow underline, dimmed/settled appearances on Slides 4–6) — relationship/membership, second in line.
- **Warm white** = public tier (Slide 4's gate, eyebrow underline, appearance on Slide 5) — universal, fully open, third in line.
- These three states appear **only** on: the gate-strip icons, the thin eyebrow-underline accents, and the tiny Slide-6 gate-icon divider. They never bleed into body text paragraphs or the JustPreneur wordmark.
- **Dimmed/settled gate state:** same hue, reduced brightness/saturation (roughly 35–40% of full brightness) and a smaller glow radius than the "open/active" gate — visually distinct from "closed" (steel-gray outline, no fill) and from "open" (full saturation, strongest glow).
- **Slide 1 teaser gates:** steel-gray outline only, no gold/steel-fill/white-glow at all — color is deliberately withheld until each tier's own slide.
- **Slide 5 accent:** a single supplementary soft violet-magenta glow is permitted behind the open archway only, as a culturally specific nod to the immersive-entertainment energy of the attraction — used once, sparingly, as atmospheric light only (never as a character, logo, or text color), with navy/gold/steel/white still the dominant palette on that slide.
- **Slide 6 (thesis):** flat deep navy base, warm white text, one gold CTA line, and the tiny gold/steel/white gate-icon divider only.
- JustPreneur wordmark: energy gold on navy, identical across all 6 slides.

- Keep all text (headline, eyebrow, body, CTA) at least **70px from every edge** (left/right/top/bottom) at 1080x1350 — well inside Instagram's crop-safe zone for feed and profile-grid previews.
- Reserve the **bottom 100px** as a clean buffer on every slide, free of essential text, since Instagram's UI (like counts, caption preview, "..." menu) can visually compete with content near the bottom edge even on standard feed posts.
- Keep the JustPreneur wordmark at least **60px** from the top and left edges, consistently sized, never touching the frame edge.
- On Slide 4 specifically, because the pricing body copy is the longest block in the set, keep total text height (eyebrow + headline + all three pricing-hierarchy lines) within the vertical band from roughly y: 420–1250, leaving the mandated top and bottom buffers intact — do not let the caveat line ("Netflix lists all prices as variable...") crowd the bottom safe margin.

5. Typography family, weight hierarchy (eyebrow → headline → body → caveat/CTA), and line-height rules stay identical across slides; only size and color accents change per §4–§5.
6. No slide introduces a color, icon, or shape not already defined in this brief (no new accent colors beyond the single Slide-5 violet-magenta glow, no new icon shapes beyond the gate/turnstile, key-card, membership-ring, and radiant-archway devices named above).

## 9. Accessibility and contrast check

- Warm white and energy gold text on the deep-navy base both exceed WCAG AA contrast ratios at the headline and body sizes specified (navy background luminance is low enough that both warm white and gold clear 4.5:1 comfortably at body-text weight, and far exceed it at headline weight).
- Steel-silver is used only for small underline accents and gate-icon fills, never for body paragraph text, since steel-gray-on-navy contrast is weaker than white/gold-on-navy — reserving it for decorative/iconographic use only keeps every readable line of copy on a high-contrast color.
- Slide 4's pricing numerals are kept in warm white/bold at a legible minimum size even though they're part of the "supporting" tier of the hierarchy — numerals are never shrunk below comfortable mobile-legibility size for the sake of hierarchy.
- No color-only meaning: the "open vs. dimmed vs. closed" gate state is also conveyed through fill vs. outline and brightness, not through hue alone, so the sequencing logic still reads for color-vision-deficient viewers.
- Line length on every body-copy block is kept short enough (roughly 30–40 characters per line at the specified type size) to remain comfortably scannable on a phone screen without zooming.
