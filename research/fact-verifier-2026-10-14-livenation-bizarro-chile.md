## Correction to Story Scout's Sequencing

The pitch framed a clean four-step 2026 roll-up: "Bizarro Peru (Jan 2026) → Movistar Arena Santiago → DG Medios Chile → now Bizarro Chile." This is inaccurate. Live Nation's majority stake in DG Medios Chile dates to **December 2019** — a pre-existing position, not a recent roll-up step. Actual recent sequence:

- Dec 11, 2025 — Majority-stake partnership announced, Movistar Arena Santiago (with BeLive Entertainment Group)
- Jan 22, 2026 — 51% stake in Bizarro Peru closes
- Oct 9–10, 2026 — Majority stake in Bizarro Chile agreed, pending regulatory approval

DG Medios must be described as an existing/longstanding Chilean foothold since 2019, not a 2025–2026 roll-up step.

## Missing Context Flagged

A 2026-10 La Tercera (Pulso) report ties Chile's competition authority (Fiscalía Nacional Económica, FNE) scrutiny to venue-allocation practices linked to Live Nation's Movistar Arena position, building on a 2019 FNE-approved merger (with behavioral conditions) between Bizarro Producciones and Grupo Fidelitas (Movistar Arena's controller). Could not confirm the outcome/status of any FNE review of the 2025–2026 deals specifically. Flag as a risk/texture note for the Strategist, not grounds to kill the story.

## Approved Facts Block (verbatim, for downstream use)

> On October 9–10, 2026, Live Nation announced it has agreed to acquire a majority stake (percentage undisclosed) in Chilean concert promoter Bizarro, a deal that remains subject to regulatory approval and has not yet closed. No financial terms were disclosed. Bizarro founder Alfredo Alonso will continue leading the company's creative and strategic direction. Live Nation CEO Michael Rapino said the agreement "complements" the company's existing partnership with DG Medios, calling Chile "one of the most dynamic music markets in Latin America, and a priority for Live Nation." Live Nation has held a majority stake in Chilean promoter DG Medios since December 2019 and announced a majority-stake partnership in Santiago's Movistar Arena (with BeLive Entertainment Group) on December 11, 2025. Separately, Live Nation closed a 51% acquisition of Peruvian promoter Bizarro Peru on January 22, 2026, for an undisclosed amount, via its local subsidiary Live Nation Perú; Bizarro Peru's Lima management team remained in place. Chilean competition regulators (the Fiscalía Nacional Económica) have previously scrutinized market concentration involving Bizarro and Movistar Arena's controlling group (2018–2019 merger review, with behavioral conditions); the current status of any review of Live Nation's 2025–2026 Chilean acquisitions is unconfirmed.

Core angle holds — Live Nation buying majority control while the founder retains creative/strategic leadership. Fixable in copy, not reasons to kill the story.
