## 6. Missing Context That Could Materially Change Interpretation

- Colombia's padel figures all trace back to a single industry-run census (TAC) and/or the brand-new federation itself — there is no independent government or third-party audit. The federation that cites the 687-court figure was itself only recognized in 2025, so it has an interest in presenting a growth narrative; this should be flagged, not treated as neutral statistics.
- US court-count sources disagree by a factor of roughly 3.6x (688 vs. 2,500+) depending on methodology (operating-only vs. pipeline-inclusive counts), so any single US figure used in the piece risks immediate contradiction by a reader who's seen a different count.
- Forbes Colombia separately reported that some Colombian cities (Bogotá, Cali) already show padel court oversupply relative to demand, with some club closures — this complicates a pure "boom" narrative and should be mentioned if the story claims uninterrupted growth.
- Padel Haus, Reserve Padel, and Kith Ivy operate different business models (multi-location club chain vs. ultra-high-end single membership club) — the story should not imply they are directly comparable "recurring revenue" plays without noting the very different price points and scale.
- No confirmation was found that Robert Farah has any current, named padel business — the entrepreneurial parallel the story wants to draw between Farah and the NYC operators is not currently supportable and should not be asserted.

## 7. Approved Facts Block (use verbatim downstream)

> Padel is expanding simultaneously as a hospitality business in Colombia and New York City, though with very different financial scales.
>
> **Colombia**: A newly recognized Federación Colombiana de Pádel (FCP) — recognized by Colombia's Ministerio del Deporte in 2025 — reports, via industry census data (the Tercer Censo Nacional de Pádel), roughly 233 padel clubs and 687 courts across Colombia as of early 2026, spread across 51 to 53 cities depending on the source (Valora Analitik; Portafolio). Portafolio reports this census also estimates cumulative infrastructure investment at more than COP $151,000 million, and separately projects — as a forecast, not a confirmed result — that the sector's 2026 revenue (court rentals, memberships, tournaments, and services) could exceed COP $100,000 million. Colombian gym chain Spinning Center Gym entered padel under the "Spin Padel" brand, opening a nine-court club at Centro Comercial San Rafael in north Bogotá, announced in August 2024 and opened in the second half of 2024 (Forbes Colombia; El Tiempo). Retired tennis doubles player Robert Farah — a two-time Grand Slam men's doubles champion (2019 Wimbledon and 2019 US Open, both with Juan Sebastián Cabal), who retired from tennis in 2023 and resides in Pereira, Colombia — has been publicly described as a promoter of padel's growth in Colombia post-retirement (Colombia.com); no specific Farah-branded padel business could be independently confirmed as of this check.
>
> **New York City**: Padel Haus, a Brooklyn-based padel club operator founded in 2022, closed a $7 million Series B round in July 2025 at a $33 million post-money valuation, bringing its disclosed funding to at least $18 million to date (Athletech News; SGB Online; Insider/Fitt). Reserve Padel operates six clubs: two in Manhattan (Hudson Yards, Upper East Side), three in the Miami area, and one "opening soon" location in Palm Beach County (PadelBrowser 2026 guide; Crain's New York Business). Kith founder Ronnie Fieg has opened Kith Ivy, a members-only wellness and padel club at 120 Leroy Street in Manhattan's West Village, with reported membership pricing of a $36,000 initiation fee plus $7,000 in annual dues — figures first reported by Emily Sundberg's "Feed Me" newsletter and corroborated by multiple outlets including Athletech News and The Fashionography. The Pro Padel League, a North American professional padel circuit, announced a $15 million Series A funding round on March 24, 2026, led by Charlotte Hornets co-chairman and governor Rick Schnall, following a $10 million seed round in March 2025; the league's 2026 season comprises five events across its 10 city-based franchises (eight in the US, one in Canada, one in Mexico), opening in New York City in July and concluding with a championship event in Miami in December.
>
> **Caveats carried forward**: US national padel court-count totals are disputed across sources (roughly 688 courts as of Q2 2025 per Misitrano Consulting vs. 1,000+ by April 2026 per PadelBrowser vs. 2,500+ per Book&Go, which appears to include planned/pipeline courts) — no single US total should be cited without naming the source and its date. Colombian financial figures trace to an industry-run census rather than independent audit and must be labeled as estimates/projections, not confirmed facts.
