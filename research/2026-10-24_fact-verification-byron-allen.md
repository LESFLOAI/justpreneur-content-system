## 6. Missing Context That Could Materially Change Interpretation

- **The slot isn't Nielsen-rated in the traditional sense for ad-sales purposes** because it's sold outright to Allen Media Group — some outlets note this affects how directly comparable the ratings framing is, though Nielsen panel data is still being produced and cited by trade press for the comparisons above.
- **Jimmy Kimmel remained the overall late-night ratings leader** throughout this period — any carousel framing Allen as "winning late night" needs this caveat, or it overstates the significance of a single-night, partial win.
- **The Oct. 6 "win" was a single night**, not a sustained trend — no evidence found of a pattern of repeated wins over Fallon between Oct. 6 and Oct. 24, 2026.
- **The debut collapse (87%/59% down) is the dominant ratings story**, and the Oct. 6 half-win is a comeback narrative from a very low base — framing it as "Byron Allen's show is now beating network late night" without that context would be misleading.
- **Streaming talks are still just talks** — as of today (Oct. 24, 2026), eight days after the initial report, there is no confirmed deal, named platform, or official statement from any party.

## 7. Approved Facts Block (verbatim for downstream agents)

> CBS canceled "The Late Show with Stephen Colbert" (announced July 2025; final episode aired May 21, 2026) and, in an unusual "time-buy" arrangement announced April 6, 2026, sold the 11:35 p.m. ET/PT slot to Byron Allen's Allen Media Group. Allen's "Comics Unleashed" debuted in the slot May 22, 2026, with companion show "Funny You Should Ask" (hosted by Jon Kelley) following. Under the deal, Allen's company produces the shows, pays CBS for the airtime, and sells its own ads; the exact payment amount has not been officially disclosed — Allen has called it "tens of millions of dollars," while reported estimates range from $15 million to $20 million annually. CBS has said the arrangement turns a reported $40 million annual loss on the old slot into about $15 million in profit — a claim Jimmy Kimmel has repeatedly and publicly disputed as "nonsensical" and not economically plausible.
>
> Colbert's finale drew 6.74 million same-day viewers (growing to 9.77 million in Nielsen's Live+7 measure) — his most-watched weeknight episode ever. "Comics Unleashed" debuted the next night to about 878,000 viewers across its two half-hours, a roughly 87% drop from Colbert's finale and a roughly 59% drop from Colbert's final-season average of 2.15 million viewers.
>
> On October 6, 2026, "Comics Unleashed" beat NBC's "The Tonight Show Starring Jimmy Fallon" among adults 18-49 in both half-hour comparisons for the first time. In total viewers, the result is mixed: Fallon's show actually won the matching 11:35 p.m.-to-midnight half-hour outright (1.175 million vs. 1.073 million), while "Comics Unleashed" edged ahead only in the following half-hour (873,000 vs. 848,000). Jimmy Kimmel remained late night's overall ratings leader throughout this period.
>
> On October 16, 2026, trade outlet LateNighter reported that Allen is in talks to bring "Comics Unleashed" and "Funny You Should Ask" to a streaming platform. The platform has not been named, it is unclear whether the deal would include back-catalog episodes, and no agreement has been confirmed by Allen, CBS, or any streaming service as of October 24, 2026.
>
