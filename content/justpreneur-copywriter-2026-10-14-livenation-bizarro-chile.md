# Project: Sell Majority, Keep the Pen

**Format:** Carousel (7 slides)
**Topic:** Live Nation's agreed majority-stake acquisition of Chilean promoter Bizarro; founder Alfredo Alonso retains creative/strategic control
**Strategy angle:** Angle A — control vs. equity as separable, situational negotiating leverage

## 2. Slide-by-Slide Copy

### Slide 1 — Cover / Hook
**Headline:** He Sold the Majority. He Kept the Pen.
**Subhead:** Live Nation's new Chile deal has a plot twist worth studying.

### Slide 2 — The Confirmed Facts
Live Nation has agreed to acquire a majority stake in Chilean concert promoter Bizarro.

Stake size: undisclosed.
Deal status: pending regulatory approval — not yet closed.

### Slide 3 — The Detail Everyone's Skipping
Founder Alfredo Alonso isn't exiting.

He's staying on to lead Bizarro's creative and strategic direction.

### Slide 4 — Gave Up / Kept
**GAVE UP**
Majority equity control. (Stake size: undisclosed.)

**KEPT**
The creative and strategic steering wheel.

### Slide 5 — The Lesson (Final Takeaway)
When you take outside capital — or get acquired — the real negotiation isn't only the check.

It's the clause that decides who keeps making the calls.

Ownership and control aren't the same thing. They can be negotiated separately — but only if you have leverage to negotiate with. Alonso had the relationships and market trust Live Nation needed. That's what bought him the pen — not a rule that works for everyone, every time.

### Slide 6 — Texture Note
Context: Chilean regulators have scrutinized market concentration involving Bizarro and Movistar Arena's controlling group before (2018–2019 merger review, with behavioral conditions).

Whether that scrutiny applies to this new deal is unconfirmed.

### Slide 7 — Closing CTA
Save this before your next term sheet.

Then ask yourself: if you sold tomorrow, what would you negotiate to keep?

## 3. Final Caption

Live Nation has agreed to acquire a majority stake in Chilean promoter Bizarro — stake size undisclosed, deal still pending regulatory approval. Standard roll-up story, right?

Here's the line worth re-reading: founder Alfredo Alonso isn't being replaced. He's staying on to run Bizarro's creative and strategic direction.

That's the actual lesson for founders and operators watching this space — not "Live Nation is buying Latin America's music market," but "control and ownership are two different negotiations." Alonso gave up the majority. He kept the pen. That wasn't automatic — it came from leverage: the relationships and trust that make a live-music market actually run, which a buyer can't just acquire on paper.

Next time you're structuring a raise, a partnership, or an exit, the equity split is only half the term sheet. The other half is who keeps making the calls.
