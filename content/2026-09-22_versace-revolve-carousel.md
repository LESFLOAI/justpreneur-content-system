## Slide-by-Slide Copy

**Slide 1 — Cover / Hook**
Donatella Versace Didn't Rebuild With Luxury.
She Rebuilt With Reach.

**Slide 2 — The Move**
On September 21, 2026, Donatella Versace and Revolve Group, Inc. (NYSE: RVLV) announced a joint venture: a new, independent fashion and beauty brand.

Donatella will serve as Chief Creative Officer. First products launch in 2027 — beauty first — across the U.S., Latin America, Asia-Pacific, and the Middle East.

**Slide 3 — Old World**
For nearly 30 years, Donatella led Versace as Chief Creative Officer.

In March 2025, she stepped into a chief brand ambassador role. Dario Vitale became Versace's CCO in April 2025. Versace is now owned by Prada Group.

She didn't leave. She holds parallel roles.

**Slide 4 — New Partner**
For her next chapter, she didn't build inside a legacy luxury house.

She built with Revolve — a digital-native retailer known for audience data, direct-to-consumer reach, and e-commerce infrastructure.

**Slide 5 — Why It Matters**
This new venture is structurally independent of the Versace fashion house — no inherited name, no backing from that fashion house.

Her confirmed role: creative leadership. Her partner: one that already controls audience and distribution at scale.

**Slide 6 — The Lesson (Takeaway)**
When you choose who to build your next chapter with, don't evaluate them on the name in the room.

Evaluate them on the infrastructure and audience they actually control.

**Slide 7 — CTA**
Follow @justpreneur for how founders and creative leaders make the partner decisions everyone else misses.

## Final Caption

Donatella Versace just announced a new venture — and the partner she picked is the real story.

Not a fashion conglomerate. Not another legacy house. Revolve — a digital-native retailer built on audience and distribution, not runway heritage.

She'll serve as Chief Creative Officer on a brand that's structurally independent from Versace (now under Prada Group), launching beauty first in 2027 across the U.S., Latin America, Asia-Pacific, and the Middle East. Equity terms haven't been disclosed by either company.

The lesson for founders: when you're picking who builds your next chapter with you, weigh what they control — audience, infrastructure, reach — over what their name signals in the room.
